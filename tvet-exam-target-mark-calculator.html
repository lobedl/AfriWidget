What this calculator does

Every TVET student in South Africa knows the ritual. The college hands back your ICASS progress report a few weeks before the national exam, and somewhere on that page is a percentage that decides how hard you have to work in the exam hall. A strong ICASS mark means you can afford a quiet exam. A weak one means you need to pull the final mark up from behind. The problem is that the arithmetic connecting the two is not always obvious, especially when the exam carries 50, 60, or even 75 per cent of the final mark depending on the stream and the subject.

This calculator takes your current ICASS mark, asks you which stream your subject sits in, and returns the exam mark you need for each of the four national bands: the 40 per cent minimum pass, the 50 per cent standard pass, the 60 per cent good pass, and the 75 per cent distinction. It also shows the exam mark required for a custom target you set yourself, so you can plan around a specific goal rather than a generic band.

Everything runs in the browser. No student number, no subject code, and no ICASS mark is uploaded anywhere. If you press the AI insight button, only an anonymous summary of the calculation is sent to a small serverless function that runs once and then stops.

How DHET weights ICASS and the national exam

The Department of Higher Education and Training sets the weightings that every public TVET college applies at the end of a trimester, semester, or academic year. There are three weightings in use, and they map onto three programme types.

Programme typeICASSISATExamPass mark
NATED / Report 191 (N1–N6)40%—60%40%
NC(V) Vocational subject25%25%50%50%
NC(V) Fundamental (Language / LO)50%—50%40%
NC(V) Fundamental Mathematics50%—50%30%

ICASS stands for Internal Continuous Assessment. It is the portfolio of tests, assignments, and practical work completed during the term, and it is marked and recorded by the college. ISAT stands for Integrated Summative Assessment Task, which is the practical component for NC(V) vocational subjects, also marked internally. The national examination is the external paper written at the end of the term and marked by an external panel under Umalusi quality assurance.

The reason the weighting matters so much is that a 60 per cent exam carries 1.5 times the weight of a 40 per cent ICASS per percentage point, and a 50 per cent exam carries the exact weight of a 50 per cent ICASS. A student who has a strong ICASS under the 40/60 system gets less relief in the exam than a student under the 50/50 system, because the exam carries more of the final decision. This is why the same ICASS mark produces different exam targets depending on the stream, and it is why the calculator asks you to pick the stream first.

The 40 per cent examination sub-minimum

NATED has a rule that catches a lot of students off guard. Even if the combined final mark would be comfortably above 40 per cent, the exam paper has to be passed at 40 per cent on its own. A student with an ICASS of 80 per cent and an exam mark of 38 per cent has a combined score of 54.8 per cent, which looks like a pass on the surface. Because the exam mark fell below 40 per cent, the subject is failed and has to be re-registered.

Why the sub-minimum exists. It stops a student from passing a subject on the strength of internal assessment alone. The national exam is the external quality check, and DHET requires every student who earns a NATED certificate to have demonstrated competence on an externally set and marked paper. The rule is not negotiable at college level.

This calculator flags the sub-minimum for NATED subjects. If the required exam mark calculated from a target is below 40 per cent, the tool reports 40 per cent as the real exam target, because anything less is an automatic fail regardless of how the combined mark looks. The NC(V) vocational subjects do not have the same exam sub-minimum, but they have a 50 per cent overall pass threshold, which is stricter than the NATED 40 per cent pass. The NC(V) fundamental subjects sit at 40 per cent for Language and Life Orientation and 30 per cent for Mathematics, with no separate exam sub-minimum.

How the reverse target formula works

Working out the exam mark you need is just the weighting formula rearranged. Instead of combining the ICASS and exam to get a final mark, you put the final mark in and solve for the exam mark.

NATED (40% ICASS, 60% exam)
Required exam mark = (Target − ICASS × 0.40) ÷ 0.60

NC(V) vocational (25% ICASS, 25% ISAT, 50% exam)
Required exam mark = (Target − ICASS × 0.25 − ISAT × 0.25) ÷ 0.50

NC(V) fundamental (50% ICASS, 50% exam)
Required exam mark = (Target − ICASS × 0.50) ÷ 0.50

The four target bands are computed by plugging 40, 50, 60, and 75 into the target variable. The custom target uses whatever value the student types. The result is always a percentage, and the calculator rounds to one decimal place.

There is one adjustment that trips people up. If the required exam mark comes out negative, the student has already secured the target from their ICASS alone and any exam mark above the sub-minimum will do. The calculator floors the result at 40 per cent for NATED, because the exam paper still has to be passed at 40 per cent even when the combined figure is already safe. For NC(V) vocational subjects there is no exam sub-minimum, so the floor is zero for those streams, and the reported target simply becomes 0 per cent with a note that any passing exam mark is enough.

A worked example for NATED

Consider a Level 3 NATED student with a current ICASS mark of 52 per cent in a subject that uses the standard 40/60 weighting. The ICASS contribution to the final mark is 52 times zero point four, which is 20.8 points. Those points are already locked in.

Target final markNeeded from examRequired exam markSub-minimum applied
40% minimum pass19.2 pts32.0%Floored to 40.0%
50% standard pass29.2 pts48.7%No change
60% good pass39.2 pts65.3%No change
75% distinction54.2 pts90.3%No change

The student can already see three realistic outcomes. A 40 per cent pass is safe with any exam mark at or above 40 per cent. A 50 per cent pass is well within reach with a 48.7 per cent exam. A 60 per cent pass is achievable with a 65.3 per cent exam, which takes discipline but is not extreme. A 75 per cent distinction would need a 90.3 per cent exam, which is possible but only for a student who has mastered the syllabus and can write under time pressure. The honest planning decision is to aim for the 60 per cent band, secure the credits, and only chase the distinction if the exam preparation feels unusually strong.

Now suppose the same student had an ICASS of 35 per cent. The ICASS contribution is 14 points. The distinction target would need 61 points from the exam, which is 101.67 per cent, and the calculator reports that as mathematically unreachable. The pass target still needs a 43.3 per cent exam, and the standard 50 per cent target needs a 60 per cent exam. The planning decision shifts accordingly: a 50 per cent pass is now the stretch goal, and the distinction is off the table for this subject.

A worked example for NC(V)

NC(V) splits the arithmetic because the exam carries half the weight and the ISAT practical carries a quarter. Take a Level 2 NC(V) vocational student with an ICASS of 62 per cent and an ISAT of 78 per cent. The ICASS contribution is 62 times zero point two five, which is 15.5 points. The ISAT contribution is 78 times zero point two five, which is 19.5 points. Together the internal components deliver 35 points of the final mark before a single exam question is answered.

Target final markNeeded from examRequired exam mark
50% standard pass15.0 pts30.0%
60% good pass25.0 pts50.0%
75% distinction40.0 pts80.0%

The 50 per cent pass is secured with a 30 per cent exam mark. The 60 per cent pass needs exactly 50 per cent in the exam. The distinction needs 80 per cent, which is a high ask but achievable for a student who has been strong all term. Because there is no NC(V) vocational exam sub-minimum, the pass is on the combined mark alone, so a strong ISAT is a real safety net.

Now take a NC(V) fundamental student in a Language subject with an ICASS of 58 per cent. The ICASS contributes 29 points under the 50/50 split. The 40 per cent pass needs 11 points from the exam, which is 22 per cent. The 50 per cent pass needs 21 points, which is 42 per cent. The 60 per cent pass needs 31 points, which is 62 per cent. The 75 per cent distinction needs 46 points, which is 92 per cent, and the calculator flags it as effectively out of reach. The realistic planning target is a 60 per cent pass, with a clear note that the ICASS base supports a comfortable outcome.

Common mistakes students make

Assuming the exam is the only thing that matters. The exam is the largest single component under NATED, but the ICASS is 40 per cent of the final mark and it is already banked. A student who ignores their ICASS and only studies for the exam is leaving guaranteed points on the table. Check the ICASS mark the day the progress report is handed out, not the week before the exam.

Ignoring the exam sub-minimum. The 40 per cent exam floor under NATED is not a suggestion. A student with an excellent ICASS who writes a lazy exam can still fail the subject outright. The pattern that catches people is a strong internal record that produces complacency in the exam hall, and the sub-minimum removes the safety net that the combined mark would otherwise provide.

Confusing the pass mark with the good pass mark. A 40 per cent pass is a pass, but it may not be enough for the next level in the same subject or for admission to a university programme. Many NC(V) routes into higher certificate and diploma study ask for a 50 per cent pass or higher in the relevant subjects. Plan the target around what the next step needs, not around the minimum the college accepts.

Calculating the target for the wrong stream. The reverse formulas are different. A student who calculates a NATED target using the NC(V) formula will get a number that does not match what the college will produce. Check the subject code or the college programme guide if you are unsure which weighting applies.

Repeating a subject without re-planning the exam target. When a subject is re-registered, the ICASS is usually carried forward from the previous sitting, and the exam target is recalculated against that same internal mark. A student who failed on the exam sub-minimum last time and re-registers with the same ICASS needs a very different exam strategy the second time round. The arithmetic is the same, the pressure is not.

Setting the target after the ICASS has stopped moving. By the time the national exam is written, the ICASS is fixed and the only variable is the exam mark. Planning a target in the last week is fine, but planning it in the first week of the term, when the ICASS is still rising, gives far more room to manoeuvre. A student who can move their ICASS from 45 to 55 during the term lowers every exam target by roughly 17 percentage points under the NATED weighting.

Treating a distinction as the only good outcome. A 75 per cent distinction is worth having, but a 60 per cent pass in a subject that would otherwise have been a fail is worth more to the student's overall record, because it secures the credit and keeps the qualification on track. When the distinction is out of reach, drop the target, secure the credits, and pick up the distinction in a subject where the ICASS base makes it realistic.

Who this calculator is for

The tool is built for South African public TVET college students preparing for the DHET national examinations across NATED Report 191 levels N1 to N6, and NC(V) levels 2 to 4. It works for trimester, semester, and year-end exams, because the weightings are the same across the calendar.

It also works for the people around those students. A parent helping a child plan revision for the end-of-year exams can enter the ICASS mark and see exactly what exam mark each goal needs, which turns a vague conversation about working harder into a specific conversation about a specific number. A lecturer who wants to show a class how the weighting works can walk through the calculator live. A student advisor at a college can use it in a one-to-one session to map out a realistic recovery plan for a subject the student is worried about.

The calculator is also useful for students who are deciding whether to drop a subject or push through. If the required exam mark for a pass is mathematically unreachable, that is important information. If the required exam mark for a pass is well within reach, that is equally important, because it can turn a student who was about to give up into a student who sits the exam and passes.

Where the calculator stops

This tool estimates the exam mark needed to hit a final mark target from a single ICASS score and, for NC(V) vocational subjects, a single ISAT score. It does not know the internal calculation rules at a specific college, which may round the ICASS to a whole number, or which may exclude a missed assessment from the final ICASS mark. It does not know what a student has done to earn the ICASS figure printed on the progress report, and it does not adjust for subjects that have been re-registered or carried forward.

It also does not model a few edge cases that occasionally come up. Some colleges apply a small scale or adjustment to the ICASS mark at the end of the term, which changes the base from which the exam target is calculated. Some subjects run over more than one term and produce a combined ICASS that is not visible on a single progress report. Some students are re-registered for a subject with an exam-only rule, which removes the ICASS from the calculation entirely. In all of those cases the official statement of results is the document that decides, and the college examinations office is the authority on how the arithmetic is actually run for a specific subject.

What the tool does give you is a fast, transparent, private way to see what each exam target requires, to check whether a goal is realistic before you commit to it, and to plan revision hours against the numbers rather than against a feeling. Used as one input among several, it is a practical planning aid for the weeks between the progress report and the exam.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum exam mark required to pass NATED (N1 to N6)?

DHET sets a mandatory 40 per cent sub-minimum on the national examination paper, and the combined final mark (40 per cent ICASS plus 60 per cent exam) must also be at least 40 per cent. That means a student cannot pass NATED on the strength of their ICASS alone. Even with a strong ICASS of 75 per cent, an exam mark below 40 per cent means the subject is failed and has to be re-registered.

How is a subject distinction calculated in TVET?

A distinction is a final combined mark of 75 per cent or higher. Under the NATED system that final mark is 40 per cent ICASS plus 60 per cent exam. Under the NC(V) vocational system it is 25 per cent ICASS plus 25 per cent ISAT plus 50 per cent exam. The word distinction is used at Umalusi and DHET level for a subject passed at 75 per cent or above, sometimes written as pass with distinction or cum laude.

What happens if my ICASS mark is below 40 per cent?

At most TVET colleges, a 40 per cent ICASS is required just to be admitted to the national examination. If the college admits you on a borderline ICASS such as 38 per cent, you would need a higher exam mark than usual to pull the combined final mark up to 40 per cent. The calculator does not know what your college has decided, so enter the ICASS figure printed on your most recent progress report and treat the result as an estimate.

Can I score high in ICASS but still fail the subject?

Yes, under the NATED system that is possible. A student with an ICASS of 80 per cent and an exam mark of 38 per cent has a combined score of 54.8 per cent, which looks like a comfortable pass on the surface. Because the exam mark fell below the mandatory 40 per cent sub-minimum, the subject is failed and must be re-registered. The reverse is not true: a strong exam mark can never rescue a below-40 exam mark, but it can rescue a low ICASS because there is no ICASS sub-minimum.

How does the NC(V) vocational weighting differ from NATED?

The NC(V) vocational subjects split the final mark into three parts: 25 per cent ICASS, 25 per cent ISAT practical, and 50 per cent exam. The NATED system splits it into two: 40 per cent ICASS and 60 per cent exam. The NC(V) fundamental subjects such as Language and Life Orientation split it into two again, but 50 per cent ICASS and 50 per cent exam. The mathematics component of the fundamentals has a lower pass threshold of 30 per cent. Each stream uses a different reverse formula, which is why the calculator asks you to pick the stream first.

What does an unreachable target mean?

If the exam mark required to hit a target is above 100 per cent, the target cannot be reached in this exam sitting, no matter how well the student performs. For example, an ICASS of 35 per cent under the NATED system needs an exam mark of 101.67 per cent to reach a 75 per cent distinction, which is impossible. The realistic response is to lower the goal for this subject to a pass or a 50 per cent pass, secure the credits, and plan a distinction on a future subject where the ICASS base is stronger.

Do all TVET colleges use the same weightings?

The national examination weightings are set by DHET and applied by every public TVET college that offers NATED Report 191 or NC(V) programmes. What can vary is the way each college calculates the ICASS portion internally, how many internal assessments count toward the ICASS mark, and whether a missed assessment is replaceable. Always take the ICASS figure from your official college progress report, because that is the number DHET will use at the end of the term.

Does this calculator store my ICASS mark or share it?

No. Every calculation runs inside your browser tab and nothing is stored on any server. If you press the AI insight button, only an anonymous summary is sent to a small serverless function that runs once and then stops. The stream, the ICASS band, the ISAT band if used, the target mark, and the computed exam targets are shared, but no student name, student number, subject name, or college identifier leaves the browser.

Is this calculator an official DHET or Umalusi tool?

No. It is an independent academic planning aid built to match the published DHET and Umalusi assessment weightings and the standard 40, 50, 60, and 75 per cent bands. The official mark, the official pass or fail decision, and the official distinction are produced by DHET and Umalusi using your full internal record. Use this calculator to plan revision realistically, and treat your official statement of results as the definitive source.

How do I use this tool to plan revision?

Enter your current ICASS mark, choose the stream that matches your subject, and read the four target rows. The row you can realistically hit tells you the exam mark you should aim for. If the 50 per cent target requires a 55 per cent exam, aim for 60. If the 75 per cent target requires a 101 per cent exam, drop the goal to a 60 per cent pass and focus your energy where the return on effort is highest. Past exam papers, memoranda, and lecturer revision sessions are the three resources that move the exam mark the most.
