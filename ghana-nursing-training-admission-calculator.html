What this calculator actually does

This tool takes the grades you earned in your WASSCE or SSSCE examination and turns them into the aggregate that Ghana’s Ministry of Health Health Training Institutions use to shortlist applicants. It does three things at once. It adds up your core and elective points, it checks whether each subject meets the minimum grade the MoH requires for the programme you have selected, and it returns a verdict about which track you are most likely to be considered for.

You choose a target programme, enter a grade for each of the three core subjects and each of your three best electives, and the calculator returns an aggregate number. If the aggregate and the grades both clear the threshold for the programme you selected, the verdict says so. If they do not, the tool tells you which requirement fell short and which programme would be a better fit, usually the certificate track or a remedial sitting.

Everything runs inside your browser. The arithmetic, the prerequisite checks, and the verdict are all produced on your device. Nothing you type is sent anywhere unless you deliberately press the optional AI button, and even then only an anonymous summary of the computed aggregate and programme travels.

The three tiers of Ghanaian nursing training

Nursing training in Ghana operates on three distinct tiers, and understanding which tier you are aiming for changes how you should read your aggregate.

Diploma programmes, three years. Registered General Nursing, Registered Midwifery, Community Health Nursing, and Registered Mental Nursing. These are the standard entry routes into the profession. Graduates sit the Nursing and Midwifery Council licensing examination and become staff nurses, midwives, or community health nurses. The MoH planning threshold is an aggregate of 24 or better across six subjects, with every subject at grade C6 or above.

Certificate programmes, two years. Nurse Assistant Clinical and Nurse Assistant Preventive. These lead to auxiliary roles in hospitals, clinics, and community health posts. The threshold is looser: an aggregate of 36 or better, with grades between A1 and E8 accepted. Certificate holders who later want to upgrade to a full diploma can do so through post-basic or sandwich programmes after a few years of practice.

Degree programmes, four years. Bachelor of Science in Nursing, offered at the University of Ghana, KNUST, UHAS, and several other public and accredited private universities. Entry is competitive, and the effective cut-off often sits in the low teens rather than the twenties because the applicant pool is much stronger. The diploma route followed by a top-up degree is often a more realistic path for students whose WASSCE results sit in the middle of the range.

How the MoH aggregate is calculated

The aggregate is a sum of points across six subjects, three core and three elective. Each grade carries a fixed number of points, and the six numbers are simply added together. Because the strongest grade carries the smallest number, a lower aggregate always indicates a stronger result. That is the opposite of how a percentage works, and it is the single most confusing thing about the system for students seeing it for the first time.

The lowest possible diploma aggregate is 6, which would be six A1 grades. The highest passing aggregate at the certificate level is 36, which is six C6 grades exactly. In practice, competitive diploma programmes at the larger colleges tend to admit around 12 to 18, and the cut-off widens to the mid-twenties at colleges with fewer applicants.

Core subjects and the C6 minimum

The three core subjects, English Language, Core Mathematics, and Integrated Science, are compulsory for every programme. There is no substitution and no negotiation. Whatever else your examination slip says, if any of those three is below C6, the diploma track is closed for the current cycle.

That single rule catches more applicants than any other requirement. A candidate with an excellent aggregate of 14 can still be excluded from RGN if Core Mathematics came in at D7. The aggregate is a ranking tool, but the core grade minimum is a filter that sits in front of it. Failing the filter means the ranking never happens.

The practical consequence. If a core subject came in below C6, the fastest route back into the diploma track is a remedial sitting of that one subject in the November or December WASSCE. The MoH accepts a combination of two sittings for some programmes, and improving a single grade from D7 to C5 can move you from ineligible to competitive within one cycle.

Elective subjects and programme fit

The three elective subjects complete the six-subject aggregate, and they also determine which programmes you can apply for. The MoH does not require a specific elective combination for every programme, but individual colleges do publish their own expectations, and some of them are strict.

For General Science candidates, the standard electives are Biology, Chemistry, Physics, and Elective Mathematics. Almost every programme will accept this combination. For General Arts candidates, the electives usually include Economics, Geography, Government, Literature, or Christian Religious Studies, and those are accepted for RGN, RM, CHN, and the certificate programmes. For Home Economics candidates, Food and Nutrition and Management in Living are the standard electives and are accepted for nursing programmes with a community or public health orientation. For Agricultural Science candidates, General Agriculture plus Chemistry is the standard route.

The one hard rule to remember is that the electives you submit have to be the three highest grades you earned, and they have to be subjects the programme accepts. If your three highest grades are in subjects the college does not recognise for that programme, the college will substitute a lower-scoring but relevant subject, and your aggregate will change accordingly.

The oral interview stage

Clearing the written requirement puts you on the shortlist for the oral interview. The interview is conducted by the college admissions panel, usually at the college itself, and it is a short conversation rather than an examination. The panel assesses academic aptitude, general knowledge about healthcare and current affairs, personal grooming and composure, fluency in spoken English, and physical fitness for clinical work.

The interview is not a formality. Applicants who clear the written bar comfortably can still be declined at this stage if the panel feels they are not ready for the demands of clinical training. Applicants who were borderline on paper can move up the list with a strong interview. The two things that matter most in practice are preparation and presentation. Preparation means being able to speak about why you want to nurse, what you understand about the role, and how you would handle a simple ethical scenario. Presentation means arriving early, dressing formally, and speaking clearly and calmly.

Certificate programmes as a real pathway

The certificate programmes, Nurse Assistant Clinical and Nurse Assistant Preventive, are not a consolation prize. They are a legitimate entry point into the health workforce, and they lead to real employment in hospitals, clinics, district health centres, and community outreach programmes across the country.

They also serve as a stepping stone. After three years of practice, a certificate holder can apply for a post-basic diploma through one of the accredited sandwich or continuing education programmes. Some certificate holders go on to complete a full degree through a distance or part-time route. The pay and scope of practice grow with each qualification, and the clinical experience gained during the certificate years is often an advantage in later training.

For a student whose WASSCE grades fall between the diploma and degree thresholds, the certificate track is often a smarter move than waiting a full year for a remedial result. It keeps you in the field, it keeps you earning, and it leaves the higher qualifications open for later.

Common mistakes when applying

The same errors come up in almost every nursing admissions cycle in Ghana. Recognising them in advance saves a lot of wasted effort.

Assuming the aggregate is the only requirement. The core grade minimum matters just as much. An excellent aggregate with a D7 in Core Mathematics will be filtered out before ranking even begins.

Submitting the wrong electives. The three electives you submit have to be the ones the college recognises for the programme. Some applicants submit their three strongest grades without checking whether the subjects are relevant, and end up with a substituted lower-scoring set.

Missing the application window. The MoH portal opens between March and April and closes in June or July. E-vouchers sell out, and late applications are not accepted. Applicants who miss the window have to wait for the next cycle.

Not bringing original documents to the interview. The interview panel will ask to see your WASSCE certificate or result slip, your birth certificate, and your school testimonials. Photocopies are usually not accepted on their own.

Ignoring the certificate track. Many applicants assume they should wait a full year and rewrite rather than accept a certificate place. In practice, the certificate route often leads to a diploma and a licence faster than a full gap year.

Treating the aggregate as the final answer. Cut-offs move. The number that was sufficient last year might not be sufficient this year if the applicant pool is stronger. Read your result as a range, not as a fixed score.

Where this tool stops

This calculator produces a planning estimate. It applies the MoH weighting rules, converts your grades to points, checks the core and elective thresholds, and returns a verdict against the programme you selected. It also shows you where the shortfall is if one part of the requirement is not met.

It does not know the exact cut-off for the specific college and programme in the specific cycle, because those cut-offs are set after the applicant pool is known and change from year to year. It does not know about any special quota or affirmative action scheme that the Ministry may apply in a given year, nor does it know whether a college has introduced a new subject requirement for a specific programme. It does not replace the oral interview or the document verification that happens at the college stage.

What it does give you is a fast, honest first read. Use it to notice early whether your grades place you in the diploma range, the certificate range, or outside both, and to decide whether a remedial sitting would move you into a stronger position. Then take that picture to the official MoH portal and the specific college for the year you are applying in. Those are the authority. This tool is the starting point that gets you there faster.

Frequently asked questions

What is the official MoH aggregate cut-off for Ghana nursing training colleges?

For the three-year diploma programmes in Registered General Nursing, Registered Midwifery, Community Health Nursing, and Registered Mental Nursing, the Ministry of Health expects an aggregate of 24 or better across six subjects. Those six subjects are English Language, Core Mathematics, and Integrated Science, plus the best three elective subjects, all at grade C6 or better. For the two-year certificate programmes in Nurse Assistant Clinical and Nurse Assistant Preventive, the threshold is an aggregate of 36 or better, with grades between A1 and E8 accepted.

Can I get into a diploma nursing college with a D7 in Core Mathematics or Integrated Science?

No. The Ministry of Health and the Nursing and Midwifery Council require a minimum of C6 in English Language, Core Mathematics, and Integrated Science for direct diploma admission. A D7 or E8 in any of those three core subjects moves you out of the diploma track, and the practical routes from there are a certificate programme or a remedial WASSCE sitting in the specific subject that fell short.

Which SHS elective programmes are accepted for nursing training in Ghana?

General Science, General Arts, Home Economics, and Agricultural Science are all accepted for the various nursing and midwifery programmes. General Science applicants often sit in a slightly stronger position during the oral interview scoring for specialised diploma programmes, but the written requirements do not exclude the other streams. What matters is that the three elective subjects you submit are relevant to the programme you are applying for.

What happens during the oral interview for MoH Health Training Institutions?

Shortlisted candidates attend a physical interview conducted by the college admissions panel. The panel looks at academic aptitude, general knowledge about healthcare and current affairs, personal grooming and demeanor, fluency in spoken English, and medical fitness. It is a short conversation rather than an exam, but the panel usually notices preparation within the first minute or two.

Can male applicants apply for Registered Midwifery in Ghana?

Under the current Ministry of Health regulations, the diploma in Registered Midwifery is reserved for female candidates. Male applicants who want a clinical healthcare career in nursing can apply for Registered General Nursing, Registered Mental Nursing, or Community Health Nursing, and can also consider physician assistantship or related allied health programmes.

When does the Ghana nursing training application portal open?

The Ministry of Health Health Training portal typically opens between March and April and closes in June or July. E-vouchers for the application are purchased from designated banks or through mobile money codes issued by the Ministry. The portal address and the voucher process are announced each year on healthtraining.gov.gh, and the dates do shift slightly from cycle to cycle.

Can I combine two WASSCE sittings for nursing admission?

The Ministry accepts a combination of two sittings for some programmes, but the core subjects must all be at C6 or better in the combined record. Some of the more competitive diploma programmes prefer a single sitting, and the certificate programmes are generally more flexible. Check the current year's admission notice for the specific college you are targeting.

What is the difference between RGN, RM, CHN, and RMN?

All four are three-year diploma programmes. Registered General Nursing covers general hospital and community nursing. Registered Midwifery focuses on pregnancy, childbirth, and postnatal care, and is restricted to female applicants. Community Health Nursing prepares nurses for public health, outreach, and clinic-based work in district settings. Registered Mental Nursing focuses on psychiatric and mental health care. Each leads to a different licence from the Nursing and Midwifery Council.

Is the calculator's aggregate the same as the college's own figure?

It should be very close if you have entered the correct grades. The official figure is the one the Ministry produces when it processes your application. Small differences can arise from rounding, from how elective subjects are mapped to programme requirements, and from the internal ranking adjustments that some colleges apply during selection. Use this result as a planning estimate, not as a replacement for the official notification.

Does the calculator send my grades anywhere?

No. Every calculation runs inside your browser and nothing you type leaves your device. If you choose to press the optional AI button, only an anonymous summary of your computed aggregate, programme, and pass or fail status is sent to generate a short written response. Nothing is stored.
