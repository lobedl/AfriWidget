What this calculator does

This tool takes the marks from your NC(V) trimester or year, combines them using the DHET weighting rules, and returns the outcome for every subject you entered. It handles the two different weightings that the framework applies: fundamental subjects use a 50:50 split between ICASS and the national examination, while vocational subjects use a 25:25:50 split between ICASS, the ISAT practical assessment, and the national examination. It also audits each subject against its own minimum pass threshold, since the threshold differs by subject type.

The arithmetic itself is straightforward once the weighting is known. What makes the tool worth using is the fact that NC(V) assessment runs on two different rules side by side, and candidates mix them up regularly. The vocational subject weighting carries a practical component that the fundamental subjects do not, and the pass thresholds are not the same across all seven subjects. Getting the weighting or the threshold wrong leads to a wrong picture of whether the certificate is actually cleared.

Every calculation runs inside your browser. No mark, no subject name, and no personal detail is uploaded. If you press the AI insight button, only an anonymous summary of the level, the number of subjects, the aggregate performance band, and the count of subjects that passed or failed is sent to a small serverless function that runs once and then stops.

How NC(V) assessment works

The National Certificate (Vocational) is a three-year qualification offered at public TVET colleges and accredited private colleges across South Africa. It runs at NQF Levels 2, 3, and 4, and each level is a full year of study in its own right. To be awarded the certificate for a level, a candidate must pass seven subjects: three fundamental subjects that every NC(V) student takes, and four vocational subjects that are chosen from the specialisation the candidate is enrolled in.

The assessment framework separates the internal work from the external work. Internal work is captured through ICASS, the Internal Continuous Assessment mark, which is built from class tests, assignments, projects, and internal trial examinations run by the college during the year. For vocational subjects there is a second internal component called ISAT, the Integrated Summative Assessment Task, which is a practical assessment of occupational competence in the chosen trade. The external component is the national examination set by DHET and quality-assured by Umalusi, written on the same date at every college in the country.

The three components are combined using the weighting that applies to the subject type, and the result is the final mark that appears on the statement of results issued by DHET. Each subject then has its own pass threshold, and a candidate who fails even one of the seven subjects does not receive the certificate for that level until the failed subject is cleared through a supplementary or rewrite examination.

Fundamental subjects and the 50:50 split

The three fundamental subjects sit at the base of the qualification and are taken by every NC(V) candidate regardless of the vocational specialisation. The official language is usually English First Additional Language, though the specific language offered depends on the college. Life Orientation covers citizenship, personal development, and health topics. Mathematics or Mathematical Literacy covers the numeracy requirement for the level.

The final mark for each fundamental subject uses a 50:50 split between ICASS and the national examination. That means the internal work carried the same mathematical weight as the external paper, which is a higher internal weighting than vocational subjects use. A candidate who performs well across the classroom year enters the national examination with a substantial buffer, and a candidate who underperforms internally has to compensate with a stronger examination performance to reach the same final mark.

Final fundamental mark = (ICASS × 0.50) + (National exam × 0.50)
Thresholds: Language ≥ 40  |  Life Orientation ≥ 40  |  Maths or Maths Literacy ≥ 30

Vocational subjects and the 25:25:50 split

The four vocational subjects are the specialisation of the qualification. They differ by programme: a Civil Engineering and Building Construction candidate takes vocational subjects in construction planning, construction supervision, materials, and one more technical subject; a Tourism candidate takes vocational subjects in tourism operations, tourism planning, and related topics; and so on across the different NC(V) programmes. Regardless of the specific subject, the assessment weighting is the same.

The final mark uses a 25:25:50 split between three components. ICASS carries 25 per cent, the ISAT practical assessment carries 25 per cent, and the national examination carries 50 per cent. This is the crucial difference from the fundamental subjects: half of the vocational mark comes from the external examination paper, and half comes from the internal assessment work split between the ongoing ICASS record and the ISAT practical.

The ISAT is not a written test. It is a practical demonstration of occupational competence, assessed against a national ISAT instrument, and it is what makes the vocational subjects genuinely vocational rather than just a written exam with a practical flavour. A candidate who is strong in the workshop but weaker in the examination room still has a pathway to a passing final mark because the practical carries 25 per cent of the weight and ICASS carries another 25 per cent.

Final vocational mark = (ICASS × 0.25) + (ISAT × 0.25) + (National exam × 0.50)
Threshold: every vocational subject ≥ 50

Pass thresholds and why they differ

The pass thresholds across the seven subjects are not uniform. The two language and orientation subjects sit at 40 per cent. Mathematics or Mathematical Literacy sits at 30 per cent, matching the general numeracy threshold used at the secondary school level. The four vocational subjects sit at 50 per cent each, which is the highest threshold in the qualification.

The higher threshold on vocational subjects reflects the industry-facing purpose of the qualification. A candidate who passes a vocational subject at 50 per cent has demonstrated both theoretical and practical competence at a level the trade accepts as a minimum for entering the workplace or progressing to further study in the field. The lower threshold on Mathematics or Mathematical Literacy reflects its role as a general numeracy requirement that runs alongside the specialisation rather than as the specialisation itself.

The two language and orientation subjects sit in between. Forty per cent is the level at which the candidate demonstrates sufficient written and functional literacy to read occupational documentation, communicate professionally, and continue learning in the field. It is the same threshold that applies to language subjects across the NQF framework.

A worked example

Take a candidate enrolled in NC(V) Level 4 Civil Engineering and Building Construction. The candidate's marks across the seven subjects are as follows.

The English mark is 56 multiplied by 0.50 plus 48 multiplied by 0.50, which is 52.00, above the 40 per cent threshold. Life Orientation is 64 plus 58 divided by two, which is 61.00, also above 40. Mathematics is 42 plus 36 divided by two, which is 39.00, above the 30 per cent threshold. The four vocational subjects use the 25:25:50 weighting. Construction Planning is 60 times 0.25 plus 70 times 0.25 plus 52 times 0.50, which is 15 plus 17.5 plus 26, or 58.50, above the 50 per cent threshold. The other three vocational subjects follow the same pattern, and all four clear 50 per cent.

The candidate passes all seven subjects and is awarded the NC(V) Level 4 certificate for the Civil Engineering and Building Construction programme. The vocational aggregate, which is the average of the four vocational subject finals, is 57.50. The fundamental aggregate, which is the average of the three fundamental subject finals, is 50.67.

Now suppose the same candidate had scored only 46 on the exam paper in one of the vocational subjects, with the ICASS and ISAT marks unchanged. The final for that subject would drop below 50, and the certificate would be withheld until that subject alone was rewritten and passed. The other six subjects stay on the record permanently. That is the practical reality of the NC(V) certificate: it is issued for the level only when all seven subjects are cleared, but the individual subject credits do not expire or need repeating.

The rewrite and supplementary route

A candidate who fails one or more NC(V) subjects has two routes to clear the outstanding work. The first is the mid-year supplementary examination, which is written in the first half of the following academic year. The second is the end-of-year national examination, taken alongside the next cohort. Both routes give the candidate a chance to rewrite only the subjects that were not passed, and the passing of a subject via either route counts towards the certificate for that level.

What differs between the two routes is timing rather than substance. The supplementary is usually taken by candidates who want to clear a subject as quickly as possible, typically because they need the certificate to enter a university or to start a workplace programme. The end-of-year route is taken by candidates who need more preparation time or who are already working and studying part-time.

In most cases the ICASS mark and, where the subject uses one, the ISAT practical mark are carried over into the rewrite, provided the rewrite is taken inside the validity window that the college applies. This matters because it means a candidate who performed well on the practical and internal work but underperformed on the examination does not have to rebuild the internal record from scratch. The examination is the only component that is rewritten, and the composite is recalculated with the new examination mark. Always confirm the exact carry-over rule with your college examination office before registering for the rewrite.

Common mistakes that delay certification

A handful of recurring errors delay NC(V) certification by a term or a full year. Understanding them in advance is worth the read.

Assuming all subjects use the same weighting. The fundamental subjects use 50:50 and the vocational subjects use 25:25:50. A candidate who applies the wrong weighting to the wrong subject will misjudge their own standing and may not realise a subject is at risk until the final mark is published.

Neglecting the ISAT practical. The ISAT carries 25 per cent of the vocational subject mark. Candidates sometimes treat the practical as a formality, only to discover that a weaker ISAT mark caps the achievable final. The practical is a genuine component with a national assessment instrument behind it, and it deserves the same preparation as the examination paper.

Ignoring the Mathematics threshold. Mathematics or Mathematical Literacy passes at 30 per cent, which is lower than the other thresholds, and some candidates assume that a fail in a fundamental subject means the whole qualification is lost. A near miss on Mathematics is the easiest of the seven thresholds to clear in a rewrite, and it should be prioritised accordingly.

Letting a single vocational subject block the certificate. A candidate who passes six subjects and fails one vocational subject does not receive the certificate. The subject credits for the six passes are retained, so the only work outstanding is the one failed subject, but the certificate is only issued once every subject is cleared. Candidates who delay the rewrite past the validity window can lose the ICASS and ISAT credit and be required to re-register the subject in full.

Assuming NC(V) Level 4 is the same as a National Senior Certificate. The two qualifications are different, and the university admission requirements that apply to the NSC do not map directly onto NC(V). An NC(V) Level 4 graduate can apply to a university or University of Technology, but the specific subject combinations and minimum marks required differ by programme, and the faculty prospectus for the year of application is the definitive source.

Missing the rewrite registration deadline. The supplementary and end-of-year examination windows are published on a fixed calendar, and registration deadlines fall well before the examination dates. Candidates who miss the registration deadline for the supplementary window have to wait for the next end-of-year cycle.

Where this calculator stops

This tool estimates the final mark for every NC(V) subject you enter, applies the DHET weighting rules and pass thresholds, and reports whether each subject clears and whether the certificate for the level is on track. It does not know your college's exact ICASS composition, does not know whether the ISAT instrument for your subject includes a specific sub-minimum requirement that the college applies in addition to the 25 per cent weighting, and does not know the validity window your college applies to a rewrite. It also does not know the specific university admission requirements for your programme of interest, or the specific subject combination required for the qualification you are applying to.

The pass thresholds used here match the published DHET NC(V) framework. The final, official outcome is the one recorded on the statement of results issued by DHET after the examination scripts have been marked and moderated. Use this calculator to see where you stand, to plan the exam strategy that gets each subject over its threshold, and to understand what a rewrite would need to achieve to clear an outstanding subject.

Frequently asked questions

How is an NC(V) subject final mark calculated?

The DHET framework uses two different weightings depending on the subject type. For a fundamental subject such as Language, Life Orientation, or Mathematics, the final mark is ICASS multiplied by 0.50 plus the national examination mark multiplied by 0.50. For a vocational subject, the final mark is ICASS multiplied by 0.25 plus the ISAT practical assessment mark multiplied by 0.25 plus the national examination mark multiplied by 0.50. In both cases the result is a percentage out of 100 and is the figure the college records on the statement of results.

Why do vocational subjects require 50 per cent while Mathematics requires only 30 per cent?

The DHET and Umalusi framework sets a higher competence threshold for vocational specialisations because they carry direct workplace and safety implications once the candidate enters industry. A 50 per cent pass on a vocational subject demonstrates both theoretical and practical readiness for that trade. The 30 per cent threshold on Mathematics or Mathematical Literacy reflects the general numeracy requirement that runs alongside a vocational specialisation, and it is set at the same baseline used at the secondary school level.

What happens if I fail one vocational subject in NC(V)?

Under the Umalusi guidelines, subject credits are retained permanently. You do not repeat the entire year. You register to rewrite only the failed subject in the mid-year supplementary examination or in the next end-of-year national examination cycle. Your existing ICASS and ISAT marks can be carried into the rewrite if they are still within the validity window the college applies, so a strong practical record does not have to be rebuilt from scratch.

What is the difference between NC(V) and NATED Report 191?

NC(V) is the three-year full-time National Certificate (Vocational) programme offered at Levels 2, 3, and 4, and it combines general academic subjects with practical vocational training. It is designed mainly for Grade 9 school leavers, though higher-grade entrants are also admitted. NATED Report 191 is the older trimester or semester modular stream that runs from N1 to N6 in Engineering and N4 to N6 in Business Studies, and it is typically taken after Grade 11 or Grade 12. The two routes lead to different qualifications, are assessed under different DHET frameworks, and progress through different workplace pathways.

Can I study at a university with an NC(V) Level 4 qualification?

Yes, subject to the specific admission requirements of the programme you are applying to. An NC(V) Level 4 certificate with a pass in English at the required level, a pass in Mathematics or Mathematical Literacy at the required level, and the vocational subjects that match the intended field of study can qualify a candidate for entry into a Higher Certificate, Diploma, or in some cases a Bachelor's degree at a South African university or University of Technology. The exact subject combination and minimum marks differ by institution and by programme, so the faculty prospectus for the year of application is the definitive source.

How long do NC(V) subject credits stay valid?

Individual subject passes are retained on the candidate's record under Umalusi rules and remain valid for the purposes of certification, but there is a practical limit on how long the ICASS and ISAT practical marks can be carried before a subject rewrite. Colleges generally apply a validity window of one to two years for the internal marks, which means a candidate who intends to clear a failed subject should plan the rewrite inside that window. After the window closes, the subject is re-registered in full, which means a new internal ICASS and ISAT record alongside a fresh national examination attempt.

Are ICASS and ISAT marks carried over if I rewrite a subject?

In most cases yes, provided the rewrite is taken inside the validity window that the college applies. The ICASS mark and, where the subject uses one, the ISAT practical mark are retained on the candidate's record and combined with the new national examination mark using the same 25:25:50 weighting for vocational subjects. This is what allows a candidate who performed well on the practical and internal work but underperformed on the examination to improve the composite by rewriting only the exam paper. Always confirm the exact carry-over rule with your college examination office before registering for the rewrite.
