How to write a cover letter that feels specific

A cover letter works best when it answers a simple question: why does this person make sense for this role at this organisation? It should not repeat an entire resume or use broad praise that could be sent to every employer. This AI cover letter generator helps you organise a first draft around a job title, company, relevant strengths, and evidence from your experience. You remain in control of the facts, voice, and final decision to send it.

Begin by reading the vacancy carefully. Notice the main responsibility, the problem the employer needs solved, and the skills repeated across the description. Select two or three of those themes that genuinely match your background. If the role asks for scheduling, explain a time you coordinated people or deadlines. If it asks for customer communication, describe the channel, audience, and outcome. A short specific example is usually more persuasive than a long list of adjectives.

What makes a useful prompt

Enter the exact or closest job title, the company name, and the skills that connect your experience to the vacancy. In the experience box, mention roles, projects, education, volunteering, or freelance work that is relevant. The evidence box is where you can add a result, challenge, improvement, or reason for your interest. You do not need perfect sentences. Notes such as “managed weekly report for five departments” or “volunteered to train new members” give the writer enough direction to create a structured draft.

Do not add confidential client information, passwords, identity numbers, or private contact details that the employer does not need. If the opportunity has a named hiring manager, you can replace “Dear Hiring Manager” after checking the spelling. If the vacancy asks for a particular format or question, follow that instruction instead of forcing everything into a standard letter.

Example for a career changer

Suppose a retail supervisor is applying for a customer success role at a software company. They may not have used the company’s exact platform, but they can describe coaching staff, resolving customer issues, tracking recurring problems, and explaining products clearly. A useful letter connects those transferable behaviours to the new role without pretending the applicant has software experience they do not have. It can say that they are ready to learn the platform and then point to evidence of learning new systems quickly.

For a recent graduate, the evidence might be a final project, internship, student association, volunteer programme, or part-time job. The goal is not to apologise for being early in a career. The goal is to show how the applicant has practised the behaviours the employer needs. For an experienced professional, choose a recent result and explain why it is relevant now. Avoid turning the letter into a second page of every achievement.

Edit before you send

After generating, read the draft as the employer would. Remove repeated phrases, generic claims, and anything that sounds unlike you. Replace placeholders with exact details. Check that the company name and role are correct, that the opening paragraph shows a clear reason for applying, and that the middle paragraph includes evidence rather than only enthusiasm. Keep the closing confident and simple. A cover letter should support the resume, not compete with it.

Personalisation does not mean copying the vacancy word for word. Use the employer’s language when it accurately describes your experience, but keep the sentences natural. If you have a portfolio, certificate, or project that supports the application, mention it briefly and link to it in the application system. Save the final letter with a clear file name and check whether the employer requested PDF, DOCX, or plain text.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use the same letter for every application?

Use a consistent structure, but tailor the role, company, evidence, and reason for interest for every meaningful application.

Should a cover letter be one page?

Usually a concise page is enough, but follow the employer’s instructions and prioritise relevant evidence over a fixed word count.

What if I do not know the hiring manager?

“Dear Hiring Manager” is a reasonable option. You can also use a team name when the vacancy clearly identifies it.

Does the tool send the letter to an employer?

No. It only creates editable text. You must review it and submit it through the employer’s requested channel.
