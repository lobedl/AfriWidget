What this finder actually does

This tool is a search and comparison engine for accredited Australian undergraduate degrees. You set a few filters, type a keyword if you want, and the tool returns a shortlist of programmes that match. You can compare up to three programmes side by side across ATAR cutoff, duration, campus, prerequisite subjects, Commonwealth Supported Place fee band, and graduate starting salary.

The search box suggests matches as you type. Start typing "Nursing" and you will see the nursing degrees in the dataset. Type "UNSW" and you will see the University of New South Wales programmes. Type "Cyber" and you will see the cyber security degrees. Clicking a suggestion loads that course directly, and pressing the search button instead returns the full filtered list.

Everything runs inside your browser. The filtering, the suggestions, the comparison, and the match badges are all computed on your device. Nothing you type is sent anywhere unless you deliberately press the optional AI button, and even then only an anonymous summary of your filter criteria travels.

How Australian university admissions works

Every Australian state runs its own tertiary admissions centre, and applicants apply through the centre rather than to each university individually. New South Wales and the ACT use the Universities Admissions Centre. Victoria uses VTAC. Queensland uses QTAC. Western Australia uses TISC. South Australia and the Northern Territory use SATAC. Tasmania runs direct admissions through the University of Tasmania.

The centre converts your Year 12 results into a rank, applies any adjustment factors you qualify for, and passes the ranked list to the universities. The universities then make offers in rounds, starting with the highest-ranked applicants and working down until each programme is full.

The important thing to understand is that there is no single national cutoff. Each programme at each university publishes its own lowest selection rank for the most recent cycle, and that number is what the tables in this tool reflect. A course that admits at 70 at one institution might sit at 90 at another, even when the degree title is identical.

ATAR, selection rank, and adjustment factors

The Australian Tertiary Admission Rank is a number between 0 and 99.95 that shows where you sit relative to your Year 12 cohort. It is not a mark. An ATAR of 80 means you performed better than 80 per cent of your state's Year 12 cohort, not that you scored 80 per cent on anything.

The selection rank is your ATAR after adjustment factors have been applied. Adjustment factors are extra points added by the university or the admissions centre to recognise disadvantage, regional or rural schooling, elite athletic status, or strong performance in specific Year 12 subjects such as advanced mathematics or a language. They can add anywhere between one and ten points to your base ATAR depending on the category and the institution.

A simple way to think about it. Your ATAR is the starting point. Your selection rank is what actually competes for a place. Two applicants with the same ATAR can have very different selection ranks if one of them qualifies for a scheme that the other does not. Check each university's adjustment factor page before you decide a programme is out of reach.

Universities also publish two related numbers: the guaranteed ATAR and the lowest selection rank. The guaranteed ATAR is a fixed number that promises you an offer if you clear it and meet the prerequisites. The lowest selection rank is the score of the last applicant admitted in the most recent cycle, and it can be higher or lower than the guaranteed ATAR depending on demand.

Prerequisites and assumed knowledge

Prerequisites and assumed knowledge are two different things, and mixing them up is one of the most common mistakes in Australian university applications.

A prerequisite is a subject you must have passed in Year 12, often with a minimum study score, before the university will make an offer. Medicine usually requires Chemistry. Engineering usually requires Mathematics Methods or Specialist Mathematics. Nursing usually requires English. If you have not met the prerequisite, the application is not considered, no matter how strong the ATAR.

Assumed knowledge is a recommendation. The university expects you to have covered the material before you start the degree, but you will not be denied an offer if you have not. In practice, most universities will enrol you in a bridging unit during the first semester to catch up, and some will recommend that you complete the bridging unit before you start. Physics and Specialist Mathematics are the two most common assumed knowledge subjects for engineering and science degrees.

Commonwealth Supported Places and fee bands

For Australian citizens, permanent humanitarian visa holders, and eligible New Zealand special category visa holders, most undergraduate places are Commonwealth Supported Places. Under a Commonwealth Supported Place, the Australian government pays a substantial part of your tuition directly to the university, and you pay a subsidised student contribution. If you are eligible for HECS-HELP, the student contribution can be deferred as a loan and repaid through the tax system once your income reaches the repayment threshold.

The government classifies each field of study into one of four bands, and the student contribution rises across the bands. The numbers move with indexation each year, and the pattern below reflects the standard 2025 to 2026 range.

BandFields coveredApproximate student contribution
Band 1Nursing, teaching, agriculture, English, mathematics, clinical psychology, languagesAbout A$4,445 a year
Band 2Science, engineering, information technology, allied health, architecture, built environmentAbout A$8,948 a year
Band 3Medicine, dentistry, veterinary scienceAbout A$12,720 a year
Band 4Law, commerce, business, economics, communications, creative artsAbout A$16,329 a year

The pattern is worth understanding because it is counter-intuitive. The courses that lead to the highest starting salaries, law and commerce, sit in the band with the smallest government subsidy and the largest student contribution. The courses that lead to the lowest starting salaries, nursing and teaching, sit in the band with the largest government subsidy. The policy is designed to nudge students toward fields with public value, not to reward the highest earners.

University alliances and what they mean

Australian universities cluster into alliances that reflect shared research priorities and similar admissions profiles. The two best-known are the Group of Eight and the Australian Technology Network.

The Group of Eight is the research-intensive group: the University of Melbourne, the University of Sydney, the Australian National University, UNSW Sydney, the University of Queensland, Monash University, the University of Western Australia, and the University of Adelaide. Cutoffs are typically the highest in the country, often in the high eighties or the nineties for competitive programmes.

The Australian Technology Network is the industry-focused group: UTS, RMIT, QUT, Curtin, UniSA, and Deakin. Cutoffs are typically five to fifteen points lower than the Group of Eight for the same field, and the degrees integrate work placements and industry projects more heavily.

The regional and innovative group includes Griffith, Flinders, La Trobe, Western Sydney, James Cook, Wollongong, Newcastle, Charles Sturt, New England, Southern Queensland, and CQUniversity. Cutoffs tend to be the most accessible, and many programmes offer flexible or online study modes.

The alliance does not decide quality on its own. A nursing graduate from a regional university is just as registered as a nursing graduate from a Group of Eight institution. The alliance matters most for research pathways, for specific professional accreditations, and for the network you build while you study.

A worked search example

Suppose a student in New South Wales has an estimated ATAR of 88 and wants to study Computer Science in Sydney. They set the field filter to Engineering and Computer Science, the state filter to New South Wales, and the ATAR field to 88. They leave the group filter on All, and press search.

The tool returns three relevant programmes in the dataset. UNSW Sydney's Bachelor of Science in Computer Science has a lowest selection rank of 90, which puts the applicant two points below the cutoff. The match badge shows "Reach" and suggests an adjustment factor or early entry pathway. The University of Technology Sydney's Bachelor of Computing Science Honours has a rank of 84, which the applicant clears by four points, and the badge shows "Target match". Western Sydney University's Bachelor of Information Technology in Cyber Security has a rank of 70, which the applicant clears by eighteen points, and the badge shows "High chance".

The shortlist now has one reach, one target, and one safe programme. That is exactly the shape a well-constructed university application should have. The applicant can add the three to the comparison bar, open the side-by-side matrix, and check prerequisites, campus, and fee band before deciding which to list first.

Common mistakes when planning

The same handful of mistakes come up in almost every Australian university application season.

Confusing ATAR with a percentage. An ATAR of 80 is a rank, not a mark. Students who scored 80 per cent on their exams often have an ATAR that is significantly higher or lower, depending on how their cohort performed.

Ignoring prerequisites. A strong ATAR does not compensate for a missing prerequisite. Engineering without Mathematics Methods, or Medicine without Chemistry, will not proceed to offer stage regardless of the rank.

Applying to one course only. Every admissions centre allows multiple preferences, and most applicants should use all of them. A single-preference application has no fallback if the cutoff moves against you.

Assuming the cutoff is fixed. Cutoffs move every cycle with the applicant pool and the number of places. A number that was accurate two cycles ago may be five points off today.

Overlooking adjustment factors. Many applicants qualify for schemes they do not realise exist. Regional schooling, financial hardship, a medical condition that affected Year 12, elite athlete status, or a high score in an advanced subject can all add points. Check each university's adjustment factor page before you write off a reach programme.

Choosing by alliance alone. The Group of Eight is not automatically the right choice. A student who would thrive in a smaller cohort with more industry placement might do better at an ATN or regional institution, even when their ATAR would clear the higher Group of Eight cutoff.

Where this tool stops

This finder gives you a searchable, sortable view of a curated dataset of Australian undergraduate degrees, with ATAR cutoffs, fee bands, and prerequisites set against a typical recent cycle. It also gives you a filter that sorts programmes into safe, target, reach, and pathway categories based on the ATAR you enter.

It does not know your adjustment factor eligibility, your English language test scores if you studied overseas, the specific prerequisite subjects your Year 12 curriculum offered, or whether the current cycle has changed a programme's requirements. It does not know which round you will be considered in, or how many places remain unfilled at the time of your application.

What it does give you is a fast, honest first read. Use it to notice which programmes are genuinely in reach, which need an adjustment factor to become realistic, and which would require a pathway route through a diploma or an internal transfer. Then take that shortlist to the specific admissions centre and the specific university page for the year you are applying in. Those are the authority. This tool is the starting point that gets you there faster.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an ATAR prerequisite and assumed knowledge?

A prerequisite is a subject you must have passed in Year 12, often with a minimum study score, before the university will make an offer. Assumed knowledge is a recommendation. The university expects you to have covered the material but will still admit you without it, sometimes requiring a bridging unit in your first semester to catch up.

What is the difference between the lowest selection rank and a guaranteed ATAR?

The lowest selection rank is the score of the last applicant admitted in the most recent cycle, after adjustment factors were applied. A guaranteed ATAR is a fixed number published in advance, below which you will still be considered but above which you are promised an offer provided you meet the prerequisites. Guaranteed ATARs are usually lower than the actual published lowest selection rank for the same programme.

What are adjustment factors or bonus points?

Adjustment factors are extra points added to your base ATAR before ranking. Common categories include educational access schemes for financial or medical disadvantage, regional and rural school bonuses, elite athlete provisions, and subject bonuses for high scores in advanced mathematics or a language. They are applied by the university or the tertiary admissions centre, not by the applicant.

Do international students use the same course finder?

Yes. International students sit outside the Commonwealth Supported Place system and pay full international tuition, typically between thirty-four thousand and fifty-two thousand Australian dollars a year. The ATAR cutoffs, course structures, prerequisites, and English language requirements such as IELTS or PTE Academic apply in the same way, and the search here returns the same programmes.

What is an Honours degree in Australia?

Honours is either an integrated fourth year inside a longer undergraduate programme, common in Engineering, Law, and Psychology, or a separate one-year research year completed after a three-year Bachelor degree. Graduates with First Class or Upper Second Class Honours can apply directly to a PhD or Master of Philosophy without first completing a coursework Masters.

How do the Commonwealth Supported Place fee bands work?

The Australian government classifies each field of study into one of four bands. Band one covers nursing, teaching, agriculture, and languages, where the student pays the smallest share. Band two covers science, engineering, and allied health. Band three covers medicine, dentistry, and veterinary science. Band four covers law, commerce, and communications, where the student pays the largest share of the tuition.

Can I use this tool if I studied outside Australia?

Yes, for research purposes. The ATAR is an Australian rank, so a non-Australian qualification has to be converted by the university or the relevant tertiary admissions centre before it can be compared against the cutoffs shown here. Use the finder to identify programmes you are interested in, then check the institution's international admissions page for its specific conversion and English language requirements.

Why do the same course names appear at different universities with different ATARs?

Demand and capacity differ between institutions. A Bachelor of Commerce at a Group of Eight university attracts far more first-preference applications than the equivalent degree at a smaller or regional institution, and the cut-off rises accordingly. The degree itself may be comparable in content, but the competition for a place is not.

Are the ATAR cutoffs in this tool current?

The figures shown are planning references drawn from published admissions pages and recent cycles. Cutoffs move from year to year with the size of the applicant pool and the number of available places. For the current cycle, always check the specific programme page at the relevant tertiary admissions centre or on the university website.

Is my search sent anywhere?

No. Every search, filter, and comparison runs inside your browser, and nothing you type leaves your device. If you choose to press the optional AI button, only an anonymous summary of your filter criteria and the number of matching programmes is sent to generate a short written response. Nothing is stored.
