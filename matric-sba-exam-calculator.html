What this calculator does

This tool takes the two numbers that decide every NSC subject, the School-Based Assessment mark and the exam mark, and combines them into a single final percentage for each subject. From there it works out your NSC level, adds up your APS points, and states the highest pass level your current record qualifies you for. It uses the standard South African rules that the Department of Basic Education and Umalusi apply across every public and independent school that writes the NSC.

The page exists because the arithmetic that sits behind a Matric certificate is simple on paper but easy to get wrong by hand. Seven subjects, two marks per subject, four pass levels with different requirements, and an APS that adds up differently depending on which subjects are counted. Running that in your head once is possible. Running it for three trial scenarios and a rewrite decision is not. The calculator handles it in a fraction of a second and shows you exactly where each figure came from.

Nothing here is stored. The tool reads the numbers you type into the browser, computes the result in the same tab, and leaves no trace. If you press the AI insight button, only an anonymous summary of the total subject count, average mark, pass level, and APS figure is sent to a small serverless function that runs once and then stops. Subject names, individual marks, and any personal details stay on your device.

How the NSC final mark is worked out

Every NSC subject with an external paper uses the same weighting: 25 per cent from School-Based Assessment and 75 per cent from the November examination. Life Orientation is the only exception, and it is assessed entirely inside the school.

Final subject mark = (SBA × 0.25) + (Exam × 0.75)
Life Orientation = 100% internal SBA and CAT

The formula itself is short. What makes it worth understanding is what each side of the split actually represents. The SBA includes term tests, formal assignments, practical tasks, and the September trial examination, all marked inside the school and moderated externally. It is the part of your year that is already banked before November. The exam is a single paper, or sometimes two papers, written under national conditions and marked by external markers through the provincial education departments.

Because the exam carries three quarters of the final mark, it is the exam that sets the ceiling on your subject result. The SBA sets the floor. A strong SBA year means you cannot finish below a certain level in that subject, even if the exam goes badly. A weak SBA year means the exam has to carry a heavier load to reach the level you are aiming for. That asymmetry is the whole reason students are encouraged to treat the trial examination and term tests as the first half of the real thing, not as a rehearsal.

Every input explained

The tool uses three fields per subject, and understanding what each one expects is the fastest way to get a reliable result.

Subject name. Optional, but useful if you want the breakdown table to show a readable name rather than a row number. Type the subject as it appears on your school timetable or report card. Life Orientation is prefilled in the fourth row because that subject sits in the same position for almost every NSC candidate.

SBA mark. A percentage between zero and one hundred. This is your current school-based average for the subject, as reported on your latest school report. If you are entering marks before the trial examination has been written, use the SBA mark up to the most recent completed task. Schools usually provide a running SBA figure to every Grade 12 candidate during the year, and that running figure is what belongs here.

Exam mark. A percentage between zero and one hundred. If you have already written the November examination, use the mark from your provisional or final statement of results. If you have not, use the mark from your trial examination as a realistic prediction, or enter a target mark that you want to test against. The calculator does not distinguish between a real and a predicted exam mark, so it is worth being honest with yourself about which one you are entering.

The Life Orientation row is different on purpose. The fourth subject has a single input rather than two, because the final mark is the internal assessment mark with no external paper attached. The note beside it says so. If your school reports a Life Orientation mark that already includes the Common Assessment Task, that is the number to enter.

Take a candidate who has already written the November exams and has provisional marks for every subject. The seven subjects and their SBA and exam figures look like this.

Every subject crosses the 30 per cent pass threshold except none in this example, and five subjects sit at Level 4 or above. The average final mark across the six academic subjects is around 61, and the Home Language mark is comfortably above the 40 per cent minimum. Running the numbers gives a Bachelor's Pass, because four subjects sit at 50 per cent or above (English, Afrikaans, Life Sciences, and Life Orientation) and the remaining subjects are above 30 per cent. The APS comes out around 34 once Life Orientation is either excluded or counted at half weight depending on the university's rule.

Now imagine the Mathematics mark was a 48 instead of 54. That single change pulls the average down by a point and drops one of the four 50 per cent subjects to a 40 per cent subject. The Bachelor's Pass survives only if another subject steps up. This is the kind of scenario the tool is built to test. Change one number, run again, and see whether the pass level holds or collapses. Doing that by hand across seven subjects is where most candidates give up and guess. Doing it here takes a second.

The four official NSC pass levels

Umalusi issues one of four pass levels on every NSC certificate. Each level has specific requirements, and each one unlocks a different set of study options.

Bachelor's Pass. Home Language at 40 per cent or higher, four subjects at 50 per cent or higher (Life Orientation excluded from the four), and two subjects at 30 per cent or higher. This is the minimum qualification for degree studies at any South African university. Meeting it does not guarantee a place in a competitive faculty, because each faculty still applies its own APS cut-off, but it makes you eligible to apply.

Diploma Pass. Home Language at 40 per cent or higher, four subjects at 40 per cent or higher, and two subjects at 30 per cent or higher. This level opens admission to diploma programmes at universities of technology and to many TVET college diplomas. Some universities also offer extended curriculum programmes that accept diploma-pass candidates into a four-year degree with a foundation year.

Higher Certificate Pass. Home Language at 40 per cent or higher, two subjects at 40 per cent or higher, and three subjects at 30 per cent or higher. This is the entry qualification for higher certificate programmes, which are typically one-year vocational qualifications at a college or university. It also qualifies a candidate to apply for certain bridging programmes that lead into a diploma or degree on successful completion.

NSC Pass. Three subjects at 40 per cent or higher, two subjects at 30 per cent or higher, and one subject at 20 per cent or higher. This is the minimum pass level for the certificate itself. It does not open university admission, but it certifies that the candidate completed Grade 12 and is the level that many entry-level jobs and learnerships require.

One detail worth holding onto. The four subjects that count for the Bachelor's and Diploma pass levels must be from the list of designated subjects, which excludes Life Orientation. That single rule trips up more candidates than any other part of the NSC. The subject that many students find easiest, Life Orientation, is the one that does not count toward the most important pass level.

The APS points that come out of your Matric

Universities do not admit on pass level alone. They rank applicants by an Admission Point Score, which translates each final subject mark into a level and adds the levels together. The standard scale runs from 1 for marks below 30 per cent up to 7 for marks at 80 per cent or higher.

80 to 100 per centLevel 7 — Outstanding7
70 to 79 per centLevel 6 — Meritorious6
60 to 69 per centLevel 5 — Substantial5
50 to 59 per centLevel 4 — Adequate4
40 to 49 per centLevel 3 — Moderate3
30 to 39 per centLevel 2 — Elementary2
0 to 29 per centLevel 1 — Not achieved1

Most universities count the six best subjects, exclude Life Orientation, and add the points to get a maximum APS of 42. A handful of universities use a maximum of 48 by counting seven subjects with Life Orientation at half weight. A few technical universities use slightly different weightings altogether. The number the calculator returns assumes the most common configuration, which is six subjects excluding Life Orientation, and shows the total out of 42 so the ceiling is clear.

The practical implication is that one level difference in one subject moves the APS by a point. Two level differences in a high-credit subject move it by two. Chasing a single extra level in a subject where you sit just below a boundary is often the most efficient way to lift the APS, because the level crossing is a step function rather than a smooth curve. The tool makes those boundaries visible, so you can see which marks are sitting one point below a threshold and which are comfortably inside their band.

Common mistakes in Matric planning

Seven mistakes come up repeatedly when Grade 12 candidates and their families work through this kind of calculation.

Confusing the SBA mark on the school report with the final SBA mark. The number on a mid-year report is usually a running figure that will change after the trial examination and the final school-based submission. Treat that number as an estimate until the school confirms the final SBA mark in October.

Assuming the trial exam is the exam mark. The trial is a valuable predictor, but every year some candidates perform very differently on the real paper. Trials marked leniently produce optimistic predictions, and trials marked strictly produce pessimistic ones. Use the trial as a range, not a single number.

Ignoring Life Orientation in the pass level calculation. Many candidates assume all seven subjects count towards the Bachelor's Pass. Only six count towards the top two pass levels, and Life Orientation is one of the two that do not. A candidate who only has four 50 per cent subjects including Life Orientation ends up with a Diploma Pass, not a Bachelor's Pass.

Leaving the Home Language requirement until last. Home Language must be at 40 per cent or higher for every pass level above the standard NSC. A Home Language mark of 39 per cent with everything else in order still produces a fail on the higher levels. This requirement is not negotiable and cannot be offset by another subject.

Chasing marks in subjects that are already comfortably above the band. Moving a subject from 82 per cent to 85 per cent does not change the level, so it does not move the APS. Moving a subject from 48 per cent to 52 per cent changes the level from 3 to 4 and moves the APS by a full point for the same effort. Chase the boundaries.

Forgetting that Mathematics and Mathematical Literacy are not interchangeable for many degrees. A Bachelor's Pass is one thing, but a Bachelor of Science in Engineering or Actuarial Science typically requires Mathematics, not Mathematical Literacy, regardless of the pass level. The university prospectus governs this, and it is worth checking before Grade 10 subject choices are locked in, because switching between the two after that point is difficult.

Not accounting for the exam weight when deciding how to spend November study time. The exam is 75 per cent of the subject mark. A candidate who spends as much time on SBA catch-up as on exam preparation in the final weeks is optimising the smaller half of the equation. The final three months before the exam belong to exam preparation.

Where this tool stops

This calculator estimates the NSC pass level and APS from the marks you enter. It does not know your school's specific SBA moderation history, does not know which subjects your school offers in the seventh slot, and does not model the effect of an application for a remark, a rewrite, or a supplementary examination. It also does not know the specific APS cut-offs of any individual university or programme, which change from year to year.

It does not replace the official channels. Your school's academic head, the provincial education department, and Umalusi are the sources of truth on your SBA mark, your official NSC result, and your pass level. Individual university prospectuses govern their own APS requirements and subject prerequisites. What this tool gives you is a fast, transparent estimate of where you sit today, so the conversations with those official channels start from a clear place.

The pass level bands and APS rules used here follow the standard national framework. Where an institution applies an unusual rule, the institution's version takes priority. Use the result as a planning aid, not as a substitute for the certificate Umalusi issues.

Frequently asked questions

What percentage does SBA count towards the final Matric mark?

School-Based Assessment counts for 25 per cent of the final mark in every NSC subject except Life Orientation. The October and November external examination carries the remaining 75 per cent. Life Orientation is different because it is assessed entirely inside the school through Common Assessment Tasks and internal SBA, with no external paper of its own.

Can a strong SBA mark save a weak exam paper?

Only up to a mathematical ceiling. An SBA mark of 80 per cent contributes twenty guaranteed points toward the hundred-point final total, so it sets a floor under your final result. But the exam still moves seventy-five of those hundred points, which means it decides the difference between a Level 4 and a Level 6 in almost every subject. SBA stabilises the outcome. The exam still sets the ceiling.

How is Life Orientation calculated in the NSC?

Life Orientation is assessed entirely through internal assessment, sometimes called the Common Assessment Task or CAT. There is no external November paper for the subject. Your school submits the internal mark directly to the Department of Basic Education, and that mark becomes your final Life Orientation result. Because the subject carries fewer credits than the other six, universities usually do not count it toward the APS unless they state otherwise in their prospectus.

What is the difference between a Bachelor's Pass and a university acceptance?

A Bachelor's Pass is the minimum statutory qualification that makes you eligible to apply for degree studies at any South African university. It is issued by Umalusi based on your NSC results. University acceptance is a separate decision. Each faculty sets its own Admission Point Score cutoff for each programme, and in competitive degrees such as medicine, engineering, and actuarial science, those cutoffs sit well above the minimum Bachelor's Pass requirement. Meeting one does not guarantee the other.

Can I rewrite Matric subjects after getting my results?

Yes. The May and June Second Chance Matric Programme lets you rewrite specific subjects to improve your marks. You can also register for the November examination as a repeater if you choose to sit the full set again. When you rewrite, the SBA component is usually carried over or reassessed depending on which programme you register for. Check the Department of Basic Education guidelines for the specific rules that apply to your situation.

How does the APS score work?

The Admission Point Score is a numeric total that universities use to rank applicants. Each NSC subject produces a level between one and seven, based on the final percentage. Those levels add up to an APS out of forty-two in most systems, with Life Orientation either excluded or counted at half weight by many universities. Some institutions cap or exclude certain subjects, so the exact figure depends on the university. Use the APS here as a planning figure and confirm against the specific prospectus.

Does the SBA weighting change between subjects?

No. The 25 per cent SBA and 75 per cent exam split applies uniformly to every NSC subject that has an external paper. The one exception is Life Orientation, which is 100 per cent internal. Practical and technical subjects sometimes carry a formal Practical Assessment Task as a separate component, but it is folded into the SBA share rather than added on top. There is no scenario in the standard NSC where SBA counts more than 25 per cent in a subject with an external paper.

What happens if I fail one subject?

You can still pass the NSC with one failed subject if you meet the other requirements, because the qualification requires a pass in six of your seven subjects. A single Level 1 will not block the certificate, but it can block a specific pass level. For example, a failed Mathematics paper blocks a Bachelor's Pass that would have been secured through Mathematics as a high-credit subject. The remedy is to rewrite the failed subject or to substitute it in a future registration with a school that supports it.

Do independent and private schools use the same SBA rules?

Yes. Independent schools that write the NSC follow the same SBA rules set by the Department of Basic Education. Their internal assessments are subject to the same moderation by Umalusi and by the provincial education departments. The 25 per cent SBA and 75 per cent exam split applies without variation. Some independent schools also offer the IEB or Cambridge qualifications alongside the NSC, and those have their own rules that are set by their own examination boards.

Can I combine my SBA and exam mark myself without a calculator?

Yes. Multiply your SBA mark by 0.25 and your exam mark by 0.75, then add the two figures together. The result is your final percentage for that subject. Doing this by hand is straightforward for one subject but quickly becomes tedious across seven, especially when you are also tracking the APS and pass level. The calculator on this page automates the whole sequence and shows the pass level at a glance.

Does this tool store my Matric marks?

No. Every calculation runs inside your browser tab and nothing is stored. If you press the AI insight button, only an anonymous summary of the number of subjects entered, the subjects that met pass level, the average final mark, the pass level awarded, and the computed APS is sent to a small serverless function that runs once and then stops. Subject names, individual marks, and any personal details never leave your device.
