Comprehensive Guide to South African TVET NATED (Report 191 N1–N6) Pass Calculation & Assessment Architecture

Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) in South Africa represents a cornerstone of the national skills development strategy administered by the Department of Higher Education and Training (DHET). The Report 191 National Accredited Technical Education Diploma (NATED) programmes—spanning Engineering Studies (N1 through N6), Business Studies (N4 through N6), Utility Studies, and Educare—equip students with practical, industry-aligned competencies.

The AfriWidget South African TVET NATED Pass Calculator executes deterministic pass evaluations based on the statutory DHET assessment framework. It accurately integrates your continuous internal term assessment (ICASS – Internal Continuous Assessment, weighted at 40%) with your final external national examination mark (weighted at 60%), rigorously enforces the mandatory 40% examination sub-minimum requirement, and determines official achievement classifications (Pass, Distinction, or Sub-Minimum Fail).

The DHET Report 191 Assessment Weighting Formula

In all accredited public and private TVET colleges across South Africa (e.g., False Bay College, Ekurhuleni West College, South West Gauteng TVET, Tshwane North TVET, and Northlink College), NATED subject outcomes are governed by the statutory 40:60 assessment split:

  • Internal Continuous Assessment (ICASS - 40%): Derived from classroom tests, practical vocational tasks, assignments, and internal trial examinations administered by your TVET college during the trimester or semester.
  • National Examination Component (60%): External national examination set, administered, and moderated nationwide by the Department of Higher Education and Training (DHET) and quality-assured by Umalusi.

Composite Mathematical Formula:

Final Subject Mark (%) = (ICASS Mark × 0.40) + (National Examination Mark × 0.60)

Mandatory DHET Pass Criteria & The Examination Sub-Minimum Rule

To be awarded a recognized pass in any NATED (N1 through N6) subject, a candidate must satisfy both of the following statutory conditions:

  1. Overall Weighted Composite Pass Rule:
  • The computed final subject mark must equal or exceed 40.0%.
  1. Mandatory Examination Sub-Minimum Rule (40% Minimum Exam Mark):
  • The candidate must achieve a raw score of at least 40.0% in the final external national examination paper.
  • Crucial Rule: Even if a student achieves an exceptional ICASS score (e.g., 90%), if the final examination mark falls below 40.0% (e.g., 38%), the overall result is formally classified as "Failed – Exam Sub-Minimum Not Met". A high internal continuous assessment mark cannot compensate for an unfulfilled examination sub-minimum.
  • Pass with Distinction | Composite Final Mark Range: 75% – 100% | Statutory Requirements: Exam Mark ≥ 40% | Academic Progression Standing: Full Subject Credit (Eligible for Merit Awards)
  • Standard Pass | Composite Final Mark Range: 50% – 74% | Statutory Requirements: Exam Mark ≥ 40% | Academic Progression Standing: Full Subject Credit (Clear Progression to Next N-Level)
  • Condoned / Minimum Pass | Composite Final Mark Range: 40% – 49% | Statutory Requirements: Exam Mark ≥ 40% | Academic Progression Standing: Subject Credit (Meets Baseline Certificate Threshold)
  • Sub-Minimum Failure | Composite Final Mark Range: Total ≥ 40%, but Exam < 40% | Statutory Requirements: Exam Mark < 40% | Academic Progression Standing: Fail (Must rewrite National Examination)
  • Outright Failure | Composite Final Mark Range: Final Total < 40% | Statutory Requirements: Any | Academic Progression Standing: Fail (Must repeat or write supplementary if eligible)

Worked Calculation Examples

Example 1: N3 Engineering Studies – Electrotechnics / Industrial Electronics

A student at Ekurhuleni East TVET College completes N3 Electrotechnics with the following marks:

  • Internal ICASS Term Mark: 58.0%
  • National Examination Mark: 62.0%

Calculation:

  • ICASS Contribution: 58.0 × 0.40 = 23.20%
  • Examination Contribution: 62.0 × 0.60 = 37.20%
  • Composite Final Mark: 23.20 + 37.20 = 60.40%
  • Sub-Minimum Audit: Final Exam Mark (62.0%) ≥ 40.0% (✓ Satisfied).
  • Official Result: PASS (Standard Pass – 60.4%).
  • Progression: The student earns full N3 subject credit and is eligible to register for N4 Engineering Studies.

Example 2: N4 Business Studies – Financial Accounting (The Sub-Minimum Trap)

A student at South West Gauteng TVET College excels in continuous term assignments but struggles in the final accounting examination:

  • Internal ICASS Term Mark: 78.0%
  • National Examination Mark: 36.0%

Calculation:

  • ICASS Contribution: 78.0 × 0.40 = 31.20%
  • Examination Contribution: 36.0 × 0.60 = 21.60%
  • Weighted Mathematical Sum: 31.20 + 21.60 = 52.80%
  • Sub-Minimum Audit: Final Exam Mark (36.0%) < 40.0% (✗ Fails sub-minimum).
  • Official Result: FAILED (Sub-Minimum Rule Not Met).
  • Academic Outcome: Despite an aggregate numerical score of 52.80%, the candidate does not pass because the external examination mark fell below the mandatory 40% baseline. The candidate is required to register for the next DHET examination cycle to rewrite the national examination paper.

Example 3: N6 Financial Management / Human Resource Management – Distinction Candidate

A candidate completing their final N6 trimester aims for a Cum Laude certificate:

  • Internal ICASS Term Mark: 84.0%
  • National Examination Mark: 88.0%

Calculation:

  • ICASS Contribution: 84.0 × 0.40 = 33.60%
  • Examination Contribution: 88.0 × 0.60 = 52.80%
  • Composite Final Mark: 33.60 + 52.80 = 86.40%
  • Sub-Minimum Audit: Final Exam Mark (88.0%) ≥ 40.0% (✓ Outstanding).
  • Official Result: PASS WITH DISTINCTION (86.4%).
  • Diploma Pathway: The candidate achieves distinction credit toward their N6 Certificate and can immediately commence their 18-month (2,000 hours) verified workplace experiential training to qualify for the National N Diploma.

The Pathway from N6 Certificate to the National N Diploma

In the South African higher education qualifications framework, completing four instructional subjects at N6 level awards the candidate the National N6 Certificate. To convert this certificate into the fully accredited National N Diploma (NQF Level 6):

  1. Instructional Subject Requirements:
  • Successful completion of all required subjects across N4, N5, and N6 levels (a total of 12 semester/trimester subjects).
  1. Workplace In-Service Training (Experiential Learning):
  • Business Studies: Minimum of 18 months (or 2,000 verified hours) of practical, relevant in-service workplace experience.
  • Engineering Studies: Minimum of 24 months (or 2,670 verified hours) of relevant industrial or trade workplace experience under certified mentorship.
  1. Logbook Submission & DHET Evaluation:
  • The candidate submits a completed, employer-signed DHET logbook and portfolio of evidence to their TVET college for verification and submission to the DHET National Diploma Directorate.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What happens if my ICASS mark is below 40% before the final examination?

Under DHET TVET regulations, a student must achieve an ICASS term mark of at least 40% (or 50% for specific occupational components) to obtain an examination admission permit (exam entry clearance). If your ICASS mark is below the college threshold, you may be barred from sitting the national examination.

How long is an ICASS mark valid in South African TVET colleges?

An ICASS mark is valid for a statutory period of one year (or two consecutive examination cycles). If you fail the national examination on your first attempt, you can carry over your valid ICASS mark to rewrite the national examination during the subsequent exam cycle without repeating all classroom assignments.

What is the exact difference between NATED (Report 191) and NCV (National Certificate Vocational)?
  • NATED (Report 191) programmes are structured as trimestral (Engineering: 10–12 weeks per N-level) or semestral (Business: 6 months per level) modular courses designed for post-matriculants (or Grade 11/12 leavers) seeking rapid technical or business qualifications.
  • NCV (National Certificate Vocational) is a 3-year full-time curriculum (Levels 2, 3, and 4) that combines academic schooling with vocational training for Grade 9 school leavers, equivalent to a technical matric.
Can I apply for remarking if my NATED examination mark is between 37% and 39%?

Yes. DHET provides an official remarking and re-checking application window following the release of national TVET examination results. Candidates can lodge a formal remarking request through their TVET college examination office upon payment of the gazetted administrative fee.

How are final marks rounded off in NATED results?

DHET examination computing systems round calculations to the nearest whole percentage point at the conclusion of composite calculations (e.g., 39.5% rounds up to 40.0%, while 39.4% remains 39.0%).
