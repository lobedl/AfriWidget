How the NATED pass calculation works

This calculator is for a common South African TVET NATED planning formula. It combines the internal ICASS mark, weighted at 40%, with the final examination mark, weighted at 60%. The calculation is: final mark equals ICASS multiplied by 0.40 plus examination mark multiplied by 0.60. Because the examination carries the larger weight, improving the exam mark can have a greater effect on the final total than an equal improvement in the internal mark.

A result is marked PASS only when two conditions are both satisfied. First, the weighted total must be at least 40%. Second, the final examination mark itself must be at least 40%. The second condition is the sub-minimum check. For example, an ICASS mark of 80% and an examination mark of 30% produces a weighted total of 50%, but it does not pass this rule because the exam mark is below 40%. The card makes that distinction visible instead of looking only at the total.

Worked examples

If ICASS is 55% and the examination is 48%, the weighted total is 22.0 plus 28.8, or 50.8%. The examination is above the 40% sub-minimum, so the supplied rule classifies the result as a pass. If ICASS is 70% and the examination is 35%, the weighted total is 49.0%, but the result remains below the sub-minimum requirement. These examples show why both inputs matter.

Use the result carefully

Enter the marks exactly as they appear on your statement or institutional record. Do not enter a fraction as a decimal unless your school presents it that way; use percentages such as 42.5. If a mark is missing, wait until the official record is available rather than treating a blank as zero. The tool performs no server upload and does not store your marks.

NATED programmes, subjects, examination sessions, and institutional policies can have additional rules. A pass result here is a calculation under the supplied weighting and pass criteria, not an official certificate, promotion decision, or transcript. Check the current Department of Higher Education and Training guidance, your TVET college instructions, and the rules for the specific N-level or subject.

Frequently asked questions

Why can a total above 40% still fail?

The supplied rule requires the examination mark itself to be at least 40%. A strong ICASS mark cannot compensate for an exam mark below the sub-minimum.

Is ICASS always exactly 40%?

This page uses the 40% ICASS and 60% examination weighting specified in the brief. Confirm the current rule for your subject and institution.

Does AfriWidget issue an official result?

No. It is a private browser calculator for planning. Your college, examination authority, or official statement remains authoritative.

Plan improvement from the formula

The weighting can help you set a realistic target. A ten-point improvement in the examination mark changes the final total by six points, while a ten-point improvement in ICASS changes it by four points. That does not replace study advice, but it explains why examination preparation matters in this particular calculation. Use the result to discuss revision, supplementary assessments, or support with your lecturer.

Enter one scenario at a time and write down the result before changing a mark. This makes it easier to compare possible outcomes without confusing a projected score with an official record. Always check the current college and examination rules for your subject, level, and session.

Before asking for help

Have your statement, subject name, assessment session, and both component marks ready when speaking with a lecturer or college office. Ask whether the pass rule applies to your specific subject and whether any moderation or supplementary assessment changes the calculation. Keep these records together for clarity.

A useful result-review checklist

Record the ICASS mark, examination mark, calculated total, and date of the calculation. If the result is below the pass rule, identify whether the overall total or the exam sub-minimum is the issue. Then ask the college which supplementary, rewrite, moderation, or progression rules apply to the exact subject and N-level.

Use the calculator for planning a target mark, but keep the official statement as the authoritative record. A pass calculation does not replace an institutional decision, and a result may be affected by rules not included in this simplified formula.
