Comprehensive Guide to the Swiss Academic Grading System & Grade Conversions

Navigating the Swiss grading system requires understanding one of the most distinctive academic assessment structures in Europe. Unlike systems in Germany, Austria, or the United States where lower numerical figures or letter grades represent top performance, the Swiss grading scale operates on a 1.0 to 6.0 system where 6.0 is the highest possible grade and 4.0 represents the minimum passing threshold (genügend / suffisant / sufficiente).

Whether you are completing the Gymnasiale Matura (Maturité gymnasiale / Maturità), preparing for first-year Block Examinations (Basisprüfung) at ETH Zurich or EPFL, studying at a cantonal university (such as the University of Zurich, University of Geneva, or University of Basel), or applying for master's programs abroad, converting your Swiss marks into ECTS letter grades, US 4.0 GPA, German grades, or UK degree classifications is essential.

This guide provides an exhaustive breakdown of how Swiss grades are calculated, how credit-weighted semester averages (ECTS) operate, how the Swiss Matura pass condition functions, and how international conversion formulas work.

The Swiss 1.0 to 6.0 Grading Scale Structure

In Switzerland, grades are awarded in numerical values ranging from 1.0 (lowest) to 6.0 (highest), usually in half-grade (0.5) or quarter-grade (0.25) intervals depending on the institution.

  • Grade 6.0 (Ausgezeichnet / Excellent / Eccellente): Outstanding performance demonstrating complete mastery of the subject matter with exceptional depth and analytical precision. Corresponds to 95% to 100% and an ECTS grade of A (Top 10%).
  • Grade 5.5 (Sehr gut / Très bien / Molto buono): Very good performance significantly above the average standard. Corresponds to approximately 85% to 94% and an ECTS grade of A/B.
  • Grade 5.0 (Gut / Bien / Buono): Good, solid performance meeting all major curriculum competencies with minor shortcomings. Corresponds to approximately 75% to 84% and an ECTS grade of B/C.
  • Grade 4.5 (Befriedigend / Satisfaisant / Soddisfacente): Satisfactory performance exceeding the minimum baseline. Corresponds to approximately 65% to 74% and an ECTS grade of C/D.
  • Grade 4.0 (Genügend / Suffisant / Sufficiente): Pass threshold. Indicates that the performance satisfies the minimum official requirements. Corresponds to approximately 55% to 64% and an ECTS grade of E.
  • Grade 3.5 (Ungenügend / Insuffisant / Insufficiente): Unsatisfactory / Fail. The student has failed to meet the required learning outcomes. In modular block exams, this mark can sometimes be compensated by higher grades in other subjects.
  • Grade 3.0 (Schlecht / Mauvais / Scarso): Poor performance with severe knowledge deficiencies.
  • Grade 2.0 (Sehr schlecht / Très mauvais / Molto scarso): Very poor performance with negligible understanding.
  • Grade 1.0 (Völlig unbrauchbar / Nul / Nullo): Completely inadequate or unattempted examination.

Official Calculation Methodology & Conversion Formulas

1. ECTS Credit-Weighted Grade Point Average (GPA)

At Swiss universities and Federal Institutes of Technology (ETH Zurich, EPFL), modular grades are weighted by their assigned European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System (ECTS) credits:

  • Weighted Swiss Grade = Sum of (Course Grade × ECTS Credits) ÷ Total ECTS Credits Attempted

For example, completing an 8 ECTS Linear Algebra course with a grade of 5.50 and a 4 ECTS Computer Science seminar with a grade of 4.50 yields:

  • Total Quality Points = (5.50 × 8) + (4.50 × 4) = 44.0 + 18.0 = 62.0
  • Weighted Average = 62.0 ÷ 12 = 5.17

2. German Grading System Conversion (Modified Bavarian Formula)

German universities utilize a 1.0 to 5.0 scale where 1.0 is the highest grade and 4.0 is the minimum pass. To convert a Swiss passing grade (4.0 to 6.0) into the German system, the Modified Bavarian Formula is applied:

  • German Grade = 1 + 3 × [(Maximum Swiss Grade - Student Swiss Grade) ÷ (Maximum Swiss Grade - Minimum Passing Swiss Grade)]
  • Simplified Formula = 1 + 3 × [(6.0 - Student Swiss Grade) ÷ (6.0 - 4.0)] = 1 + 1.5 × (6.0 - Student Swiss Grade)

Under this formula:

  • Swiss 6.0 converts to German 1.0 (Sehr gut / With distinction)
  • Swiss 5.5 converts to German 1.75 (Sehr gut / Gut)
  • Swiss 5.0 converts to German 2.50 (Gut)
  • Swiss 4.5 converts to German 3.25 (Befriedigend)
  • Swiss 4.0 converts to German 4.00 (Ausreichend / Minimum Pass)

3. United States 4.0 GPA Standard

Converting Swiss marks to a US 4.0 Grade Point Average requires mapping through linear percentage equivalence and international credential evaluation guidelines (e.g., WES and AACRAO):

  • Swiss 5.75 to 6.00: 4.00 US GPA (Grade A+)
  • Swiss 5.25 to 5.50: 3.70 to 3.90 US GPA (Grade A / A-)
  • Swiss 4.75 to 5.00: 3.30 to 3.50 US GPA (Grade B+ / B)
  • Swiss 4.25 to 4.50: 2.70 to 3.00 US GPA (Grade B- / C+)
  • Swiss 4.00: 2.00 US GPA (Grade C / Minimum passing standard)
  • Below 4.00: 0.00 US GPA (Grade F / Failing grade)

4. French Grading System Conversion (0 to 20 Scale)

In the French higher education and Baccalauréat system, 20 is maximum and 10 is the passing mark. For passing grades in Switzerland:

  • French Grade = 10 + 10 × [(Student Swiss Grade - 4.0) ÷ 2.0]

Under this linear progression:

  • Swiss 6.0 = 20.0 / 20 (Très bien avec félicitations)
  • Swiss 5.0 = 15.0 / 20 (Bien)
  • Swiss 4.5 = 12.5 / 20 (Assez bien)
  • Swiss 4.0 = 10.0 / 20 (Passable)

5. Swiss Gymnasiale Matura Pass Criteria (Maturitätsausweis)

To be awarded the Swiss Federal Matura certificate, candidates must fulfill two strict legal criteria across their examined subjects:

  • Criterion 1: The overall arithmetic average of all Matura grades must be at least 4.00.
  • Criterion 2 (Double-Deficit Rule): The sum of all grade points below 4.0 multiplied by 2 must not exceed the sum of all grade points above 4.0. In other words, positive compensation points must be at least twice as large as the total deficit points: Sum of (Grade - 4.0) for passing subjects >= 2 × Sum of (4.0 - Grade) for failing subjects.

Worked Calculation Examples

Worked Example 1: ETH Zurich Mechanical Engineering First-Year Block Exam (Basisprüfung)

An undergraduate student at ETH Zurich completes their first-year Block 1 examination consisting of four mandatory subjects:

  • Analysis I & II: 8 ECTS Credits, Grade = 4.75. Quality points = 4.75 × 8 = 38.0
  • Physics I: 6 ECTS Credits, Grade = 5.25. Quality points = 5.25 × 6 = 31.5
  • Linear Algebra: 4 ECTS Credits, Grade = 3.75 (Compensable failing grade). Quality points = 3.75 × 4 = 15.0
  • Mechanics I: 6 ECTS Credits, Grade = 5.00. Quality points = 5.00 × 6 = 30.0

Calculation Steps:

  • Total ECTS Credits Attempted: 8 + 6 + 4 + 6 = 24 ECTS
  • Total Quality Points: 38.0 + 31.5 + 15.0 + 30.0 = 114.5 Quality Points
  • Weighted Swiss Grade Average: 114.5 ÷ 24 = 4.77 / 6.00
  • Block Exam Outcome: PASSED. Even though Linear Algebra was below 4.0 (3.75), ETH Zurich examination regulations allow compensation because the overall weighted average of the block is 4.77 (>= 4.00).
  • Equivalent US GPA: 3.40 / 4.00 (Grade B+)
  • Equivalent German Grade: 1 + 1.5 × (6.0 - 4.77) = 2.85 (Befriedigend / Gut)
  • Equivalent ECTS Grade: C (Good)

Worked Example 2: Swiss Gymnasiale Matura Candidate (Maturité Examination Audit)

A Swiss Gymnasium student in Canton Zurich receives their final Matura subject marks across 8 key examination subjects:

  • German (First National Language): Grade = 5.0 (+1.0 above pass)
  • French (Second National Language): Grade = 4.5 (+0.5 above pass)
  • English (Third Language): Grade = 5.5 (+1.5 above pass)
  • Mathematics (Major Subject): Grade = 3.5 (-0.5 deficit)
  • Biology & Chemistry: Grade = 4.0 (0.0 deviation)
  • Physics: Grade = 3.5 (-0.5 deficit)
  • History & Civics: Grade = 5.0 (+1.0 above pass)
  • Visual Arts / Music: Grade = 5.5 (+1.5 above pass)

Pass Condition Audit:

  • Sum of All Grades: 5.0 + 4.5 + 5.5 + 3.5 + 4.0 + 3.5 + 5.0 + 5.5 = 36.5
  • Overall Arithmetic Average: 36.5 ÷ 8 = 4.56 / 6.00 (Fulfills Criterion 1: >= 4.00)
  • Total Positive Compensation Points: (+1.0) + (+0.5) + (+1.5) + (+1.0) + (+1.5) = +5.5 points
  • Total Negative Deficit Points: (4.0 - 3.5) + (4.0 - 3.5) = 1.0 point
  • Double-Deficit Test: 2 × Total Deficit = 2 × 1.0 = 2.0 points. Since +5.5 points >= 2.0 points, the student easily satisfies Criterion 2.
  • Verdict: Matura Successfully Awarded (Maturitätszeugnis erteilt) with an honors-level average of 4.56.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is considered a good grade at a Swiss university?

At Swiss universities like ETH Zurich, EPFL, University of Zurich (UZH), and University of Geneva, grading is rigorous and grade inflation is virtually non-existent. A grade of 5.0 is considered solidly good, 5.5 is very good (often top 10% to 15% of the cohort), and grades between 5.75 and 6.0 are reserved for exceptional, top-tier performance. A cumulative average of 5.0+ places students in strong standing for competitive master's and doctoral admissions.

What is the minimum passing grade in Switzerland?

The minimum passing grade across all Swiss educational tiers—from primary and secondary school through the Gymnasiale Matura, vocational apprenticeships (EFZ), and universities—is strictly 4.00. Any mark below 4.0 (such as 3.5, 3.0, or 2.0) is classified as unsatisfactory (ungenügend / insuffisant).

How does the block compensation system (Basisprüfung) work at ETH Zurich and EPFL?

At ETH Zurich and EPFL, first-year subjects are grouped into exam blocks (Basisprüfung). Students do not need to pass every single individual subject with a 4.0. Instead, as long as the credit-weighted average of the entire block equals or exceeds 4.00, the student passes the block and receives full ECTS credits for all courses in that block, even if one course received a 3.5.

How do Swiss grades convert to the ECTS grading scale?

The European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System (ECTS) uses a relative scale based on statistical cohort ranking:

  • ECTS Grade A (Excellent): Top 10% of successful students (typically Swiss 5.75 to 6.0)
  • ECTS Grade B (Very Good): Next 25% of successful students (typically Swiss 5.25 to 5.5)
  • ECTS Grade C (Good): Next 30% of successful students (typically Swiss 4.75 to 5.0)
  • ECTS Grade D (Satisfactory): Next 25% of successful students (typically Swiss 4.25 to 4.5)
  • ECTS Grade E (Sufficient): Final 10% of successful students who pass (Swiss 4.0)
  • ECTS Grade FX / F (Fail): Below 4.0 (requires re-examination or retake)
Why is 6.0 the maximum grade in Switzerland instead of 1.0?

The Swiss grading tradition dates back to 19th-century cantonal school legislation where numerical points represented earned performance points on a 1 to 6 scale (where 6 points equaled complete fulfillment). Unlike neighboring Germany, which adopted an ordinal ranking system where 1 represents first rank, Switzerland preserved the ascending point system.

Any mark below 4.0 (such as 3.5, 3.0, or 2.0) is classified as unsatisfactory (ungenügend / insuffisant).

How does the block compensation system (Basisprüfung) work at ETH Zurich and EPFL?

At ETH Zurich and EPFL, first-year subjects are grouped into exam blocks (Basisprüfung). Students do not need to pass every single individual subject with a 4.0. Instead, as long as the credit-weighted average of the entire block equals or exceeds 4.00, the student passes the block and receives full ECTS credits for all courses in that block, even if one course received a 3.5.

How do Swiss grades convert to the ECTS grading scale?

The European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System (ECTS) uses a relative scale based on statistical cohort ranking:

  • ECTS Grade A (Excellent): Top 10% of successful students (typically Swiss 5.75 to 6.0)
  • ECTS Grade B (Very Good): Next 25% of successful students (typically Swiss 5.25 to 5.5)
  • ECTS Grade C (Good): Next 30% of successful students (typically Swiss 4.75 to 5.0)
  • ECTS Grade D (Satisfactory): Next 25% of successful students (typically Swiss 4.25 to 4.5)
  • ECTS Grade E (Sufficient): Final 10% of successful students who pass (Swiss 4.0)
  • ECTS Grade FX / F (Fail): Below 4.0 (requires re-examination or retake)

The Swiss grading tradition dates back to 19th-century cantonal school legislation where numerical points represented earned performance points on a 1 to 6 scale (where 6 points equaled complete fulfillment). Unlike neighboring Germany, which adopted an ordinal ranking system where 1 represents first rank, Switzerland preserved the ascending point system.
