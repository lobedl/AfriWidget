Complete Guide to Zambia TEVETA Assessment, Grading Scales & Certification

Prepared by the AfriWidget Vocational Standards Panel in alignment with the Technical Education, Vocational and Entrepreneurship Training Authority (TEVETA) of Zambia.

1. Understanding the TEVETA Qualifications Framework (TQF)

In Zambia, technical education, trade vocations, and applied engineering diplomas are governed by the Technical Education, Vocational and Entrepreneurship Training Authority (TEVETA), established under the TEVET Act No. 13 of 1998 (amended by Act No. 11 of 2005). TEVETA standardizes curriculum design, continuous assessments, institutional accreditation, and national trade examinations across all technical colleges and vocational training institutes.

TEVETA qualifications sit within the Zambia Qualifications Framework (ZAQF), providing structured career ladders from entry-level trade competence to university degree bridging:

TEVETA Qualification Hierarchy & ZAQF Alignment

Level 6: TEVETA Diploma ZAQF Level 6

3-year comprehensive technical qualification (e.g., Electrical Engineering, Mechanical Technology, Information Technology, Journalism). Eligible for direct 2nd or 3rd year entry into bachelor's degree programs at UNZA and CBU.

Level 5: Technician / Advanced Certificate ZAQF Level 5

2-year supervisory technical programs focused on advanced trade management, specialized laboratory techniques, and operational oversight.

Level 4: Craft Certificate ZAQF Level 4

2-year practical trade qualification (e.g., Automotive Mechanics, Heavy Equipment Repair, Plumbing, Bricklaying, Electrical Installation).

Level 3: Trade Test Certificate / Skills Award ZAQF Level 3

6 to 12 months competency-based skills assessment validating hands-on workshop mastery and industrial readiness.

2. Official TEVETA Grading Scale & Competence Benchmarks

Unlike university letter scales (A to F) or secondary school numerical ranks (1 to 9), TEVETA evaluates students based on Competence-Based Education and Training (CBET) principles. Every candidate must demonstrate mastery across both theoretical knowledge and practical trade proficiency.

Distinction (80% – 100%)

High level of competence. Demonstrates exceptional problem-solving, mastery of workshop tools, and flawless theoretical understanding.

Distinction

Credit (65% – 79%)

Sound technical competence. Capable of performing trade tasks independently to industrial safety and engineering standards.

Credit

Pass (50% – 64%)

Meets basic vocational benchmark. Satisfies minimum industrial competence requirements for certification.

Pass

Refer / Supplementary Re-Assessment (40% – 49%)

Conditional failure in a specific unit or practical workshop. The student is permitted to sit a supplementary examination in that module without repeating the entire academic year.

Refer

Fail (0% – 39%)

Incompetent in trade requirements. Candidate must re-register and repeat the coursework, practical logs, and final examination.

3. How the TEVETA Composite Score Is Calculated

A student's recorded grade in any TEVETA module is a weighted composite of internal institutional evaluation and national external examinations:

Step 1: Continuous Assessment (CA) Calculation (Typically 40%)

Your college calculates your CA score by compiling mid-term tests, practical workshop projects, laboratory experiment logs, and classroom assignments. If your CA is 75 out of 100 under a 40% model:
CA Contribution = 75 × 0.40 = 30.0 Marks

Step 2: National Examination Calculation (Typically 60%)

At the end of the term or academic year, you sit the standardized national TEVETA examination paper. If you score 80 out of 100 under a 60% weighting:
Exam Contribution = 80 × 0.60 = 48.0 Marks

Step 3: Summing the Final Composite Mark

Add the two weighted components together to determine your overall module percentage:
Final Score = CA Contribution + Exam Contribution = 30.0 + 48.0 = 78.0% (Credit Standing)

4. Worked Practical Examples

Example 1: Diploma in Electrical Engineering at NORTEC (Chanda)

Module: Electrical Machines & Drives (40% CA + 60% Final Exam)

Continuous Assessment (Workshop + Tests):Score 85% (85 × 0.40 = 34.0 pts)
National TEVETA Written Exam:Score 82% (82 × 0.60 = 49.2 pts)

Final Module Mark = 34.0 + 49.2 = 83.2%

Classification Verdict = Distinction (High Competence)

Standing = Full unit pass awarded towards TEVETA Level 6 Diploma.

Example 2: Craft Certificate in Automotive Mechanics (Bwalya)

Module: Engine Overhaul & Diagnostics (50% CA + 50% Practical Assessment)

Workshop Continuous Practical Log:Score 68% (68 × 0.50 = 34.0 pts)
TEVETA External Trade Test Panel Exam:Score 72% (72 × 0.50 = 36.0 pts)

Final Module Mark = 34.0 + 36.0 = 70.0%

Classification Verdict = Credit (Sound Competence)

Standing = Satisfies trade certification requirements under ZAQF Level 4.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the minimum passing mark in TEVETA examinations? ▼

The minimum passing mark for any module or trade unit under TEVETA is 50%. A score between 40% and 49% results in a "Refer" status (eligible for supplementary re-assessment), while any score below 40% is classified as a clear fail.

Can I bridge from a TEVETA Level 6 Diploma to a university degree? ▼

Yes. Holders of an accredited TEVETA Level 6 Diploma with a Distinction or Credit standing are eligible for advanced diploma entry or credit exemptions into bachelor's degree programs at major Zambian universities, including the University of Zambia (UNZA), Copperbelt University (CBU), and Mulungushi University.

What happens if I fail the practical workshop component of a TEVETA course? ▼

Because TEVETA operates under Competence-Based Education and Training (CBET), passing the practical workshop component is mandatory. Even if your written examination score is high, failure to achieve competence in practical assessments may prevent certification until the workshop competency is demonstrated and re-assessed.

How many times am I allowed to resit a referred TEVETA module? ▼

Candidates referred in one or two modules are typically granted up to two consecutive supplementary assessment sittings. If a candidate fails to clear the referral within the stipulated examination windows, they must re-register for the entire module and repeat continuous assessment.
