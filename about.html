About AfriWidget

Built in Ghana. Built Around Real Problems.

AfriWidget started with a simple question: why should calculating a student's aggregate, checking a GPA, or working out a loan payment be harder than it needs to be?

What Is AfriWidget?

AfriWidget is a collection of free online tools that run directly inside your web browser. You don't have to download an app, create an account, or hand over your personal details to use any of it. You open the website, pick the tool you need, type in your numbers, and see your answer right there on your screen.

I built AfriWidget because I kept seeing simple problems turned into complicated online experiences. A student needs to calculate an aggregate. A freelancer needs an invoice. Someone wants to estimate a loan payment. These shouldn't require an account, an app download, or a subscription.

Today, AfriWidget has more than 120 tools divided across five areas: education, finance, health, productivity, and business. Every tool is built to be fast, private, and straightforward.

The idea is simple: find the tool, enter the numbers, get the result, and move on.

The Story Behind AfriWidget

Every website starts somewhere. Mine started with a real headache right here in Ghana.

Back in 2024, my younger cousin came to my room in Accra holding his WASSCE result slip. He wanted to know what his grades added up to and whether they met the cut-off points for university admission. The calculation itself was simple arithmetic. But finding a straightforward way to do it was an entirely different story.

At the time, students around us were walking to cyber cafes or asking middlemen to help add up their grades, often paying money just for someone to look at the slip and write down an aggregate on scrap paper. Why should a student have to spend money or travel somewhere just to do a basic addition that could be done on a phone?

I had studied business at Keta Business College, but I was spending a lot of my free time teaching myself web development and JavaScript.

Learning to Build

I built AfriWidget without a team of developers behind me. I was learning web development on my own, working with an old laptop and whatever internet access I could afford at the time. I learned by building, breaking things, reading documentation, fixing mistakes, and starting again.

I opened my code editor, wrote a simple script following official scoring criteria, and sent the link to my cousin on WhatsApp so he could try it on his phone.

Then he shared it with his classmates. Within days, students from other schools were using it. After that, a junior high school teacher messaged me about BECE calculations. University students asked for semester GPA and CGPA tools. Freelancers asked for simple invoice generators. Shopkeepers asked for margin tools.

Instead of building dozens of scattered little websites, I brought them together under one roof and named it AfriWidget.

Your Calculations Stay in Your Browser

When you enter your grades, salary, loan amount, weight, or other numbers into an AfriWidget calculator, the calculation is performed locally in your browser.

Your calculator inputs are not sent to our server for the calculation, and AfriWidget does not store those inputs.

You don't need an account to use the calculators, and you don't need to submit personal information just to get a result.

Your numbers are yours. The calculator is simply there to do the math.

Bottom line: Your numbers stay on your device. Always.

Why Many AfriWidget Tools Start With African Education

AfriWidget was started in Ghana, so some of the first problems I wanted to solve were problems I could see around me.

That's why you'll find tools for systems such as WASSCE, BECE, JAMB, KCSE, KUCCPS, South African APS, and other education systems.

These aren't generic calculators with a different title. They exist because students actually need to work with these grading and admission systems.

But AfriWidget isn't limited to African users. Many of the finance, health, business, productivity, and everyday tools can be useful regardless of where someone lives.

Ghana is where I started building it. It doesn't have to be where the usefulness ends.

The Tools I Build

I organize the platform into five main categories, covering the calculations people face most often.

Education & Academic Tools

This section covers the calculations students, applicants, and parents regularly need for school grading and tertiary admissions: WASSCE and BECE aggregates, KCSE and KUCCPS cluster points, JAMB UTME score weighting, South African APS scores, and semester GPA/CGPA conversions. The aim isn't to replace official university admission boards or examination councils. It's to make grading formulas clear and easy to calculate on your own phone without having to pay someone at a cyber cafe.

Finance & Money Tools

This section covers the calculations people often need before making a financial decision: loan repayments, compound interest, savings growth, markups, and other everyday money calculations. The aim isn't to tell you what financial decision to make. It's to give you the numbers you need to understand the decision.

Health & Wellness Tools

This section covers everyday body and wellness calculations: Body Mass Index (BMI), Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR), Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE), target calorie estimates, and daily water intake needs. The aim isn't to diagnose health conditions or prescribe diets. It's to provide straightforward mathematical estimates based on established formulas, helping you understand your baseline body metrics.

Productivity & Everyday Utilities

This section covers the practical digital tasks that take up time during a workday: converting case and formatting text, cleaning up messy lists, generating downloadable QR codes, estimating reading and speech times, and converting units. The aim isn't to offer a complicated productivity suite. It's to give you single-purpose tools that solve immediate small tasks cleanly without ads or paywalls.

Business & Freelance Tools

This section covers the operational math small business owners, market vendors, and independent freelancers face every week: generating clean PDF invoices and receipts, calculating profit margins and markups, figuring out break-even sales volume, comparing payment processor fees (PayPal, Stripe, Square), and setting sustainable freelance hourly rates. The aim isn't to force you into expensive accounting software. It's to give you quick, accurate business numbers so you know your actual margins and net payouts.

How I Build and Maintain the Tools

Every tool on this site follows a straightforward process.

Step 1: Start with a Real Need

I build tools when people encounter a problem they can't easily solve online. When students, teachers, freelancers, or small business owners tell me about a calculation that's causing trouble, that's when I start coding.

Step 2: Research the Official Rules

Before writing the JavaScript, I look up official documentation. For academic tools, that means reading the exam board's published grading criteria. For finance tools, that means checking standard banking and interest formulas. If official guidelines vary or leave room for interpretation, I explain that right on the page.

Step 3: Keep the Code Simple

I write clean, plain HTML, CSS, and lightweight JavaScript. No massive frameworks, heavy dependencies, or background scripts that slow down your browser or drain your battery.

Step 4: Test with Known Examples

I test the calculations against known examples and relevant published requirements before publishing a tool. When rules change, I update the tool where necessary.

Step 5: Listen, Fix, and Update

Once a tool is live, the work continues. When users message me about an edge case or when an exam board updates their grading scale, I update the code as quickly as I can.

What AfriWidget Is Not

I like being upfront about what this site is and what it isn't.

This is not a substitute for professional advice. If you have a serious medical symptom, please see a doctor. If you're signing a major mortgage or loan contract, consult a certified financial advisor. AfriWidget gives you fast, reliable estimates, but it doesn't replace professional human judgment.

I am not a data broker. I don't harvest your numbers, I don't sell user lists, and I don't run tracking schemes. My only goal is to provide useful tools that work.

I am not building a venture-backed startup. I don't take outside capital, and I don't lock basic features behind paid tiers. I just want to build reliable tools that help people get work done.
