AfriWidget was built on a simple idea: finding a useful tool should not be harder than using it.

When people need to calculate examination grades, check university admission requirements, understand their academic performance, or plan their next steps, they should not have to jump between multiple websites, navigate unnecessary pages, or pay for basic functionality. They should be able to find the right tool, get a useful result, and understand what that result means.

That is why AfriWidget exists.

Founded on 3 August 2026 by Obed Avorlenu, a self-taught web developer from Ghana, AfriWidget brings practical digital tools together in one accessible platform. Education is its primary focus, with additional tools for finance, business, and productivity.

But the purpose goes beyond putting calculators on a website.

AfriWidget is being built to help people understand their results, identify opportunities for improvement, compare possibilities, track progress, and make more informed decisions. The goal is to combine convenience with useful guidance, giving people more value than a number on a screen.

What Is AfriWidget?

AfriWidget is a free, browser-based platform offering educational calculators, academic planning tools, and practical utilities for everyday life.

Instead of visiting several different websites to complete related tasks, users can find a growing collection of tools in one place. The platform is designed to make useful technology easier to access, particularly for people who want straightforward solutions without unnecessary barriers.

Education is at the heart of AfriWidget. Its academic tools help students work through examination calculations, estimate admission scores, evaluate eligibility, and understand their academic standing. Selected tools also provide additional features that help learners assess their knowledge, identify weaknesses, compare results, and monitor progress.

Beyond education, AfriWidget offers tools for financial planning, business calculations, and everyday productivity. These categories support the broader purpose of making practical digital solutions accessible without requiring separate applications for every small task.

The platform is designed around three important principles:

Accessibility

Find and use practical tools without unnecessary registration, downloads, or paywalls. Open on any modern phone or browser instantly.

Privacy

Keep supported calculations and personal records on the user's device wherever the relevant features are implemented locally.

Practical Value

Make results easier to understand and use through relevant explanations, tracking, comparisons, and helpful features.

AfriWidget is not intended to replace every specialist application or professional service. Its purpose is to make common tasks easier by providing useful tools in a single, convenient place.

Why AfriWidget Was Created

The Problem Was Never a Lack of Websites. It Was the Effort Required to Get Things Done.

The internet has made information widely available, but finding a practical solution can still involve more work than necessary.

Imagine a student trying to calculate a WASSCE aggregate, check the admission requirements for a university programme, and determine whether their results meet a target. They may have to visit several websites, interpret different explanations, perform calculations manually, and search again whenever they need another answer.

The same inconvenience appears in other areas of life.

A freelancer might need one website to calculate a price, another to estimate transaction fees, and another to prepare an invoice. Someone comparing loan options may need to open several calculators before understanding how different repayment periods affect the total cost.

Each website might solve one problem. The difficulty comes from having to move between them, learn different interfaces, and repeat the process for related tasks.

AfriWidget was created to reduce that unnecessary effort.

Its founder, Obed Avorlenu, wanted to use his interest in web development to solve practical problems by making useful tools easier to find and use. Rather than building a website around information alone, he set out to create a platform where people could perform calculations and complete useful tasks directly.

The idea was not simply to increase the number of available calculators. It was to make access to those calculators more convenient and to build features that help people get more out of their results.

From Finding an Answer to Knowing What to Do Next

Getting a result does not always resolve the problem that brought someone to a calculator.

A student might calculate an aggregate but still need to understand their admission options. Someone checking their GPA may want to know how their performance is changing. A learner preparing for an examination may need to identify topics that require more practice rather than simply seeing another score.

This distinction matters.

A calculator can tell someone where they stand mathematically, but a useful tool can also help them understand the implications of that result.

AfriWidget therefore aims to go beyond isolated calculations wherever practical. Its growing set of features includes academic progress tracking, weakness assessment on supported educational tools, result comparison, saved favourites, and optional AI-powered insights.

These features address different needs, but they share one purpose: helping users turn information into something they can actually use.

For students, that may mean identifying an area to revise. For someone planning their finances, it may mean understanding how changing one figure affects a result. For a regular visitor, it may simply mean returning to a familiar tool without starting again.

The underlying idea remains the same: technology should make the next step easier, not leave people with another unanswered question.

Why Education Comes First

Education is AfriWidget's primary focus because academic calculations and planning can have a direct influence on a student's next decision.

Examination results, grading systems, admission formulas, and programme requirements are not always easy to navigate. Rules differ between countries, institutions, qualifications, and academic years. Even when students can find the relevant information, applying it correctly may require several calculations.

For students who rely on their phones to research opportunities and plan their education, a straightforward tool can make that process more manageable.

AfriWidget aims to make these calculations easier to access and understand. Its educational tools cover several academic systems, including:

  • Ghana and West Africa: WASSCE and BECE aggregate calculations and related academic utilities.
  • Nigeria: JAMB UTME aggregate calculations and selected admission-scoring tools.
  • Kenya: KCSE grade calculations and KUCCPS-related points estimation.
  • South Africa: Matric Admission Point Score (APS) calculators and TVET and NATED academic tools.
  • Uganda and Tanzania: Selected examination-point and admission-related calculators (UCE, UACE, UNEB, Form Four, ACSEE).
  • Higher education: GPA, CGPA, academic target calculations, and other student-focused utilities.

The purpose is to help learners calculate their results, evaluate possible outcomes, and understand the criteria relevant to their academic goals.

Important note on official criteria: Where a tool uses a particular grading formula or admission requirement, the applicable rules must be checked carefully. Requirements may change or vary between institutions, and an estimated result does not guarantee admission. AfriWidget aims to make the calculation easier. Official institutions remain the authority on their own admission decisions.

Helping Students Move Beyond the Score

Academic success involves more than obtaining a number.

Students need ways to evaluate their performance, understand what they should improve, and plan for future assessments. That is why selected AfriWidget educational tools include additional capabilities beyond basic calculation.

The platform's academic progress features help users review performance over time. Its weakness-assessment features can help learners investigate areas where they need further practice. Its comparison feature gives users another way to examine results alongside a friend.

Together, these capabilities support a more active approach to learning.

Instead of treating every calculation as a final destination, AfriWidget aims to make it part of a wider process of understanding, preparation, and improvement.

Its approach is to bring useful tools together, make them straightforward to access, and develop features that help people do more with the results.

Useful Tools in One Place

Fast, Straightforward Access

Local processing and storage can reduce the need to send personal inputs to a remote server. However, not every feature necessarily works in the same way. Optional AI services and other external integrations may process information differently, so their data handling should be explained separately.

AfriWidget's objective is to minimise unnecessary data collection and make privacy-conscious access a central part of the experience.

4

Helpful Context Alongside Calculations

A result is more useful when people understand what it represents.

On supported tools, AfriWidget provides explanations, practical breakdowns, or optional AI-powered insights that help users interpret their results.

For students, this may involve understanding a calculated score or considering an academic target. For financial tools, it may involve examining how different assumptions influence a repayment or savings estimate.

The goal is to make results easier to interpret without suggesting that an estimate or AI-generated explanation is infallible.

5

Features That Support Continued Progress

AfriWidget includes features that help users return to their work, keep relevant records, compare outcomes, and investigate areas for improvement.

This means the platform can support more than a one-time calculation. Where the relevant features are available, users can continue exploring their options without having to reconstruct the entire process from the beginning.

6

Designed Around Practical Needs

AfriWidget is developed using lightweight web technologies, including HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, with an emphasis on straightforward interfaces and mobile accessibility.

The aim is to make the platform usable on different devices, including smartphones, without requiring visitors to learn complicated software.

That practical standard guides the development of the platform.

Features That Make AfriWidget More Useful

AfriWidget continues to develop capabilities that help users work with their results rather than simply view them.

The following features illustrate how the platform supports different stages of a task. Availability depends on the particular tool and implementation.

Find My Weaknesses

For students, knowing a score does not necessarily reveal what they need to study next.

Find My Weaknesses is designed to help learners investigate gaps in their understanding through relevant questions and feedback on supported educational tools. Users can work through questions, evaluate their answers, and identify topics or concepts that deserve further practice.

The purpose is to make revision more focused. The feature is intended to support learning, not to label students or replace a teacher's assessment.

Compare Results with a Friend

Students often work towards similar academic goals but may have different results, targets, or areas of difficulty.

AfriWidget's comparison feature allows users to compare supported results with a friend and examine the differences side by side. This makes academic discussions more concrete, helping users understand how different scores affect the outcome.

Comparison is a way to learn from differences, not a measure of a person's overall ability or future potential.

Academic Progress Tracking

Academic progress is easier to evaluate when results can be viewed over time.

On supported tools, users can record relevant academic results and review changes across terms or assessment periods. This helps learners monitor GPA or CGPA, evaluate progress towards a target, and consider whether current performance aligns with their plans.

100% In-Browser Privacy

Calculations and evaluations are processed directly inside your device's browser, eliminating external server dependencies.

Your sensitive grades, target scores, and personal figures stay on your device without remote telemetry or persistent server tracking.

Saved Favourites

Users can save frequently used tools so they are easier to find on subsequent visits.

This is especially useful for students who regularly check academic results or visitors who rely on particular financial, business, or productivity utilities.

Continue Where You Left Off

Supported tools can preserve previous inputs in the browser, allowing users to return to a task without re-entering every figure.

This is useful when comparing scenarios, adjusting targets, or working through calculations that require several attempts. Local storage may be affected by browser settings or clearing data, so it should not be treated as a permanent backup.

Optional AI-Powered Insights

On relevant tools, optional AI-powered insights can provide additional explanations and practical context around a result.

The feature is designed to help users interpret outputs and consider possible next steps without treating a generated response as definitive authority. Consequential decisions should always be checked against authoritative sources.

Sharing and Reusing Results

Where supported, AfriWidget allows users to share or copy relevant results and links, making it easier to discuss outcomes with friends or return to a particular tool.

These capabilities complement the platform's broader focus on making useful calculations easier to access and work with.

How AfriWidget Is Built

Behind a useful tool is a practical question: does it solve the problem it was designed for?

AfriWidget's development approach begins with identifying a task that people need help completing. The next steps involve researching the relevant formula or criteria, building an interface around the task, and checking that the implementation produces appropriate results.

Step 1

Understanding the Problem

A new tool should address a recognisable need rather than exist simply to increase the number of available pages.

The development process begins by defining what the user needs to calculate, understand, or accomplish.

Step 2

Researching the Relevant Rules

Where a tool depends on a mathematical formula, grading policy, or admission criterion, the underlying logic needs to be examined carefully.

Official documentation and authoritative references are particularly important for education-related calculations because requirements can vary or change. Where uncertainty exists, the limitations and assumptions of a tool should be made clear.

Step 3

Building for the Browser

AfriWidget uses lightweight web technologies to provide tools that can be accessed through modern browsers.

The development approach favours direct interfaces and avoids unnecessary complexity where a simpler implementation can achieve the same purpose.

Step 4

Testing and Improving

Calculations should be checked against appropriate examples and expected outcomes. Inputs that fall outside ordinary ranges, missing information, and other edge cases also need consideration.

Tools may be refined as problems are identified, requirements change, or users provide useful feedback. No software should be assumed to be error-free, but careful testing and ongoing maintenance help improve reliability.

Privacy, Access, and Transparency

AfriWidget aims to make its core tools accessible without mandatory account creation or payment for ordinary use.

Where calculations run locally, the browser performs the relevant mathematical operations on the user's device. Features that preserve inputs or progress may use local storage, rather than a remote account.

However, browser-based computation does not automatically mean that every interaction across a website is private or that no data is ever transmitted. Hosting services, analytics, advertising, external integrations, and optional AI features may involve separate data practices.

Users should consult the platform's Privacy Policy for information about data handling and any applicable third-party services.

AfriWidget also recognises that useful tools have limits:

  • An educational calculator can estimate a score but cannot guarantee admission.
  • A financial calculator can model a scenario but cannot predict every market outcome.
  • A wellness tool can estimate a measurement but cannot diagnose a medical condition.

The purpose of the platform is to make calculations and planning easier while helping users understand the limits of the information they receive.
