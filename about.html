About AfriWidget

Built in Ghana. Made for the World. A Platform That Puts People First

The story, the motivation, and the reason I built AfriWidget: a free, browser-based toolkit designed to help students, workers, and everyday people get things done without paywalls or data tracking.

What Is AfriWidget?

AfriWidget is a collection of free online tools that run directly inside your web browser. You don't have to download an app, create an account, or hand over your personal details to use any of it. You open the website, pick the tool you need, type in your numbers, and see your answer right there on your screen. That's the entire process.

I built AfriWidget because useful everyday tools should be free and accessible to everybody. Not just people who can pay for software subscriptions, hire consultants, or afford high-speed unlimited Wi-Fi. If you're a high school student calculating your exam aggregate, a freelance designer writing an invoice, a parent checking your child's BMI, or someone planning monthly repayments on a bank loan, these tools are here for you without costing a single cedi.

Today, AfriWidget has more than 120 tools divided across five areas: education, finance, health, productivity, and business. Every tool is built to be fast, private, and straightforward. Nothing is hidden behind a paywall. Nothing is sold to advertisers. And nothing asks you to sign in before you can use it.

In one sentence: AfriWidget exists to get rid of the annoying obstacles that stop people from solving everyday problems. No logins. No fees. Total privacy.

The Story Behind AfriWidget

Every website starts somewhere. Mine started with a real headache right here in Ghana.

I grew up in Accra and Volta Region. Like a lot of young people across our country, money was tight and good opportunities were hard to come by. I saw family members and close friends get stuck making important decisions simply because they didn't have the right calculation tool. And whenever a tool did exist online, it was usually buried behind a subscription wall, cluttered with spammy banners, or completely broken on an entry-level mobile phone.

I studied business at Keta Business College. While I was there, I noticed how often everyday life comes down to simple math that people feel unsure about. A student trying to guess what exam grade will protect their GPA. A market trader trying to calculate if a high-interest microloan will actually make them a profit. A parent worried about a teenager's weight. An applicant holding an exam slip, wondering if their grades meet university admission cut-offs. In every case, the answer was just basic arithmetic. But there was no free, clean, private website to do the math for them.

So I decided to build it myself.

Learning to Build

I work alone without co-founders or outside investors, fancy tech mentors, or a computer science degree. What I had was an old, beat-up laptop, a lot of patience, and an internet connection whenever I had enough spare cash to buy a prepaid data bundle. I taught myself how the web works from free documentation. I learned how to structure HTML, style pages with CSS, write JavaScript logic, and host a live website.

It was not easy. Not at all. I spent countless late nights staring at confusing syntax errors on a dim screen. I built tools that gave the wrong output on the first try. I wrote code that crashed my mobile browser. But every bug was a lesson, and I kept fixing them one by one.

Slowly, my small side project turned into a working platform. The first tools were basic: a GPA calculator, a compound interest model, and a simple BMI check. But as people started using the site, they began messaging me asking for more. A student needed WASSCE aggregate math. A teacher wanted BECE placement tools. Every message turned into another late night writing code, and every new user reminded me why this work mattered.

Why Privacy Comes First

From day one, I built AfriWidget with a hard rule: I will never collect your data. That wasn't a marketing gimmick. It's a personal conviction.

When you type your exam grades, your monthly salary, your body weight, or your debt balance into a calculator, that information belongs to you. It's nobody else's business. It should never be sent across the web, stored in a database, or bundled up to sell to ad brokers.

That's why every calculator on AfriWidget runs entirely inside your own browser. Your phone or computer does all the math locally. Nothing gets sent to my server. When you close the browser tab, your numbers are gone. No history logs, no user profiles, and no tracking cookies. I built it this way from the start.

“When you help someone solve a real problem, they remember it. That is the only marketing that matters.”
Obed Avorlenu, Founder of AfriWidget

Why I Focus on Africa and the World

AfriWidget was born in Ghana, and I pay special attention to African users. That's because this is where the gap between needing a good tool and finding one is the widest.

Across Africa, hundreds of millions of people access the internet almost exclusively on mobile phones. Mobile data is expensive. Many people use older Android phones with limited memory. Connections drop frequently between 2G, 3G, and 4G. When a website is packed with heavy scripts and auto-playing video ads, it freezes the phone and burns through prepaid airtime before the page even finishes loading. That's unacceptable.

I build AfriWidget specifically for this reality. Pages are lightweight and load in less than a second. There are no bloated libraries, no flashing pop-ups, and no heavy graphics. The layout is designed for your thumb on a mobile screen first, because that's how most of us browse.

At the same time, the tools work for anyone in the world. A student in the UK can use the UCAS points calculator. Someone in Canada can calculate loan amortization. A freelancer in the US can generate a PDF invoice. A parent anywhere can check daily calorie needs. The platform is grounded in Accra, but it's built for everyone.

What Makes AfriWidget Different

There are plenty of calculator sites online. Some are okay, but most are cluttered with intrusive ads, paywalls, and confusing menus. Here's how I do things differently.

1. No Accounts, Ever

You never have to sign up. You never give me an email address or create a password. Just open the tool, calculate your numbers, and close the tab when you're done.

2. Everything Runs in Your Browser

Your inputs never leave your device. I don't collect them, I don't store them, and I can't see them even if I wanted to. The math happens on your phone, not on my server.

3. Built for Slow Connections and Cheap Phones

Every page is feather-light. AfriWidget works reliably on slow 3G networks, older smartphones, and tight prepaid data plans. You don't need a flagship device to get an instant answer.

4. Verified Formulas and Real Rules

I don't guess the math. Health calculators follow official World Health Organization formulas and Mifflin-St Jeor standards. Academic tools follow published grading criteria from WAEC, KNEC, and official university boards. Financial calculators use standard compound interest and amortization formulas.

5. 100% Free Forever

There's no pro version, no trial period, and no hidden charges. What you see is completely free to use whenever you need it.

The Tools I Build

I organize the platform into five main categories, covering the calculations people face most often.

Education & Academic Tools

This is where AfriWidget started. High school and university students across Africa have to deal with confusing admissions systems. A Ghanaian student needs WASSCE aggregate points. A Kenyan student needs KCSE and KUCCPS cluster points. A Nigerian applicant needs JAMB UTME calculations. South African students need their APS score from Matric results. Zimbabwean students need ZIMSEC points.

I turned these complicated grading systems into simple forms. You type in your grades, and the tool gives you an accurate aggregate based on official exam board rules. No middlemen, and no paying twenty cedis to someone at a cyber cafe just to add up your slip.

Finance & Money Tools

Money decisions can make or break your finances. Should you take out a small business loan? What will you actually pay in total interest? How fast will your savings grow with compound interest? My finance tools cover loans, mortgages, compound growth, and emergency savings. Every tool shows you the breakdown so you understand where every cedi, naira, or dollar goes.

Health & Wellness Tools

These tools help you keep an eye on basic health metrics. You can calculate body mass index (BMI), basal metabolic rate, daily water intake, and calorie needs. But here's an important reminder: these calculators are for general awareness only. They're not medical diagnoses. If you have real health concerns, always see a doctor.

Productivity & Everyday Utilities

All the small, annoying digital tasks that pop up during the week. Clean up messy text, format cases, generate a quick QR code, calculate reading time for a speech, or convert units without searching through five different ad-loaded websites.

Business & Freelance Tools

Built for solo freelancers, trade workers, and small shop owners. You can generate clean PDF invoices with tax and discounts, calculate profit markups, and figure out your break-even point without paying for expensive accounting software.

How I Build and Maintain the Tools

Every tool on this site goes through the same disciplined steps.

Step 1: Start with a Real Need

I don't build tools just to look busy. I build them when people ask for them. When students, teachers, or shopkeepers message me with a problem they can't easily solve online, that's when I start coding.

Step 2: Research the Official Rules

Before I write a single line of JavaScript, I dig into the official documentation. For academic tools, that means reading the exam board's published grading syllabus. For finance tools, that means checking standard banking formulas. If the official guidelines are vague, I say so right on the page.

Step 3: Keep the Code Simple

I write clean, plain HTML, CSS, and lightweight JavaScript. No massive JavaScript frameworks, no heavy external libraries, and no tracking scripts that slow down your browser.

Step 4: Test with Real Examples

I test every calculation against real exam slips, loan contracts, and health charts. If a WASSCE aggregate doesn't match what WAEC calculates, the tool doesn't go live.

Step 5: Listen, Fix, and Update

Once a tool is live, my work isn't done. When users email me about an edge case or when an exam board updates their grading scale, I update the code as fast as I can.

How I Protect Your Privacy

Privacy isn't an afterthought on AfriWidget. It's the core architecture of the entire platform.

When you use any calculator here, here is exactly what happens:

  • Your browser downloads the lightweight page.
  • You type your numbers into the form.
  • Your device runs the math locally using client-side JavaScript.
  • The answer appears instantly on your screen.
  • Nothing is sent to a server. Nothing is saved. Nothing is logged.

I don't run a database that records what you calculate. I don't build profiles about your finances or health. Even if someone asked me to reveal what you calculated, I couldn't do it, because the data never reaches me in the first place.

I use basic privacy-friendly traffic analytics to see how many visitors come to the site, but it never tracks your personal identity or your inputs. Small Google AdSense ads help cover my server and domain costs, but those ads have zero access to your numbers.

Bottom line: Your numbers stay on your device. Always.

What AfriWidget Is Not

I like being upfront about what this site is and what it isn't.

This is not a substitute for professional advice. If you have a serious medical symptom, please see a doctor. If you're signing a major mortgage or loan contract, consult a certified financial advisor. AfriWidget gives you fast, reliable estimates, but it doesn't replace professional human judgment.

I am not a data broker. I don't harvest your numbers, I don't sell user lists, and I don't run tracking schemes. My only goal is to provide useful tools that work.

I'm not trying to build a bloated corporate startup. I don't want venture capital, and I don't want to lock features behind paid tiers. I just want to build great tools that help people get work done.
