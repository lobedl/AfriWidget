What this calculator does

This tool takes the grades you have entered for three Principal subjects, General Paper, and Subsidiary Mathematics or Subsidiary ICT, converts them to UNEB points, and produces three outputs: your raw UACE score out of a maximum of 20, the number of Principal Passes you hold, and a PUJAB weighted score that reflects how public universities in Uganda rank candidates for competitive programmes. The PUJAB section also lets you add O-Level points and the female affirmative action bonus, both of which influence the final cut-off figure used by Makerere University and other public universities.

The arithmetic is short, but the two rules that govern admission in Uganda are not the same rule, and mixing them up is a common source of confusion. The raw UACE score out of 20 is the UNEB figure that appears on the certificate. The PUJAB weighted score is a separate figure produced by the universities when they apply subject weights to your A-Level grades and add the O-Level contribution. A candidate can score well on one and moderately on the other, depending on which subjects they took and how the weights fall for their target programme.

Every calculation runs inside your browser. No grade, no subject name, and no personal detail is uploaded. If you press the AI insight button, only an anonymous summary of the number of Principal Passes, the raw points, the weighted score, and the eligibility band is sent to a small serverless function that runs once and then stops.

The 20-point UACE structure

The Uganda Advanced Certificate of Education is the terminal A-Level examination administered by UNEB at the end of Senior Six. Every candidate who registers for the full UACE takes three Principal subjects and two subsidiary subjects. The subsidiary subjects are almost always General Paper and Subsidiary Mathematics or Subsidiary ICT, though some combinations substitute a third subsidiary in particular circumstances. The maximum raw score out of 20 is built from those five subjects.

Three Principal subjects at the top grade contribute 18 points, and the two subsidiary subjects at a pass contribute 2 more, taking the total to 20. A candidate who earns A grades in all three Principal subjects and passes both subsidiary subjects reaches the maximum. A candidate who earns E grades in all three Principal subjects and passes both subsidiary subjects reaches 8 points, which is still enough for direct degree entry because the threshold is a count of Principal Passes rather than a minimum total.

The scale runs from 6 at the top down to 0 at the bottom, and each Principal subject contributes independently to both the raw total and to the count of Principal Passes. A Grade O on a Principal subject still contributes 1 point to the raw total but does not count as a Principal Pass, because a Principal Pass requires a Grade E or better, which is 2 points or more.

Principal and subsidiary subjects explained

Principal subjects are the three main subjects that define a candidate's A-Level combination. They are the subjects that a university looks at most closely when assessing an applicant, and they carry the greatest weight in the PUJAB formula. A candidate who takes Physics, Chemistry, and Mathematics has three Principal subjects in the sciences. A candidate who takes Economics, Geography, and Divinity has three Principal subjects in the humanities. The combination is chosen at the start of Senior Five and determines the range of degree programmes the candidate can apply for.

Subsidiary subjects sit alongside the Principal subjects and are assessed at a lower level of depth. General Paper is compulsory for every UACE candidate and covers reasoning, communication, and general knowledge. Subsidiary Mathematics or Subsidiary ICT is compulsory for candidates whose Principal combination does not already include Mathematics or Computing at Principal level. Both subsidiary subjects contribute one point to the raw total for a pass, and both are weighted at 1 in the PUJAB formula. Failing either removes both the raw point and the weighted contribution.

The total UACE combination is therefore five subjects in most cases: three at Principal level and two at Subsidiary level. Some candidates take an additional subsidiary subject, but the standard UNEB structure for the twenty-point score is three Principals and two Subsidiaries, and that is the structure this calculator applies.

The two Principal Pass rule for degree entry

The minimum requirement for direct entry into a Bachelor's degree programme in Uganda is two Principal Passes obtained at the same sitting of the UACE examination. A Principal Pass means a Grade E or better on a Principal subject, corresponding to 2 points or more on the UNEB scale. The rule is set by the National Council for Higher Education and applied by every public university and by most accredited private universities, though some private institutions apply additional or slightly different thresholds for specific programmes.

The count of Principal Passes is independent of the raw total. A candidate with two Grade E subjects and one Grade F subject has two Principal Passes and one fail, and the raw total from those three subjects is 4 points. That candidate qualifies for direct degree entry even though the raw total is low, because the rule is expressed in terms of passes rather than points. A candidate with three Grade O subjects has three subsidiary passes and zero Principal Passes, and does not qualify for direct degree entry despite earning 3 raw points.

The sitting requirement is also important. Both Principal Passes must be obtained at the same UACE examination. Combining a Principal Pass from one sitting with a Principal Pass from another is not accepted for direct degree entry under the standard rules, and this is one of the reasons candidates who narrowly miss the threshold in a single sitting often choose to repeat a year at a school or to enter a diploma programme that bridges to a degree later.

Direct degree entry: two Principal Passes (Grade E or better) in one sitting.
Diploma entry: one Principal Pass plus two subsidiary passes, or a relevant certificate.
Certificate entry: subsidiary passes only, or UCE with bridging.

The PUJAB weighting system in detail

When public universities in Uganda receive applications from thousands of UACE candidates for a limited number of places on each programme, they need a ranking system that reflects how relevant each subject is to the programme. The Public Universities Joint Admissions Board uses a weighting system that assigns each A-Level subject to one of three categories, and applies a multiplier to the grade points depending on the category.

Essential subjects are the two most critical subjects for the degree programme being applied for. For Medicine and Surgery, the Essential subjects are Biology and Chemistry. For Electrical Engineering, they are Mathematics and Physics. For Law, they are Literature in English and one other subject specified by the programme. Each Essential subject is weighted at 3, so a Grade A contributes 18 to the weighted score, a Grade B contributes 15, and so on down the scale.

The Relevant subject is the third Principal subject that supports the programme. It is weighted at 2, so a Grade A contributes 12 to the weighted score and a Grade C contributes 8. The Relevant subject is a meaningful contributor but carries two-thirds the weight of an Essential subject.

Desirable subjects are General Paper and Subsidiary Mathematics or Subsidiary ICT. Both are weighted at 1, so each contributes between 0 and 1 point to the weighted score. They are small contributors in absolute terms, but on competitive programmes where the cut-off margin is a fraction of a point, they can make the difference between an offer and a rejection.

PUJAB A-Level score = (Essential 1 × 3) + (Essential 2 × 3) + (Relevant × 2) + (GP × 1) + (Subsidiary × 1)
Then add O-Level points and any affirmative action bonus.

An important point of detail is that the assignment of subjects to Essential, Relevant, and Desirable categories changes from programme to programme. A candidate applying for Engineering has different Essential subjects than the same candidate applying for Economics. The weights shown in this calculator assume the first two Principal subjects are Essential and the third Principal subject is Relevant, which is the most common arrangement. Candidates applying to a programme where the categories differ should consult the specific university prospectus for that programme.

O-Level points and affirmative action

The PUJAB weighted score does not end at the A-Level contribution. Universities add points for O-Level performance at a rate that varies slightly between institutions but follows a common pattern. Each Distinction at UCE level adds 0.3 points, each Credit adds 0.2 points, and each Pass adds 0.1 points. The number of subjects counted is usually capped, and the cap varies by institution and programme, so the O-Level contribution typically tops out at around 2.5 to 3.0 points on a strong UCE record.

The female affirmative action bonus adds a flat 1.5 points for every female applicant, regardless of grades. The bonus is applied after the A-Level and O-Level contributions have been summed, and it is one of the reasons that the effective cut-off for a female applicant is slightly lower than the published cut-off for the same programme. The bonus reflects a policy of widening access for female candidates and is applied consistently across all public universities that participate in the PUJAB system.

Both the O-Level points and the female bonus are added to the weighted score rather than to the raw UACE score. The raw score out of 20 remains the UNEB figure on the certificate, and the weighted score is the university-specific figure used for ranking. Candidates should keep the two separate in their heads, because the raw score is what an employer or a foreign university will recognise, while the weighted score is what determines admission to a specific Ugandan programme.

A worked example

Take a female candidate applying for a Bachelor of Science in Civil Engineering at Makerere University. The candidate's A-Level combination is Mathematics, Physics, and Chemistry, with General Paper and Subsidiary ICT at subsidiary level. The grades are Mathematics A, Physics B, Chemistry C, General Paper pass, Subsidiary ICT pass. The candidate also has six UCE distinctions and three UCE credits on the O-Level record.

The raw UACE score is calculated first. Mathematics at Grade A contributes 6 points, Physics at Grade B contributes 5 points, Chemistry at Grade C contributes 4 points, General Paper pass contributes 1 point, and Subsidiary ICT pass contributes 1 point. The raw total is 17 out of 20. The candidate holds three Principal Passes, since all three Principal subjects are at Grade E or better, so the direct degree entry rule is comfortably satisfied.

The PUJAB weighted score uses the Essential and Relevant categories for Civil Engineering. Mathematics and Physics are the Essential subjects for this programme, so Mathematics contributes 6 multiplied by 3, which is 18, and Physics contributes 5 multiplied by 3, which is 15. Chemistry is the Relevant subject, contributing 4 multiplied by 2, which is 8. General Paper contributes 1 multiplied by 1, which is 1. Subsidiary ICT contributes 1 multiplied by 1, which is 1. The A-Level weighted subtotal is 18 plus 15 plus 8 plus 1 plus 1, which equals 43.

The O-Level contribution is then added. Six Distinctions at 0.3 each contribute 1.8. Three Credits at 0.2 each contribute 0.6. The O-Level subtotal is 2.4. Because the candidate is female, the affirmative action bonus of 1.5 is added. The final PUJAB weighted score is 43 plus 2.4 plus 1.5, which equals 46.9. Whether that figure is above the cut-off for Civil Engineering at Makerere in any given year depends on the applicant pool and the number of places, but a weighted score in the high 40s is historically competitive for the programme.

To see the sensitivity of the score, suppose the candidate had taken Chemistry instead of Physics as the second Essential subject, which would be the case for a Chemical Engineering application rather than a Civil Engineering one. The subject weights would shift, and the weighted score would change even though the raw UACE score remained identical. This is why the programme of application matters as much as the grades when estimating a PUJAB cut-off.

Common mistakes that cost admission

A handful of recurring errors turn an otherwise competitive application into a rejection. Understanding them in advance is worth more than any single revision session.

Confusing the raw score with the weighted score. The raw score out of 20 is the figure on the UNEB certificate. The weighted score is the figure the university uses for ranking. A candidate who prepares an application around the raw score alone can be surprised when the weighted score, which depends on the Essential and Relevant categories for the specific programme, comes out much lower or much higher than expected. Always estimate the weighted score for the specific programme being applied for, not just for the general combination.

Ignoring the same-sitting rule. Both Principal Passes must be obtained at the same UACE sitting. A candidate who earns one Principal Pass in one year and the second in the following year cannot combine them for direct degree entry. The second year of study is not wasted, because the second Principal Pass can still count toward diploma admission or toward a future direct application if the candidate repeats the first subject to bring both passes into the same sitting.

Underestimating subsidiary subjects. General Paper and Subsidiary Mathematics or Subsidiary ICT each contribute one point to the raw score and one point to the weighted score. On a competitive programme where the cut-off margin is half a point, failing General Paper can be the difference between an offer and a rejection. The subsidiary subjects are not the main event, but they are not negligible either.

Assuming the O-Level contribution is fixed. The number of O-Level subjects counted, and the exact rate at which Distinctions, Credits, and Passes convert to points, varies slightly by institution and programme. A candidate who treats the O-Level addition as a fixed 2.4 points across all applications may find that the effective contribution is lower or higher at a specific university. Check the prospectus.

Missing the programme-specific subject combination requirement. Most competitive degree programmes require specific Principal subjects at Grade C or above as a hard prerequisite. A candidate with a strong PUJAB weighted score but a Grade D in a required subject can be rejected outright on the subject combination rule, regardless of the weighted score. The JAMB-equivalent subject combination table for the programme is usually in the university prospectus, and it should be read carefully before applying.

Assuming all public universities weight the same way. The PUJAB system is applied across the participating public universities, but the assignment of subjects to Essential, Relevant, and Desirable categories can differ for the same programme at different universities. The same candidate applying for Economics at Makerere and at Kyambogo may see different weighted scores, because the two institutions define the Essential subjects differently. Use the weighting that applies to the specific institution you are targeting.

Where this calculator stops

This tool estimates the UACE raw score and the PUJAB weighted score from the grades you enter, applying the standard UNEB scale, the two Principal Pass rule, and the standard PUJAB weighting conventions. It does not know the specific Essential, Relevant, and Desirable assignment for the programme you are targeting, does not know the exact number of O-Level subjects that your chosen university will count, and does not know the current-year cut-off for any specific programme. Those details change annually with the applicant pool and the number of places, and the university prospectus for the year of application is the definitive source.

The raw score out of 20 and the count of Principal Passes shown here match the published UNEB framework, and the PUJAB weighted score follows the Essential, Relevant, Desirable multiplier pattern used across the participating public universities. What the tool does not do is guarantee an admission outcome, because the cut-off for any given programme depends on how many candidates apply, how strong their grades are, and how many places the university has allocated. Use this calculator to see where you stand, to plan a rewrite or a diploma route if the two Principal Pass rule is not yet met, and to estimate what a change in one subject grade would do to the weighted score before you decide where to apply.

Frequently asked questions

How is the 20-point UACE score calculated?

The UACE raw score is built from three Principal subjects, General Paper, and Subsidiary Mathematics or Subsidiary ICT. Each Principal subject is scored on a six-point scale where A earns 6, B earns 5, C earns 4, D earns 3, E earns 2, O earns 1, and F earns 0, for a maximum of 18 points from the three Principal subjects. General Paper and the Subsidiary subject each earn 1 point for a subsidiary pass, adding another 2. The maximum raw score is therefore 20. Grades below the subsidiary pass level contribute nothing, so a candidate who fails General Paper loses that point entirely.

What is the minimum requirement for direct university degree admission in Uganda?

Under the framework published by the National Council for Higher Education and applied by the public universities, the minimum requirement for direct entry into a Bachelor's degree programme is two Principal Passes obtained at the same sitting of the UACE examination. A Principal Pass means a Grade E or better on a Principal subject, which corresponds to 2 points or more on the UNEB scale. A candidate with only one Principal Pass can enter a Diploma programme, and a candidate with only subsidiary passes can enter a higher certificate or a skills-based training programme.

How does the Makerere University PUJAB weighting system work?

Public universities in Uganda weight each UACE subject according to its importance for the specific degree programme being applied for. Essential subjects, which are the two most critical for the programme, are multiplied by 3. The Relevant subject, which is the third Principal subject supporting the programme, is multiplied by 2. Desirable subjects, namely General Paper and Subsidiary Mathematics or Subsidiary ICT, are multiplied by 1. On top of the A-Level contribution, the university adds points for O-Level performance at the rate of 0.3 per Distinction, 0.2 per Credit, and 0.1 per Pass, and applies a 1.5-point bonus for female applicants under the affirmative action scheme.

What happens if I get a Grade O on a Principal subject?

A Grade O is a subsidiary pass on the UNEB scale. It earns 1 point toward your UACE raw score, so it does contribute to the total out of 20, but it does not count as a Principal Pass for the two Principal Pass rule that governs direct degree entry. A candidate with one Grade E and two Grade O subjects therefore has one Principal Pass and two subsidiary passes, which qualifies for a diploma programme but not for a direct Bachelor's degree. If a degree is the goal, the subject needs to be resat and cleared at Grade E or higher.

Can I combine UACE results from two different exam sittings?

For direct degree admissions under the government and most private university regulations, the minimum two Principal Passes must be obtained at the same sitting of the UACE examination. Combining a Principal Pass from one year with a Principal Pass from another is generally not accepted for direct degree entry, though it is sometimes accepted for diploma admission under rules that vary by institution. If you are close to the two Principal Pass threshold and did not clear it in one sitting, speak to the admissions office of the university you are targeting to find out whether a supplementary sitting or a diploma route is the better path.

Does General Paper contribute to the university cut-off?

Yes. General Paper contributes one point to your UACE raw score if you obtain a subsidiary pass, and it is weighted at 1 in the PUJAB formula. Failing General Paper removes both the raw point and the weighted contribution, which can be a meaningful loss on a competitive programme where the cut-off margins are tight. Subsidiary Mathematics or Subsidiary ICT works in exactly the same way, contributing one raw point and one weighted point for a subsidiary pass.

How are O-Level points added to the PUJAB weighted score?

The PUJAB weighting system adds points for your Uganda Certificate of Education performance. Each Distinction at D1 or D2 level adds 0.3 points, each Credit at C3 to C6 level adds 0.2 points, and each Pass at P7 or P8 level adds 0.1 points. These O-Level points are added to the A-Level weighted score and can shift the final cut-off figure by two or more points on a strong UCE record. The exact number of subjects counted varies by institution and programme, so check the specific university prospectus before treating the O-Level addition as fixed.

Does this calculator store my grades or share my results?

No. Every calculation runs inside your browser tab and nothing is stored. If you press the AI insight button, only an anonymous summary of the number of Principal Passes, the raw points out of 20, the PUJAB weighted score, and the eligibility band is sent to a small serverless function that runs once and then stops. Your subject names, grade entries, and any personal identifiers never leave the browser.
