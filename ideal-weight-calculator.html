What “Ideal Weight” Really Means

Ideal weight calculators are based on reference equations that connect height with a historical weight estimate. They were originally developed for specific clinical, insurance, or dosing contexts, not as a universal definition of attractiveness, health, or personal worth. A height-based formula can provide a rough comparison, but it cannot see muscle mass, bone structure, body-fat distribution, age, medical history, or how a person feels and functions.

This tool shows four familiar formulas: Devine, Robinson, Miller, and Hamwi. They produce a reference value from height above a base height of five feet, with different coefficients for the selected sex category. Showing several results makes the disagreement visible and is more honest than presenting one number as the answer. The displayed range is a comparison of those formulas, not a recommended target.

How to Use This Calculator

Enter height in centimetres and select the sex category used by the historical equations. If you know your height in feet and inches, convert it before entering it: 1 inch equals 2.54 centimetres. Stand without shoes on a level surface, keep your head in a neutral position, and measure consistently. Small height errors can shift the result because the formulas use inches.

After selecting Calculate, compare the four reference values. They will usually be different because each formula uses different assumptions. The broad comparison range is the lowest to highest displayed value, rounded for readability. It should not be interpreted as the medically “safe” or “correct” range for every person at that height.

Why the Formulas Disagree

The Devine formula was developed in the 1970s and is often encountered in medication dosing references. Robinson and Miller introduced alternative equations with different base values and increments. Hamwi is another clinical reference formula. These equations were created in eras when populations, measurement practices, and clinical questions differed. None was designed to account for every body shape or every global population.

Formula outputs are especially limited for people with high muscle mass, unusual body proportions, significant fluid retention, limb differences, pregnancy, older age, or conditions that change body composition. A person can be healthy outside a formula range, and a person inside a range can still have a health concern. Height-based results should be a conversation starter, not a label.

Better Context Than a Single Number

Body mass index uses height and weight but has similar limitations. Waist circumference, blood pressure, cholesterol, blood glucose, fitness, sleep, diet quality, medications, and family history may offer more useful context depending on the question. A clinician can interpret those factors together. If your goal is strength, sport, recovery, or a change in body composition, a scale target alone may not measure progress well.

Healthy weight is also influenced by culture, food access, stress, work, disability, climate, and the ability to move safely. Sustainable habits can include regular meals, adequate protein and fibre, enjoyable movement, restorative sleep, hydration, and routine medical care. You do not need to reach a formula result to deserve respect or to benefit from caring for your health.

Medical and Educational Disclaimer

This calculator is for general education only. It does not diagnose undernutrition, overweight, obesity, or any other condition, and it does not prescribe a diet, calorie target, medication dose, or treatment. The equations are population-based references with limitations and should not replace a doctor, registered dietitian, or qualified healthcare professional.

Do not use a reference weight to justify crash dieting, severe restriction, purging, excessive exercise, or unsafe weight gain. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, under 18, recovering from an eating disorder, managing a chronic condition, taking medicines affected by weight, or experiencing unexplained weight change, ask a clinician for individual guidance. Seek help promptly if weight concerns are causing distress or interfering with daily life.

Ideal Weight Calculator FAQ

Which formula is most accurate?

None is universally most accurate. Devine, Robinson, Miller, and Hamwi are reference equations with different origins and assumptions. A professional assessment is more appropriate for an individual decision.

Why do you show a range?

The formulas often disagree. Showing their spread prevents one historical equation from looking like a precise personal target.

Can I use this for children?

No. These adult reference formulas are not appropriate for children or teenagers. Growth should be assessed with age- and sex-specific charts by a qualified clinician.
