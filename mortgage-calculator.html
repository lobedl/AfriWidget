About This Mortgage Calculator

This tool estimates the principal-and-interest payment for a fixed-rate loan and helps compare down payments, interest rates, and repayment terms. It is useful before speaking with a lender because it separates the loan mathematics from other housing costs such as taxes, insurance, maintenance, and closing fees.

How to Use the Mortgage Calculator

Enter the property price, down payment, annual interest rate, and loan term. Add gross income and existing monthly debts when you want the DTI analysis. Review monthly payment, total interest, and the amortisation schedule together, then compare a second scenario with a different down payment or term.

Example: Comparing a Loan Scenario

For a $200,000 property with a $40,000 down payment, the starting principal is $160,000. The final monthly payment depends on the interest rate and term entered, while the full schedule shows how early payments contain more interest and later payments reduce principal faster. The result is an estimate, not a lender quote.

How the Mortgage Payment Calculator Works

This calculator uses the standard fixed-rate mortgage amortisation formula to compute your monthly principal and interest payment: M = P × [r(1+r)ⁿ] ÷ [(1+r)ⁿ − 1], where P is the loan principal (home price minus down payment), r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12), and n is the total number of monthly payments (loan term in years multiplied by 12).

The result is the amount you pay each month to fully retire the loan by the end of the term. Note that this figure covers principal and interest only — your actual monthly housing cost will also include property taxes, homeowner's insurance, and potentially private mortgage insurance (PMI) if your down payment is below 20% of the purchase price.

Understanding Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio

If you enter your gross monthly income, the calculator computes two critical DTI metrics that lenders use to assess your borrowing eligibility. The front-end DTI (or housing ratio) is your monthly mortgage payment divided by your gross monthly income — most conventional lenders prefer this to remain below 28%. The back-end DTI adds all your other monthly debt obligations (car loans, credit cards, student loans, etc.) to your mortgage payment and divides by income — a back-end DTI below 36% is generally considered strong, while most lenders will approve loans up to 43%.

The Power of the Down Payment

Your down payment is one of the most powerful levers available to you as a homebuyer. A larger down payment reduces your principal, which directly reduces your monthly payment and the total interest you will pay over the life of the loan. It also eliminates or reduces private mortgage insurance requirements on conventional loans — PMI typically costs 0.5%–1.5% of the loan amount per year until your equity reaches 20%. For buyers in African markets where mortgage products are structured differently — often with shorter terms, higher rates, or balloon payment features — the principles of down payment leverage are equally applicable.

Mortgage Term: 30 vs. 15 Years

The choice between a 30-year and 15-year mortgage term is one of the most consequential decisions in home financing. A 30-year mortgage offers a lower monthly payment, preserving cash flow for other financial goals. However, you will pay significantly more in total interest — often more than the original purchase price of the home — over the life of the loan. A 15-year mortgage carries a higher monthly payment but typically comes with a lower interest rate, and you build equity twice as fast while paying far less total interest. Use this calculator to compare both scenarios side by side and understand the real cost of each option.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the payment include taxes and insurance?

No. The main payment is principal and interest. Add property taxes, insurance, service charges, maintenance, and any mortgage insurance when estimating the full monthly housing cost.

Why does a longer term cost more?

A longer term usually lowers the required monthly payment but gives interest more months to accumulate. Compare total interest as well as monthly affordability.

Is this a lender approval decision?

No. Lenders also review credit history, income verification, property valuation, deposit source, local rules, and their own affordability criteria.
