Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between statutory minimum admission and a faculty cut-off?

Statutory minimum admission means you carry the right endorsement on your Matric certificate. A Bachelor’s pass makes you legally qualified to apply for a degree. A faculty cut-off is a much higher competitive point score that individual departments apply once thousands of applications arrive for a few hundred places. You can meet the statutory minimum and still miss the faculty cut-off by a wide margin.

How is Life Orientation counted in the APS?

Institutional rules differ. Universities such as Pretoria and Stellenbosch leave Life Orientation out of the calculation entirely, so the maximum APS is 42. Universities such as Johannesburg assign a reduced score, usually between one and four points depending on the level you achieved. Some institutions include it as a full seventh subject. The calculator applies the rule of the institution you select.

What should I do if my APS is one or two points below the cut-off?

Apply anyway. Extended Curriculum Programmes, which add a foundational first year to a standard degree, often admit candidates with APS scores a few points below the direct-entry threshold. List an ECP route, a related diploma, or a less competitive institution as a second and third choice so that a good outcome stays realistic.

Does the calculator send my marks anywhere?

No. Every calculation runs inside your browser and nothing you type leaves your device. If you choose to press the optional AI button, only an anonymous summary of your computed APS, pass level, and faculty target is sent to generate a short written response. Nothing is stored.

Can I use this for TVET colleges and universities of technology?

Yes. The calculator includes a National Diploma or TVET stream with typical APS benchmarks in the low twenties. Universities of Technology such as TUT, DUT, and CPUT apply those thresholds and often use a Diploma pass endorsement rather than a Bachelor’s pass.

Does the result replace an official admissions decision?

No. What you get here is a planning estimate. The official decision comes from the university after it has verified your certificate, checked your subject prerequisites, and ranked your application against the current year’s applicant pool. Use this result to plan, not to decide.
