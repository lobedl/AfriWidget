How to calculate your South African APS

The Admission Point Score, commonly called APS, is one of the numbers South African matriculants use when researching university programmes. It converts National Senior Certificate percentages into points. The point total helps an applicant compare a result with a programme’s published minimum, but it is not a universal national admission guarantee. Wits, UCT, Stellenbosch, and other institutions can use different subject rules, faculty requirements, and selection methods.

This calculator follows the scale supplied for this tool. A percentage from 80 to 100 earns 7 points, 70 to 79 earns 6 points, 60 to 69 earns 5 points, 50 to 59 earns 4 points, 40 to 49 earns 3 points, 30 to 39 earns 2 points, and 0 to 29 earns 1 point. Enter six main subject percentages to get a maximum of 42 points. The page does not ask for a subject name because the point conversion is based on the percentage; you should still compare the result with the exact subjects required by your programme.

Life Orientation is institution-specific

Some institutions exclude Life Orientation from their APS calculation, while others may include it under specific rules. That is why the calculator has a separate checkbox. Leave it unticked when your university says to exclude Life Orientation. Tick it only when your institution’s current prospectus or admissions page includes it, then enter the percentage. When included, the result can reach 49 points because a seventh subject contributes up to 7 points.

The checkbox does not change the six main subject values. It adds Life Orientation as an additional converted subject. If it is selected but no Life Orientation percentage is entered, the calculator asks you to complete the field rather than treating a blank as zero. That distinction matters because an empty input is missing information, not a low mark.

Example calculation

Suppose six percentages are 82, 74, 68, 61, 55, and 47. They convert to 7, 6, 5, 5, 4, and 3 points, for an APS of 30. If a university permits Life Orientation and the result is 72, it contributes 6 additional points and the displayed total becomes 36. Another university might exclude it and use the six-subject result of 30. Always use the version that matches the admissions rule for the programme.

Planning beyond the number

APS is only one part of an application. A programme can require Mathematics rather than Mathematical Literacy, a minimum percentage in Physical Sciences, a language requirement, or a specific subject combination. Competitive programmes can also rank applicants above a published minimum when there are more applicants than spaces. Check application deadlines, faculty selection tests, residence dates, and document requirements. Use this calculator to organise your questions, not to replace official advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is the highest APS on this page?

Six main subjects produce a maximum of 42 points. If Life Orientation is explicitly included, the maximum becomes 49 under this scale.

Should I include Life Orientation?

Only when the university or programme’s current rules say it counts. Policies vary, so the checkbox is optional by design.

Does APS guarantee admission?

No. Confirm minimum points, subject requirements, capacity, and selection rules with the institution.

Comparing South African programme options

When researching a South African degree, write down the APS minimum beside the subject-specific requirements. A programme may publish a general APS number but still require a particular level in Mathematics, English, or a science subject. Some faculties also consider a higher score for selection when demand is strong. Comparing those details side by side helps you distinguish between meeting a minimum and being a competitive applicant.

Use the calculator with final marks when possible. Predicted marks can be helpful for revision planning, but they should be clearly labelled as scenarios. Check whether the institution uses the best six subjects, excludes Life Orientation, or applies its own conversion. Keep a copy of the prospectus or admissions page with the date you checked it, because requirements and deadlines can change between application cycles.

If you are applying to more than one institution, calculate the six-subject result first and then review each university’s separate APS policy. This prevents one institution’s formula from being assumed to apply everywhere.
