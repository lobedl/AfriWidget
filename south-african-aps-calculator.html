What the APS actually is

Every South African university that admits undergraduates starts the same way: it converts your Matric percentages into a single number and uses that number to decide whether you have a chance. That number is the Admission Point Score, and it is what sits behind the cut-off figures you see in every prospectus.

The logic is simple. Each subject percentage on your National Senior Certificate is mapped to one of seven achievement levels, and each level carries the same number of points as its name. A subject where you scored 82 percent becomes Level 7 and gives 7 points. A subject where you scored 55 percent becomes Level 4 and gives 4 points. Add up the levels of your best six subjects and you have your APS, out of a maximum of 42.

What makes the APS tricky is not the arithmetic. It is that different universities treat certain subjects differently, and some require specific grades in specific subjects as a gate on top of the score. That is why two candidates with the same APS can have very different outcomes at the same university, and why you cannot just look at your number and decide where you can apply.

The seven-level NSC scale

The Department of Basic Education publishes the scale on every Matric certificate. It is the same for every subject, every year, and every school in the country.

LevelPercentageAPS pointsDescription
780 to 1007Outstanding achievement
670 to 796Meritorious achievement
560 to 695Substantial achievement
450 to 594Moderate achievement
340 to 493Adequate achievement
230 to 392Elementary achievement
10 to 290Not achieved

Notice that Level 1 gives no points. A failed subject does not help your APS at all, which is why the standard advice is that if you need to fail something, fail it outside the six subjects that will be counted. Also notice the sharp jump at each boundary. Going from 79 to 80 percent moves you from Level 6 to Level 7, which is a full extra point on your APS. That single percentage point can move you over a cut-off.

How the six-subject APS is built

The standard calculation takes your six best subjects and adds their levels together. Six subjects at Level 7 gives the maximum of 42 points. Six subjects at Level 4 gives 24. Anything in between is possible.

Which six subjects count depends on what you took and what the university requires. In the general case, the six are your two languages, Mathematics or Mathematical Literacy, and your three electives. But if the university you are applying to requires Mathematics and Physical Sciences for your chosen programme, and you took both, then those two are locked into the six whether they are your best scores or not.

That is why the number this calculator gives you is a maximum. It takes the six subjects you entered, converts each one to its level, and adds the levels. If any of those subjects is one that your chosen university does not count, or if any is one that the university requires a higher grade in than you achieved, then the figure you see here will not match what the university actually uses.

The Life Orientation question

Almost every South African university handles Life Orientation differently, and this is the single biggest source of confusion about the APS. There are three broad approaches.

Excluded from the APS

Universities such as Wits, UCT, Stellenbosch, and the University of Pretoria calculate your APS on six academic subjects only. Life Orientation does not contribute points. That is because those institutions want to compare applicants on subjects that reflect academic difficulty, and Life Orientation is widely regarded as less demanding than the others. Some of these universities still require Life Orientation at a minimum level (often Level 4 or 5) as a prerequisite, but they do not count it towards the score.

Included as a seventh subject

Universities such as the University of Johannesburg and several universities of technology count Life Orientation as a full seventh subject. Your APS is then calculated out of 49 instead of 42, with Life Orientation contributing up to seven additional points. A strong LO mark is genuinely useful in these calculations, so check whether it applies at the institution you are targeting.

Half weight

Some universities give Life Orientation half weight. UKZN, UFS, and a handful of others award a maximum of three or four points for a Level 7 in Life Orientation, recognising the subject without giving it the same weight as an academic subject. This gives a maximum APS somewhere between 42 and 49 depending on the exact rule.

How to use the toggle. Leave the checkbox off if you are applying to a university that excludes Life Orientation from the APS, which is most of the traditional research universities. Turn it on if you are applying to one that counts it, such as UJ or a university of technology. When in doubt, calculate both figures and check which one matches the cut-off published in the prospectus you are looking at.

Because UP excludes Life Orientation, the APS is 6 + 5 + 7 + 7 + 6 + 5 = 36 out of 42. That clears the typical BCom entry requirement at UP, which in recent years has sat around 34 with Mathematics at Level 6. It is also comfortably above the minimum for most other commerce programmes at the same university.

Now suppose the same candidate applies to UJ, which includes Life Orientation. The APS becomes 36 + 6 = 42 out of 49. Different denominator, but the same underlying performance. That is why it is essential to know which scale the university is using before comparing your number against a published cut-off.

What your APS opens up

The bands below are indicative ranges, not official cut-offs. Universities set their own minimums each year and they move slightly with competition. What the bands do is give you a rough sense of where you stand before you check the specific figure for your programme.

APS (out of 42)Programmes typically within reach
38 to 42Medicine (MBChB), Actuarial Science, and the most selective engineering streams at top universities. NBT scores are usually also required.
34 to 37Most engineering degrees, Computer Science, BCom, Law (LLB) at competitive universities.
30 to 33Science degrees, education, social work, humanities at research universities.
26 to 29General BA and BSc streams, education, and many BCom programmes at less selective universities.
20 to 25Diploma programmes at universities of technology, or extended degree programmes.
15 to 19Higher certificate programmes, which often serve as a bridging route into a diploma or degree.

Two things to keep in mind. First, meeting the cut-off is necessary but not sufficient. Selective programmes rank applicants by APS after the cut-off, so the actual entry point for medicine at a top university is usually higher than the published minimum. Second, some programmes require an additional gate such as a National Benchmark Test, a portfolio, an interview, or a specific subject grade that sits above the general minimum. Those are described in the individual prospectus entry for each programme.

Subject requirements that sit on top of the APS

Even if your APS is comfortably above the cut-off, you can be turned away for missing a subject requirement. The common ones are worth knowing.

  • Pure Mathematics vs Mathematical Literacy. Degrees in engineering, medicine, actuarial science, the physical sciences, and most of commerce require Pure Mathematics, usually at Level 6 or higher. Mathematical Literacy does earn you APS points, but it will not satisfy the subject requirement for those programmes. It is fine for humanities, education, law, and some commerce streams.
  • Physical Sciences. Required for engineering, the health sciences, and most BSc programmes. Usually needs to be at Level 5 or 6.
  • Home Language. Most programmes require a pass at a specified level in your home language. Some require a specific grade for Law or journalism.
  • Life Orientation. Even universities that exclude LO from the APS often require it at a minimum level (usually 4 or 5) as a condition of admission. A fail in LO can block an otherwise strong application.
  • Programme-specific subjects. Medicine typically requires Mathematics, Physical Sciences, and Life Sciences at strong grades. Accounting requires Accounting or Mathematics at Level 6 or higher. Architecture often requires Mathematics and sometimes a portfolio. Check the prospectus entry for your exact programme.

If you are missing a required subject, there is often a workaround. Some universities allow you to take the required subject in an extended or bridging programme, or to substitute a related subject if your APS is strong enough. Others simply will not consider your application. The only way to know is to ask the admissions office directly.

Common mistakes when self-calculating

A few errors show up again and again when candidates try to work out their own APS.

Using the wrong subjects. The APS counts your best six subjects, but if a programme requires specific subjects, those are locked into the calculation whether they are your best scores or not. A candidate with a strong commercial subject combination applying for engineering might find that their actual APS is lower than the number they calculated, because Mathematics and Physical Sciences replace two of their best commercial subjects.

Forgetting the endorsement. Before any APS is considered, you need the correct Matric endorsement for the qualification level you are applying to. A Bachelor's Degree Pass requires at least 50 percent in four designated subjects plus 40 percent in your home language. A Diploma Pass requires 40 percent in four subjects. A Higher Certificate Pass requires 40 percent in your home language and 30 percent in the others. If you do not have the right endorsement, the APS is academic.

Mixing scales between universities. A candidate with an APS of 36 out of 42 might see a cut-off of 42 in a UJ prospectus and assume they are far off. But if the UJ cut-off is out of 49 and their equivalent 7-subject APS is 42, they are right at the line. Always check which scale the published number is using.

Ignoring the difference between 79 and 80 percent. A single percentage point at the boundary between Level 6 and Level 7 is worth a full APS point. If you are sitting at 79 percent in a subject, the value of revising to cross 80 is much higher than the value of pushing a 75 to a 76. Prioritise the boundary crossings.

Assuming the calculation is the whole decision. The APS determines whether you are eligible. It does not determine whether you are admitted. Selective programmes rank eligible applicants by APS, and some also require an NBT, an interview, or a portfolio. A strong APS makes you competitive but does not guarantee a place.

Where this calculator stops

This tool applies the standard NSC seven-level scale and produces both the 42-point and the 49-point APS. That covers the two most common patterns in South African university admissions.

It does not know which six subjects your specific programme will count. If the programme requires Mathematics and Physical Sciences, and you entered your six best subjects without those, the figure above will be higher than what the university will actually calculate. The same applies to any programme with subject-specific rules.

It does not know the half-weight rules that UKZN, UFS, and a few others use for Life Orientation. Those universities calculate an APS somewhere between 42 and 49 depending on the exact rule, and you would need to reduce your LO contribution manually to match.

It does not account for the National Benchmark Test, the endorsement requirement, or programme-specific gates such as portfolios and interviews. Those sit alongside the APS, not inside it.

What it does give you is a clean starting figure you can take to the admissions office or the prospectus. Use it to understand where you stand, to compare programmes, and to prioritise your final Matric revision. The official calculation made by the university is the one that decides.

Frequently asked questions

What is an APS score in South Africa?

APS stands for Admission Point Score. It is a single number that South African universities use to compare Matric results across applicants. Each subject percentage is converted to a level between 1 and 7, and the levels of your six best subjects are added together. The maximum on this standard scale is 42 points, or 49 if Life Orientation is included.

Why do some universities count Life Orientation and others do not?

Each university sets its own admissions policy. Institutions such as Wits, UCT, Stellenbosch, and the University of Pretoria generally exclude Life Orientation from the APS calculation because they want to compare applicants on academic subjects only. Others, such as the University of Johannesburg and several universities of technology, include it as a full seventh subject. A third group, including UKZN and UFS, gives Life Orientation half weight. Always check the policy for the specific institution and programme you are applying to.

What is a good APS score for university admission in South Africa?

It depends entirely on the programme. Medicine and actuarial science usually need the mid-thirties to low forties out of 42. Engineering sits in the mid-thirties. Commerce and law are usually in the low thirties. Humanities and education are often in the high twenties. Diplomas at universities of technology tend to need around 20 to 26. The only figure that matters for your specific application is the cut-off the university publishes for that programme that year.

Can I use Mathematical Literacy for an engineering or science degree?

No. Even though Mathematical Literacy earns you APS points on this calculator, professional degrees in engineering, medicine, actuarial science, and the physical sciences require Pure Mathematics, usually at Level 6 (70 percent) or higher. Mathematical Literacy is generally acceptable for humanities, education, law, and some commerce streams. Check the specific programme's subject requirements before you apply.

Does my Matric certificate need a specific endorsement to apply?

Yes. Before any university looks at your APS, you must have earned the correct pass endorsement on your certificate. A Bachelor's Degree Pass requires at least 50 percent in four designated subjects plus 40 percent in your home language. A Diploma Pass requires 40 percent in four subjects. A Higher Certificate Pass requires 40 percent in your home language and 30 percent in the others. The APS is calculated on top of this endorsement, not instead of it.

Does the APS calculation differ between IEB and DBE results?

No. Both the Department of Basic Education (public schools) and the Independent Examinations Board (private schools) use the same seven-level NSC achievement scale. Universities South Africa treats IEB and DBE percentages equivalently for APS purposes, so the calculation is identical whichever route you took.

What happens if I miss the APS cut-off by one or two points?

You have a few options. You can apply to a different programme at the same university, apply to a different institution, enrol in a diploma and later transfer to a degree if your diploma marks are strong, or take a gap year and improve your Matric results through a rewrite. Some universities also offer extended or augmented programmes for candidates just below the cut-off. Speak to the admissions office directly about which of these routes applies to you.

Do National Benchmark Tests (NBTs) affect the APS?

No. The NBT is a separate test set by a national agency, and its results are used alongside the APS for certain programmes, particularly at UCT, Wits, Stellenbosch, and some health sciences courses. A strong NBT score cannot compensate for an APS below the minimum, and a weak NBT score can reduce your ranking within a pool of applicants who all meet the cut-off.
