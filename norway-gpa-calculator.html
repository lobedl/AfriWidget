What this calculator measures

This page does two jobs in one place. The first mode computes your university grade point average on the Norwegian 0.0 to 5.0 scale using the ECTS credits attached to each course. The second mode audits your upper secondary competition score for the Samordna opptak admissions service, which is the centralised application portal used by most Norwegian universities and university colleges.

The two jobs are separate on purpose. A university student who is already enrolled and thinking about a Master's degree needs the ECTS-weighted GPA and its mapping to the US 4.0 scale. A secondary school graduate applying for a bachelor's degree needs the Samordna points, which follow a different formula that adds bonus points for advanced science courses, age, and service.

Everything runs in your browser. No course code, ECTS credit, grade, or bonus value is sent anywhere. The only part of the page that reaches the network is the AI insight button, and it only fires when you press it.

The Norwegian A to F scale

Norwegian universities and university colleges grade on a national A to F scale set out in the Regulations on Studies and Examinations at Universities and University Colleges. The scale is criteria-based, meaning your grade reflects how well you met the learning outcomes, not how you ranked against your classmates. This differs from countries that use a strict statistical curve.

Each letter carries a numeric point equivalent that feeds directly into the GPA calculation. The table below shows the values and their official descriptors.

A handful of courses are graded Bestått or Ikke bestått, which means Pass or Fail. These courses count towards your total completed ECTS credits and are required for the degree, but they are excluded from the numeric GPA. You will see them flagged as Pass in the calculator so that they appear in the total credit count without dragging or lifting the average.

The ECTS-weighted GPA formula

Every higher education course in Norway carries an ECTS credit value. A single semester is normally 30 ECTS, and a full three-year bachelor's degree is 180 ECTS. Courses come in 5, 7.5, 10, 15, or 30 ECTS blocks depending on the programme.

The GPA is a weighted average, not a plain average of your grades. That means a 30 ECTS course moves the number far more than a 7.5 ECTS elective. The formula is:

Weighted GPA = the sum of (grade point × ECTS credits) divided by the sum of ECTS credits for graded courses only.

Pass or Fail courses are excluded from both the numerator and the denominator. This matters because a student with a heavy load of Pass/Fail practical modules can end up with a GPA that reflects only a small share of their total credits. When you read the output, look at the graded ECTS count as well as the GPA itself, because a GPA built on 60 graded ECTS carries more weight than one built on 15.

Converting to the US 4.0 scale

Norwegian universities report grades on the 0.0 to 5.0 scale, but many international graduate programs ask for a US 4.0 GPA. Credential evaluation services such as WES and AACRAO use a band mapping between the two scales rather than a single mathematical formula. The mapping below follows the general pattern used by these services.

Samordna opptak quotas and points

Samordna opptak is the shared application portal for most Norwegian universities and university colleges. Admission is decided by competition points, and the points are calculated differently depending on which quota an applicant competes in.

The First-Time Diploma Quota, known in Norwegian as Kvote for førstegangsvitnemål, reserves half of the study places for applicants who are 21 years old or younger in the application year and who completed upper secondary education within the standard three years without retaking any subject. The score in this quota is called Skolepoeng and it is built only from your original VGS grades plus science and language bonus points plus any gender bonus that applies.

The Ordinary Quota, known as Ordinær kvote, is open to everyone. It adds age points, military or folkehøgskole points, and any improved grades that come from retaking subjects as a private candidate. The final score in this quota is called Konkurransepoeng.

Science and language bonus points, called realfags- og språkpoeng, follow a national list. Mathematics R2 gives 1.0 point, Mathematics R1 or S2 gives 0.5 each, Physics 1 and Physics 2 give 0.5 each, Chemistry 1 and Chemistry 2 give 0.5 each, Biology 2, Information Technology 2, and Geosciences 2 give 0.5 each, and a foreign language at level III gives 1.0 point. The statutory combined cap on these bonus points is 4.0.

Age points, aldrespoeng, add 2 points for each calendar year from the year you turn 20, up to a maximum of 8 points. Military service, civilian service, a full year at a folk high school, or 60 ECTS of completed higher education adds another 2 points, and the total from these service routes is also capped. Gender bonus points, kjønnspoeng, are awarded at some programmes to encourage balanced intake, most commonly in engineering and veterinary studies, and they are set per programme rather than per applicant.

A worked university example

Take a third-year Bachelor of Science student at NTNU in Trondheim who is finishing an autumn semester of 30 ECTS credits.

Object-oriented programming is 7.5 ECTS and scored 78 percent, which is an A at 5.0 points, giving 37.5 quality points. Linear algebra is 7.5 ECTS and scored 66 percent, a B at 4.0, giving 30.0 quality points. Algorithms and data structures is 7.5 ECTS and scored 64 percent, a B at 4.0, giving 30.0 quality points. Software engineering is 7.5 ECTS and scored 82 percent, an A at 5.0, giving 37.5 quality points. A health and safety module, HMS0002, is graded Pass or Fail and carries no numeric weight.

Total graded ECTS are 30. Total quality points are 135. The weighted GPA is 135 divided by 30, which is 4.50. That lands on the A band. The US 4.0 equivalent is a clean 4.00. The Pass or Fail module does not appear in either side of the fraction, but it does count towards the 30 ECTS the student needs for the semester.

Now add the Examen Philosophicum, a 7.5 ECTS course that most Norwegian universities require. If the student earns a C at 3.0 points, the quality points added are 22.5 and the new GPA across 37.5 graded ECTS becomes 4.20. The single C pulls the average below the A band. This is the kind of arithmetic the tool exists to make visible before the semester is set in stone.

A worked Samordna example

Take a 22-year-old applicant who has just finished their upper secondary education and is applying for Medicine at the University of Bergen. Their VGS average across 23 subjects is 5.40. They completed Mathematics R1 and R2, Physics 1, Chemistry 1 and 2, giving them 3.0 realfag points. They have spent one year in the military.

Karakterpoeng is 5.40 multiplied by 10, which is 54.00. Realfag points add 3.00, so Skolepoeng is 57.00. That is the First-Time Diploma score, but the applicant is 22, so this score is recorded for reference only and cannot be used to compete in the Førstegangsvitnemål quota.

For the Ordinary Quota, age points add 6.00 because the applicant is in the year they turn 22. Military service adds 2.00. The total Konkurransepoeng is 54.00 plus 3.00 plus 6.00 plus 2.00, which is 65.00. On the historical cutoffs, that score sits comfortably above the typical Medicine intake at UiB, and the applicant is well positioned for admission.

The contrast between the two scores is the point. A student who takes a year off between upper secondary and university loses the First-Time Diploma quota but gains a substantial number of Ordinary Quota points. The tool lets you model both sides before you decide whether to take a gap year or apply immediately.

Common mistakes

Weighting grades by headcount instead of ECTS. A plain average of letter grades produces a number that does not match what the university records. Always weight by ECTS credits, and exclude Pass or Fail courses from the calculation.

Assuming the First-Time Diploma quota is available after age 21. The quota locks to applicants who are 21 or younger in the year they apply. If you cross that line, your only route is the Ordinary Quota, even if you have never retaken a subject.

Forgetting to claim realfag points. Many applicants do not realise that their advanced mathematics and physics count towards a bonus that can lift the score by up to 4.0 points. Check your vitnemål against the national list before submitting.

Retaking subjects to improve the First-Time Diploma score. This does not work. Retakes count only in the Ordinary Quota. If you are determined to raise your score by retaking, plan for the Ordinary Quota route and factor in the age points you will gain.

Ignoring the 60 ECTS service bonus. A single year of higher education at 60 ECTS, a completed year of military service, and a full year at a folk high school all give the same 2.0 point bonus. Two of these do not stack into four points; the cap is 2.0 for the whole service category.

Limits of the estimate

This tool reflects the current national standards as published by UHR and Samordna opptak. It cannot see your official vitnemål or your Studentweb transcript, and it cannot know if your faculty applies a special rule for a particular programme. A few Master's programmes use a weighted average across only the specialisation courses from the last two years of your bachelor's degree, rather than all courses from all years, and that calculation can differ from the one you see here.

Samordna opptak updates its cutoffs each year with each admission round. The historical cutoffs mentioned on this page are for context, not for guarantees. A score that cleared the cutoff last year can miss it this year, and a score that missed it can clear it in the supplementary round. Track the current numbers on the Samordna opptak portal before you commit to a strategy.

Finally, the tool does not replace the official transcript. It is a planning aid, and it works best when you use it side by side with the actual documents, in a quiet moment before applications open, so that any correction can still be made in time.

Frequently asked questions

What Norwegian GPA is required for a Master's degree?

Most Norwegian public universities set a minimum weighted GPA of C, which is 3.0 on the national 5.0 scale, for admission into two-year Master programs. Competitive programs at NHH, NTNU, and UiO typically ask for a B average at 4.0 or better, and some research tracks require the A band at 4.5 and above.

How does the Norwegian 0–5 GPA map to ECTS letter grades?

A weighted GPA of 4.50 to 5.00 maps to ECTS grade A, 3.50 to 4.49 to ECTS B, 2.50 to 3.49 to ECTS C, 1.50 to 2.49 to ECTS D, and 1.00 to 1.49 to ECTS E. Anything below 1.00 is a fail. Norwegian grades are criteria-referenced, so the ECTS band is a broad label, not a fixed statistical curve.

Are Pass or Fail courses counted in the Norwegian GPA?

No. Courses graded Bestått or Ikke bestått count toward your total completed ECTS credits and toward degree completion, but they are excluded from the GPA formula. Only courses with letter grades A through F contribute to the weighted GPA.

What is the difference between Skolepoeng and Konkurransepoeng?

Skolepoeng is the First-Time Diploma score, used only in the Kvote for førstegangsvitnemål, which reserves half of study places for applicants who are 21 or younger and completed upper secondary in three years. Konkurransepoeng is the Ordinary Quota score, which adds age points, military or folk high school points, and any improved grades from privatist retakes.

How many bonus points does Realfag give?

Advanced mathematics R2 gives 1.0 point, R1 or S2 gives 0.5 points each. Physics 1 and 2 give 0.5 points each. Chemistry 1 and 2 give 0.5 points each. Biology 2, Information Technology 2, and Geosciences 2 give 0.5 points each. Advanced foreign language level III gives 1.0 point. The statutory combined cap on science and language points is 4.0.

Can retaken privatist exams improve my First-Time Diploma score?

No. If you retake upper secondary subjects as a private candidate after graduating, your improved grades only count in the Ordinary Quota. Your First-Time Diploma score remains locked to the grades on your original graduation diploma.

Does Examen Philosophicum count toward my GPA?

It depends on the institution. At most faculties, if Exphil is graded A through F on your Studentweb transcript, its ECTS credits are fully weighted into your GPA. If it is offered as Bestått or Ikke bestått, the credits count toward your degree but do not affect the numeric GPA.

Does the calculator store my grades?

No. Every calculation runs in your browser. No course code, ECTS credit, or grade is sent anywhere, and nothing is stored. The only part of the page that reaches the network is the AI insight button, and only when you press it.
