What the UCAS Tariff actually is and how Size and Grade Bands work

The UCAS Tariff is a numerical translation scale managed by the Universities and Colleges Admissions Service (UCAS) to convert regulated Level 3 post-16 qualifications into a single comparable score. Instead of writing separate admissions thresholds for dozens of academic, applied, and technical certificates, a UK university department that wants roughly the attainment of an A and two Bs at A-Level can publish an entry requirement of 128 UCAS Tariff points. That single benchmark allows an admissions tutor to evaluate a three A-Level sixth-former, a BTEC National Extended Diploma college student, a T-Level technical learner, and an Access to Higher Education adult returner on a unified arithmetic scale.

The numbers inside the Tariff tables are not arbitrary integers chosen by committee guesswork. Every regulated qualification admitted onto the post-2017 UCAS Tariff framework is scored by multiplying two structural components:

  • Size Band (1 to 4): Assigned from the qualification's notional study hours or Guided Learning Hours (GLH). Small ancillary awards under 100 hours sit in Size Band 1; half-size awards such as the Extended Project Qualification (120 GLH) and decoupled AS-Levels (180 GLH) sit in Size Band 2; intermediate certificates sit in Size Band 3; and a full two-year GCE A-Level or BTEC Extended Certificate (360 GLH) sits in Size Band 4. Double awards (720 GLH) and triple awards such as a BTEC National Extended Diploma (1,080 GLH) are treated as two or three Size Band 4 blocks respectively.
  • Grade Band (3 to 14): Assigned by mapping the academic demand of each passing grade across awarding bodies. At A-Level, grade E sits at Grade Band 4, D at 6, C at 8, B at 10, A at 12, and A* at the maximum Grade Band 14.

Multiplying Size Band by Grade Band produces the exact figures you see on university prospectuses. A full A-Level (Size Band 4) at grade A* (Grade Band 14) yields 4 multiplied by 14, or 56 points; grade A (Grade Band 12) yields 4 multiplied by 12, or 48 points; and each letter drop removes 8 points. An Extended Project Qualification (Size Band 2) at grade A* yields 2 multiplied by 14, or 28 points—exactly half an A-Level. One deliberate exception in the modern framework is the standalone AS-Level: when AS examinations were decoupled from full A-Levels in England, UCAS calibrated AS-Levels at 40 per cent of an A-Level rather than 50 per cent to reflect first-year syllabus depth, giving an AS grade A 20 points rather than 24.

Crucially, the Tariff is an administrative currency rather than a statement that two qualifications teach identical content. A BTEC National Extended Diploma at DDM carries the exact same 128 points as A-Level grades ABB (whereas DMM carries 112 points, matching BBC), yet admissions tutors know that a continuous-assessment vocational diploma develops practical studio or laboratory fluency while linear A-Levels test unseen terminal examination synthesis. Many undergraduate degrees welcome both profiles; heavily mathematical or clinical degrees insist on specific syllabus content regardless of how high your point total climbs.

How totals are added up: non-duplication and qualification caps

In its pure mathematical form, the UCAS Tariff is additive: each distinct Level 3 qualification you complete contributes its point value to a running sum, with no averaging and no automatic ceiling. However, university admissions offices enforce three operational rules that determine how much of your raw sum actually counts toward clearing an offer.

1. The subject non-duplication (subsuming) rule

Whenever you complete two qualifications at progressive levels in the exact same subject, the higher-level award subsumes the lower-level award. You never add both together:

  • AS-Level followed by full A-Level: Sitting AS Chemistry in Year 12 (grade A, 20 points) and continuing to full A-Level Chemistry in Year 13 (grade B, 40 points) gives you 40 points for Chemistry, not 60. Only if you drop a fourth subject after sitting the standalone AS exam at the end of Year 12 can you keep the AS points alongside your three unrelated full A-Levels.
  • Scottish Higher followed by Advanced Higher: Progressing from S5 Higher Mathematics (grade A, 33 points) to S6 Advanced Higher Mathematics (grade B, 48 points) means only the 48-point Advanced Higher counts in non-duplicated Tariff calculations, because the S6 curriculum builds directly on the S5 syllabus.
  • Graded music, dance, and drama exams: Passing ABRSM Grade 6 Piano, Grade 7 Piano, and Grade 8 Piano Distinction gives you 30 points from Grade 8 alone—not 58 points from adding all three certificates. By contrast, Grade 8 Piano Distinction (30 points) and Grade 7 Violin Merit (12 points) are separate instruments, so both remain distinct on the Tariff.

2. Institutional qualification-count caps

Read the exact phrasing of every conditional offer on UCAS Hub. There is a major difference between an offer worded "120 UCAS Tariff points" and one worded "120 UCAS Tariff points from a maximum of three Level 3 qualifications" (or "from three A-Levels or equivalent, excluding General Studies and Critical Thinking"). Under the uncapped offer, an applicant holding BCC at A-Level (104 points) plus an EPQ at grade B (20 points) reaches 124 points and meets the condition. Under the three-qualification cap, the admissions computer sums only the three full A-Levels, stops at 104 points, and flags the applicant as 16 points short unless an admissions tutor exercises discretion.

3. The Level 2 GCSE floor underneath every Tariff offer

Why GCSEs and IGCSEs carry zero UCAS points yet still block offers: GCSEs, International GCSEs, and Scottish National 5s are Level 2 qualifications and contribute 0 points to your Tariff total. Despite carrying zero points, almost every UK university imposes a compulsory Level 2 gatekeeper—minimum grade 4 (grade C) in English Language and Mathematics for general degrees, rising to grade 6 or 7 in Mathematics for Economics, Computer Science, Finance, and Psychology, and five or more grades at 7 to 9 for Medicine and Dentistry. Always verify the GCSE tab on UCAS Course Search before assuming a high Level 3 Tariff total guarantees an offer.

Five worked student profiles across qualification routes

Working through concrete candidate portfolios shows how raw Tariff arithmetic interacts with real university entry rules across different post-16 curricula.

Profile 1: Standard three A-Level STEM and Economics candidate

A student completes three linear A-Levels: A in Mathematics (48), B in Physics (40), and B in Economics (40). Adding the three Size Band 4 awards gives 48 + 40 + 40 = 128 UCAS points. In UK higher education, 128 points (ABB) sits right on the boundary between selective research universities (such as Leeds, Sheffield, Southampton, Nottingham, and Liverpool for many engineering, finance, and social science programmes) and upper-mid comprehensive institutions. Because Mathematics is held at grade A, this student also satisfies quantitative subject prerequisites that a 128-point candidate with A in Sociology and BB in Humanities would miss.

Profile 2: Hybrid academic, applied science, and EPQ portfolio

A sixth-former combines A-Level Biology at A (48), A-Level Chemistry at A (48), a BTEC Level 3 National Extended Certificate in Applied Human Biology at Distinction (48), and an EPQ on antibiotic resistance at grade A (24). The full uncapped total is 48 + 48 + 48 + 24 = 168 UCAS points, numerically matching three A* grades. Even if a selective Pharmacology or Biomedical Science department caps its main calculation at three 360-GLH qualifications, the core trio still delivers 144 points (AAA equivalent), while the grade A EPQ frequently activates an alternative dual offer (such as ABB instead of AAB).

Profile 3: BTEC National Extended Diploma computing specialist

A further-education college student completes the 1,080-GLH BTEC Level 3 National Extended Diploma in Computing with grades DDM, earning 128 UCAS points. Notice how steep the step sizes are on BTEC Extended Diplomas below DDD: dropping a single overall grade step from DDM to DMM cuts the score by 16 points to 112 points, whereas moving from DDD to D*DD changes the score by only 8 points (144 to 152). For BTEC students, crossing the internal unit-point threshold from DMM into DDM is often worth two full A-Level grade boundaries in university eligibility.

Profile 4: Full International Baccalaureate (IB) Diploma candidate

An IB Diploma student achieves 35 IB points out of 45, distributed as follows:

  • Higher Level (HL): History Grade 6 (48), Economics Grade 6 (48), English A Literature Grade 5 (32) = 128 points.
  • Standard Level (SL): Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches Grade 6 (24), Biology Grade 5 (16), French B Grade 5 (16) = 56 points.
  • IB Core: Extended Essay Grade B (10) and Theory of Knowledge Grade C (8) = 18 points.

Summing every component yields 128 + 56 + 18 = 202 UCAS Tariff points. New IB applicants are often surprised to see 202 points—well above the 168-point ceiling of three A* grades at A-Level—from a solid but non-maximum 35-point IB Diploma. That structural inflation happens because IB candidates study six subjects plus two core elements rather than three subjects. UK admissions teams know this arithmetic well: Selective universities therefore express IB offers directly in IB Diploma points (typically 32 to 38 out of 45, specifying 6,6,5 or 6,6,6 at Higher Level) rather than allowing an IB student with modest Higher Level grades to pass a 128-point Tariff filter on volume alone.

Profile 5: Scottish SQA Highers and Advanced Highers candidate

A student in Scotland sits five Highers in S5—Mathematics A (33), Chemistry A (33), Physics B (27), English B (27), and History B (27)—for an S5 total of 147 points. Staying on for S6, the student completes Advanced Higher Mathematics at B (48), Advanced Higher Chemistry at B (48), and a fresh Higher in Modern Studies at B (27). Under the UCAS non-duplication rule, the two S6 Advanced Highers replace the earlier S5 Highers in Mathematics and Chemistry rather than stacking on top of them. The accurate non-duplicated Tariff score is 48 + 48 + 27 + 27 + 27 + 27 = 204 points (not 270).

Tariff offers versus grade offers: how UK universities screen

Before using your Tariff score to pick your five UCAS choices, you need to distinguish between institutions that admit by letter grades and institutions that admit by Tariff points. Confusing the two is one of the costliest errors in UCAS strategy.

UK higher education providers broadly fall into three operational groups:

  1. Strict letter-grade selectors (Zero Tariff conversion): Oxford, Cambridge, Imperial College London, LSE, UCL, St Andrews, Durham, Warwick, Bristol, Bath, and Edinburgh (for non-Scottish entries) express standard offers exclusively in grades such as A*A*A, A*AA, or AAA. If an offer specifies AAA (which equals 144 points on paper), presenting A*AB (also 144 points) or ABB plus an A in EPQ (152 points) does not automatically satisfy the contract, because the grade B breaches the minimum A-grade floor.
  2. Hybrid selectors (Grades for A-Levels, Tariff or mixed rules for applied routes and Clearing): Universities such as Manchester, Leeds, Nottingham, Sheffield, Birmingham, Southampton, Newcastle, Liverpool, Queen Mary, Cardiff, and Lancaster publish most standard entries as AAB or ABB, yet several faculties—particularly Nursing, Healthcare Sciences, Environmental Science, Construction Management, Media, and Education—accept combined A-Level and BTEC profiles or switch to Tariff benchmarks during August Clearing.
  3. Tariff-primary selectors and Degree Apprenticeships: Modern and comprehensive universities—including Manchester Metropolitan, Nottingham Trent, Oxford Brookes, Coventry, Portsmouth, Swansea, UWE Bristol, Northumbria, Aston, and De Montfort—alongside major corporate Degree Apprenticeship employers (engineering, quantity surveying, digital technology, and chartered management schemes) routinely express entry requirements directly as 96 to 136 UCAS Tariff points.

Subject prerequisite traps that override Tariff points

Equivalence in Tariff points never overrides syllabus prerequisites. Four degree families routinely reject high-Tariff applicants who lack specific subject coverage:

  • Engineering, Physics, and Quantitative Economics: Almost all accredited MEng/BEng Mechanical, Aerospace, Electrical, and Civil Engineering courses at research-intensive universities require A-Level Mathematics (and usually Physics). A student holding 168 points from BTEC Extended Diploma Engineering D*D*D* without A-Level Mathematics is often required either to sit A-Level Mathematics alongside the BTEC or enter via a Foundation Engineering year, because university first-year vector calculus assumes full A2 pure mathematics fluency.
  • Medicine, Dentistry, and Veterinary Science: UK medical schools rarely use Tariff points at all; they mandate A-Level (or IB Higher Level) Chemistry and Biology plus UCAT admissions test percentiles and Multiple Mini Interviews (MMIs), typically excluding BTEC Applied Science as a standalone substitute.
  • Computer Science and Data Science: Top-20 departments normally demand A-Level Mathematics at grade A or A*, whereas mid-tariff universities (112 to 128 points) frequently welcome BTEC Extended Diplomas in Computing or IT, T-Levels in Digital Production, Design and Development, or any three A-Levels without Mathematics.
  • Nursing, Midwifery, Physiotherapy, and Allied Health: Regulated NHS clinical degrees enthusiastically use UCAS Tariff offers (typically 104 to 128 points) and welcome BTEC Health and Social Care, T-Level Health, and Access to HE (Health Professions) Diplomas, provided you meet biological science credit minimums and occupational health/DBS checks.

International and African qualifications in UCAS admissions

Thousands of applicants from Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, South Africa, Uganda, Zimbabwe, Tanzania, and international schools worldwide apply to UK undergraduate degrees through UCAS each cycle. Whether your school-leaving results convert into UCAS Tariff points depends on which examination board issued your certificate.

Lane 1: International qualifications that carry full UCAS Tariff points

If you studied at an international school or private sixth-form college offering Cambridge Assessment International Education (CAIE) AS and A-Levels, Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Levels (IAL), OxfordAQA International A-Levels, or the International Baccalaureate (IB) Diploma, your grades carry the exact same official UCAS Tariff points as domestic UK awards. An A* in CAIE Physics in Accra, Lagos, Nairobi, or Harare is worth 56 UCAS points—no downward adjustment or credential conversion fee applies. Zimbabwe's ZIMSEC Advanced Level is also widely benchmarked alongside GCE A-Level grades for direct Year 1 UK entry by international admissions teams.

Lane 2: Direct Year 1 entry without UCAS Tariff conversion (South Africa, Uganda, Tanzania)

Several 12-to-13-year national curricula sit outside the numerical UCAS Tariff tables yet are accepted by UK universities for direct Year 1 bachelor's entry using country-specific equivalency schedules:

  • South African National Senior Certificate (NSC) and IEB Matric: Most UK universities accept the NSC/IEB with a Bachelor's Degree endorsement directly into Year 1 (excluding Life Orientation from academic subject counts). Rather than converting Matric achievement levels 1–7 into UCAS Tariff points, international offices map five relevant subjects directly to A-Level bands—for instance, requiring 75555 to 76666 (70% to 80%+ across five subjects) where a domestic applicant needs ABB (128 points), or 77766 where AAA (144 points) is required.
  • Ugandan UACE and Tanzanian ACSEE: Because East African Advanced Certificates follow a two-year post-O-Level sixth-form structure modelled historically on GCE A-Levels, many UK universities consider strong Principal Pass profiles (such as AAB to BBB across three Principal subjects) for direct Year 1 admission via institutional country pages, even though UNEB and NECTA awards are not listed inside the automated UCAS Tariff calculator.

Lane 3: Secondary certificates requiring an International Foundation Year (WASSCE, NECO, KCSE)

West African WASSCE and NECO Senior School Certificate Examinations (12 years of schooling) and the Kenyan KCSE are evaluated by UK ENIC (formerly UK NARIC) as comparable in academic tier to GCSE/IGCSE/AS transition level rather than full Level 3 GCE A-Levels. Consequently, WASSCE and KCSE grades carry zero UCAS Tariff points and generally do not grant direct entry into Year 1 of a three-year English bachelor's degree. Instead, students follow one of two proven routes:

  1. One-Year International Foundation Programme (IFP): Offered on campus by almost every UK university (requiring typically 5 WASSCE credits at A1 to C6 including English and Mathematics, or a KCSE mean grade of C+ to B-). Completing the IFP progresses you straight into Year 1 of the bachelor's degree.
  2. Four-Year Scottish Undergraduate Degree: Because Scottish honours degrees run for four years rather than three, universities in Scotland (such as Aberdeen, Strathclyde, Dundee, and Stirling) sometimes admit exceptional WASSCE or KCSE candidates with top A1/A grades directly into Year 1 or dedicated international first-year pathways.

English language cost-saving tip for WASSCE, KCSE, and NSC applicants: Dozens of UK universities formally accept WASSCE English Language at grade C6 or B3+, KCSE English at grade C+ or B, or South African NSC English at Level 5 (60%+) as proof of English proficiency under UK Student Visa (UKVI) assessment rules, waiving the need to pay for a separate IELTS Academic exam. Always check the target university's English language country list before booking a test centre.

Clearing flexibility, contextual offers, and revision trade-offs

Understanding how Tariff points behave at the margins helps you allocate revision time before summer exams and act decisively on August results day.

Where an extra revision hour unlocks the biggest Tariff jump

Students juggling mixed qualifications often treat all subjects equally during revision season, ignoring how unevenly UCAS points step across qualification types:

  • A-Level grade boundaries (+8 points per step): Moving a borderline C (32) to a B (40) or a B (40) to an A (48) adds a steady 8 Tariff points.
  • IB Higher Level 5-to-6 jump (+16 points in one boundary): On the official UCAS Tariff, an IB Higher Level Grade 5 is worth 32 points while Grade 6 jumps straight to 48 points—a 16-point surge from a single point on the IB 1–7 scale. Pushing one borderline HL subject from 5 to 6 yields four times the UCAS Tariff gain of nudging an EPQ up one letter grade.
  • BTEC Extended Certificate Merit-to-Distinction jump (+16 points): Because a 360-GLH BTEC Extended Certificate has no intermediate grade between Merit (32) and Distinction (48), securing the internal unit points needed to cross from M to D delivers +16 UCAS points—equivalent to raising two separate A-Levels by one grade each.
  • EPQ and AS-Level boundaries (+4 points per step): Raising an EPQ from B (20) to A (24) adds just 4 Tariff points, yet its true value often lies in triggering a contextual/alternative grade reduction (dropping a main AAB offer to ABB, effectively saving 8 points on your core A-Levels).

Contextual offers and widening participation

Most UK universities operate automated contextual admissions policies using your postcode deprivation index (IMD/POLAR4), school attainment profile, care-experienced background, or Free School Meals record. Contextual offers commonly reduce standard requirements by one to two A-Level grades (8 to 16 UCAS Tariff points)—turning a published 136-point (AAB) course into a 120-point (BBB) offer. Because contextual flags are assessed from your UCAS application fields automatically, select the "Custom Tariff" or lower benchmark dropdown in the calculator above to test both the standard headline offer and the published contextual threshold for your target course.

How UCAS Clearing uses Tariff points in August

UCAS Clearing opens from early July and peaks on A-Level results day in mid-August. During the main cycle, universities publish aspirational entry tariffs; during Clearing, unfilled course seats become perishable inventory. A department advertising 128 points in October frequently drops its live Clearing threshold to 112 or 104 points on results morning if recruitment is light, provided you hold required GCSEs and any compulsory science or maths subject. Before phoning a Clearing hotline, calculate your exact verified Tariff score here, click Copy breakdown, and keep your UCAS Personal ID, GCSE English and Maths grades, and individual unit/module breakdown ready in front of you—admissions tutors on phone lines make provisional verbal decisions in under four minutes when candidates quote clear, non-duplicated numbers.

Common mistakes students make with UCAS points

Every admissions cycle, applicants misjudge their competitiveness because of six preventable calculation errors:

  • Double-counting AS-Levels alongside the same A-Level: Entering both AS Biology and A-Level Biology inflates your score by up to 20 phantom points. Keep only the full A-Level whenever you complete the two-year course.
  • Adding GCSE or IGCSE numeric grades (9 to 1) as Tariff points: Seeing numbers on a GCSE statement of results misleads some students into adding 9s, 8s, and 7s to their Tariff score. Level 2 certificates contribute zero UCAS points.
  • Stacking sequential music or speech grades on the same instrument: Only your highest achieved Level 3 grade (Grade 6, 7, or 8) in a given instrument or discipline counts—never the sum of Grades 6, 7, and 8 together.
  • Overlooking three-qualification caps on mixed portfolios: Holding four modest A-Levels at CCCD (120 points) will not satisfy an offer demanding "120 points from three A-Levels", because your best three grades (CCC) yield only 96 points.
  • Confusing predicted grades with achieved bankable points: Conditional offers are made on teacher-predicted grades, which historically exceed final August examination outcomes for a substantial share of applicants. Run this calculator twice: once with your optimistic predicted grades for your two ambitious choices, and once with a conservative floor (one grade lower in your toughest subject) to ensure your Insurance choice is genuinely safe.
  • Ignoring excluded subjects: Many universities explicitly disregard A-Level General Studies, Critical Thinking, Global Perspectives, or an A-Level taken in your native home language if sat late simply to boost points. Check the course entry profile's exclusion note before counting them.

Because it is a general-purpose calculator rather than an institutional admissions portal, it does not automatically delete duplicate AS/A-Level subject pairs if you manually enter both rows, enforce three-qualification caps specific to an individual university offer, verify compulsory subject or GCSE grade 4/6 prerequisites, or convert non-Tariff African secondary certificates such as WASSCE and KCSE. Always verify the binding entry criteria on the official UCAS Course Search page and the university's own undergraduate prospectus before submitting your choices.

Privacy and data handling: All UCAS Tariff point lookups, target comparisons, and clipboard summaries run locally in your web browser, and your qualification entries are never saved to an external database. Only if you choose to press the optional Get AI insight button does your browser send an anonymous calculation summary (total points, target benchmark, qualification count, grade list, and admission tier—never your name, school, or UCAS Personal ID) to /api/ai-insight to generate written commentary.

Frequently asked questions

What is the UCAS Tariff?

The UCAS Tariff is a national system that converts post-16 Level 3 qualifications into a single numerical score. Each grade on an eligible qualification carries a fixed point value based on its study hours and academic demand, allowing university admissions teams to compare A-Level, BTEC, T-Level, IB, and Scottish Higher portfolios on a shared scale.

Do all UK universities use UCAS Tariff points?

No. Highly selective institutions including Oxford, Cambridge, Imperial College London, LSE, UCL, Warwick, and St Andrews make offers almost exclusively in specific letter grades such as A*AA or AAA rather than Tariff points. Most comprehensive, modern, and technical UK universities publish offers in Tariff points, while several Russell Group departments use points selectively for mixed portfolios or during UCAS Clearing.

How many UCAS points is an A* at A-Level worth?

An A* at A-Level is worth 56 UCAS Tariff points. Grade A carries 48 points, B carries 40, C carries 32, D carries 24, and E carries 16. Each grade boundary step changes your score by 8 points. Three A* grades produce 168 points, and four A* grades total 224 points.

Can I combine A-Levels and BTECs for UCAS points?

Yes. Combining academic and applied qualifications is the primary purpose of the UCAS Tariff. For example, a BTEC National Extended Diploma at DMM provides 112 points and a single A-Level at grade B adds 40 points for a combined 152 points, or two A-Levels at BB (80 points) plus a BTEC Extended Certificate at Distinction (48 points) produce 128 points, provided you meet any compulsory subject prerequisites.

Does an EPQ count towards my UCAS points total?

Yes, when a university expresses its offer as an open UCAS Tariff total without restricting the score to three full A-Levels. The Extended Project Qualification is weighted at half an A-Level: A* is worth 28 points, A is 24, B is 20, C is 16, D is 12, and E is 8. Even at grade-offer universities that do not count Tariff points, an A or A* in a relevant EPQ frequently triggers an alternative conditional offer one A-Level grade lower.

What is a good UCAS points score?

A competitive score depends on the university tier and degree subject. Selective research universities and Russell Group courses that use the Tariff typically ask for 120 to 152 points (BBB to A*AA equivalent). Mid-tier comprehensive universities generally ask for 96 to 120 points (CCC to BBB), while integrated foundation years commonly recruit between 48 and 80 points.

Can I include my GCSEs in the UCAS points total?

No. GCSEs, International GCSEs, and National 5s are Level 2 qualifications and carry zero UCAS Tariff points. However, nearly every UK undergraduate degree still requires Level 2 passes in GCSE English Language and Mathematics at grade 4 (grade C) or higher, and selective courses such as Medicine, Economics, or Psychology often require grade 6 or 7.

Do international A-Levels carry the same points as UK A-Levels?

Yes. Cambridge International (CAIE), Pearson Edexcel International A-Levels (IAL), and OxfordAQA International A-Levels carry the exact same official UCAS Tariff points as domestic UK GCE A-Levels: A* equals 56, A equals 48, B equals 40, C equals 32, D equals 24, and E equals 16.

Do WASSCE, NECO, or KCSE results convert into UCAS Tariff points?

No. West African WASSCE and NECO certificates and the Kenyan KCSE are secondary leaving certificates that sit outside the official UCAS Tariff tables. Applicants holding WASSCE or KCSE typically enter UK universities via a one-year International Foundation Programme, whereas South African NSC/IEB Matric and Ugandan UACE holders are often assessed directly for Year 1 entry using institutional subject-grade rules rather than Tariff points.

How many UCAS points does an Access to Higher Education Diploma carry?

An Access to HE Diploma comprises 45 graded Level 3 credits and 15 ungraded study-skills credits. Achieve all 45 graded credits at Distinction and you earn 144 UCAS points (equivalent to AAA). A profile of 30 Distinctions and 15 Merits equals 128 points, 15 Distinctions and 30 Merits equals 112 points, 45 Merits equals 96 points, and 45 Passes equals 48 points.

Do Grade 6, 7, and 8 music or drama exams count towards university offers?

Graded practical and theory examinations at Grades 6, 7, and 8 from regulated boards such as ABRSM, Trinity College London, RSL, and LAMDA carry official Level 3 UCAS Tariff points (for instance, Grade 8 Practical Distinction equals 30 points, Merit 24, and Pass 18). However, only your highest grade on each instrument counts, and many academic courses exclude graded performance points when an offer specifies points from three A-Levels or full-subject equivalents.

Does this calculator store my grades or send them to a server?

All UCAS Tariff calculations run locally in your web browser, and your qualification rows are never stored on a server. Only if you click the optional Get AI insight button does the page send an anonymous summary (total points, target points, qualification count, grade list, and admission tier, with no personal or school details) to /api/ai-insight to generate commentary.
