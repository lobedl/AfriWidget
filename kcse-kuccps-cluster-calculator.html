Frequently asked questions

What are KUCCPS Weighted Cluster Points?

Weighted Cluster Points are the number KUCCPS uses to rank applicants for each degree programme. Every programme requires four specific prerequisite subjects. The applicant's scores in those four subjects form the raw cluster points, out of a maximum of 48. That raw score is combined with the applicant's total KCSE aggregate across seven subjects, out of a maximum of 84, using a square-root formula. The result is the Weighted Cluster Point score, out of 48.000.

What is the exact KUCCPS formula for cluster points?

The formula is: Weighted Cluster Points = Square Root of [(r ÷ 48) × (t ÷ 84)] × 48. r is your total points across the four prerequisite subjects, out of a maximum of 48. t is your total KCSE aggregate points across seven subjects, out of a maximum of 84. The square root balances two things: strength in the specific subjects the programme requires, and consistency across the whole KCSE.

Why do KUCCPS cut-off points change every year?

Three things move the cut-offs. First, national performance in the cluster subjects: if more candidates score highly in Mathematics, Chemistry, or Biology, the cut-off rises across every university that uses those subjects. Second, university capacity: if a department adds places, the cut-off can soften; if it reduces places, the cut-off rises. Third, applicant preference: popular programmes with thousands of first-choice applicants push their cut-offs higher than the same programme at a less popular institution.

What is the difference between first and second revision in KUCCPS?

First revision is the main application window, opened after KCSE results are released. Candidates submit up to four degree choices and a set of diploma or certificate alternatives based on their actual grades. Second revision is opened only for candidates who were not placed in the first round, and it lets them pick from programmes that still had vacancies. Being placed in the first round is always better, because the remaining vacancies in the second round are usually in less popular programmes and institutions.

Can I transfer to a different university after KUCCPS placement?

Yes. KUCCPS runs an Inter-Institutional Transfer window after placements are confirmed. To transfer, you must meet the receiving university's published cut-off for that programme, and the receiving university must have a vacancy. Transfers are handled through the official KUCCPS portal, not by contacting the universities directly. Be cautious of anyone who claims they can arrange a transfer outside the official process.

Does the KUCCPS calculator know the cut-offs for this year?

No. It cannot. Cut-offs for a given admission cycle are only known after the placement run has finished, because they depend on that year's applicant pool and the number of available places. This calculator computes your Weighted Cluster Points, which is the number KUCCPS will compare against whatever cut-offs emerge. The historical ranges shown in the results panel are indicative and shift from year to year.

Can I use this calculator for a diploma or certificate programme?

The Weighted Cluster Points formula is designed for degree programmes at public and private universities. Diploma and certificate placements at Technical and Vocational Education and Training institutions and at universities are handled separately, often by a different placement track. The calculator still gives you a sense of your academic profile, but the specific programme you are applying for has its own entry rules.

How does the Higher Education Financing (HEF) model link to my KUCCPS placement?

Placement by KUCCPS into an accredited degree or TVET programme guarantees eligibility to apply for government funding under the Student-Centred Higher Education Financing (HEF) framework. Students are assessed through the Means Testing Instrument (MTI) and placed into Band 1 (Vulnerable) through Band 5 (Less Needy), determining the percentage of government scholarships, HELB student loans, and household upkeep allowances awarded.

Why can two students with the same KCSE mean grade have drastically different cluster points?

Because the official KUCCPS formula places disproportionate weight on your performance in the specific four prerequisite subjects required by that cluster. An applicant who scored straight A grades in the four cluster subjects will achieve a significantly higher Weighted Cluster Point score than a peer with the same aggregate points who scored A grades in humanities electives but C+ in the mandatory science prerequisites.

Can an applicant apply for Inter-Institutional Transfer after KUCCPS placement?

Yes. Following the conclusion of placement rounds, KUCCPS opens a formal Inter-Institutional Transfer (IIT) online portal window. To transfer successfully, the applicant must meet the receiving university's previous year cut-off point for that programme, the receiving institution must have available capacity and endorse the request, and the releasing institution must grant formal academic clearance.
