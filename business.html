Practical business arithmetic for freelancers, traders, and small teams

Running a small business or working as a freelancer rarely looks like the tidy case studies in a textbook. It looks like pricing a job you are not sure how to price, checking whether a payment processor is quietly eating half your margin, and deciding whether an hourly contract really pays better than the salary offer sitting in your inbox. The calculators on this page exist for exactly those moments. They take a small, concrete set of numbers and return a result you can act on. No signup, no cloud account, no spreadsheet skill required.

Every tool is built around a formula that accountants and analysts actually use. The point is not to replace professional advice for anything serious, but to let you check your own arithmetic before you commit to a price, sign a contract, or quote a client. When you understand the numbers behind a decision, you make better decisions. That is the whole idea.

Pricing, margin, and the markup confusion

The difference between profit margin and markup trips up more small business owners than any other piece of financial vocabulary. Margin is profit divided by the selling price. Markup is profit divided by the cost. A product that costs 60 and sells for 100 has a 40 percent margin and a roughly 67 percent markup. When a business owner sets a price by applying a 50 percent markup and expects a 50 percent margin, they end up with a 33 percent margin instead, and the shortfall quietly eats into what the business actually keeps.

The profit margin calculator on this page shows both figures side by side so the two numbers stop being interchangeable in your head. It also works backwards: give it a target margin and it returns the selling price you need. That is the kind of arithmetic you want to be able to do quickly when you are standing in front of a supplier deciding whether their wholesale price is workable.

Payment processor fees and cross-border reality

PayPal, Stripe, and Square are wonderful until you notice that 3 percent of every sale never reaches your bank. On a single small transaction the amount is tiny. Across a year of modest revenue it becomes real money. The fee calculator on this page lets you compare the major processors on the same transaction, add the flat per-transaction fee in the correct currency, and see the net amount that actually lands in your account. A reverse mode handles the harder question: if you want to net a specific amount after fees, what price do you quote?

This is especially important for freelancers and online sellers working across borders. Cross-border surcharges can add more than a percentage point to the effective rate, and they apply on top of the base fee. Modelling the true cost of accepting payment from a client in another country, before you agree the price, is the difference between a contract that pays and one that quietly does not.

Freelance rates, billable hours, and the salary illusion

Freelance rate setting is where a lot of well-intentioned people undercharge themselves. The mistake is to take the hourly rate from a salaried job, add a small premium for the lack of benefits, and call it a day. That approach ignores the fact that a freelancer's working year is not 2,080 billable hours. Administration, invoicing, marketing, client acquisition, and the quiet gaps between contracts usually consume 20 to 30 percent of available hours. Two weeks of unpaid holiday and a handful of sick days absorb more. A rate that looks generous on paper can produce a modest annual take-home once those realities are in the arithmetic.

The freelance rate calculator and hourly-to-salary converter walk through this honestly. They let you enter the income you actually need, the expenses you actually carry, and the hours you can realistically bill, and then return a rate that stands up to the comparison. For a business hiring freelancers, the same tools work in reverse: enter a target annual cost and see the hourly rate that gets you there.

Startup costs, runway, and cash discipline

A new business rarely fails because it has a bad idea. It fails because the cash runs out before the idea has time to work. Working out launch capital is only half of the planning. The other half is the monthly burn rate: how much does the business spend every month to keep the lights on, before any revenue is counted? Divide the cash reserve by the monthly burn and you have a runway in months. That single number is more useful than any revenue forecast, because it tells you how long you have to make the plan work.

The startup cost calculator and the ROI calculator together cover the two ends of the same question. One tells you what the business needs to launch and survive. The other tells you what a specific investment or project actually returns over time, expressed as a percentage and as an annualized compound rate. Both figures help you prioritise where the next unit of cash should go.

Documents, customer contact, and the small friction points

Some of the most valuable business tools do not involve complex formulas at all. A clean invoice tells a client you are professional. A well-formed WhatsApp link turns a passive phone number into a one-tap conversation. A QR code on a shop counter or business card removes the small friction of asking customers to type a number into their phone. These are not deep financial calculations; they are the connective tissue of a business that is easy to work with. The documents and communication tools on this page handle them without asking you to install anything or create an account.

AI copy tools as a first draft, not a final answer

The AI-assisted tools on this page generate first drafts: names to explore, product descriptions to edit, gig listings to tighten, summaries to skim. They are built to break through a blank page, not to replace your judgement. Every output should be read, corrected, and adapted to sound like you. Use them as a starting point, take responsibility for the final text, and never publish a draft you have not read properly yourself. That is true of any AI tool on the market, and it is worth saying plainly.

What stays on your device

Every calculation on this page runs inside your browser using JavaScript. Your cost figures, prices, hourly rates, and business numbers never leave your device. If a tool offers an optional AI button, only an anonymous summary of the computed values is sent, and nothing is stored. That makes these tools safe for sensitive information: wholesale pricing you are testing, contractor rates you have not announced, project budgets you would rather not publish. Use them freely, knowing the numbers stay with you.

These calculators are planning aids, not accounting, tax, or legal advice. Confirm important figures against your own records, your accountant, or the relevant professional before making a business commitment.
