How to use this Leaving Cert points calculator

This tool takes your Leaving Certificate subjects, levels, and grades, converts each one to its CAO points value, adds the Higher Maths bonus where it applies, and returns your total from your best six subjects. It handles Higher Level, Ordinary Level, Foundation Level Maths, and LCVP Link Modules. Everything runs inside your browser. No grades are uploaded or stored.

To get an accurate result, enter every subject you sat and the grade you achieved. If you are estimating your points before results day, use your best guess from mock exams. The calculator allows up to nine subjects so you can see exactly which six are counting. You do not need to enter subjects you failed or subjects you did not sit, as they will not add to your total.

If you are calculating points for Higher Maths, type "Maths" or "Mathematics" in the subject name field and select Higher Level. The calculator automatically detects this and applies the 25-point bonus if your grade is H6 or above.

Understanding the CAO points scale

Since 2017, the Irish Leaving Certificate has used a banded grading system rather than the old A1, A2, B1 structure. This change was introduced to reduce the tiny percentage differences between students and to make the points system more transparent. Under the current system, grades are awarded in ten-percentage-point bands.

Higher Level grades run from H1 to H8. Ordinary Level grades run from O1 to O8. The points awarded for each band are fixed and apply to every subject equally, with one exception for Higher Level Maths. The maximum points available for a single subject is 100 (a H1).

Figure 1: The CAO points scale. Notice that an O1 (56 points) is worth exactly the same as an H5 (56 points).

The scale is designed so that a strong Ordinary Level result can be worth as much as a middling Higher Level result. An O1 and an H5 both carry 56 points. An O2 and an H6 both carry 46 points. This means that choosing the level where you will perform best is often more strategic than choosing the level that sounds more impressive. A student who scrapes a H7 in Higher Level Maths will get 37 points, while a student who gets an O1 in Ordinary Level Maths will get 56 points.

The Higher Maths bonus explained

The Higher Maths bonus is a policy designed to encourage students to take the subject at the highest level. Ireland has a recognised skills gap in STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics) fields, and the government wants to incentivise students to pursue these areas at third level. The bonus provides a significant advantage.

If you sit Higher Level Mathematics and achieve a H6 or higher (which means a grade of at least 40 percent), the CAO adds 25 bonus points to your Maths score. If you get a H7 or H8, you do not get the bonus. If you take Ordinary Level Maths, you do not get the bonus.

The maths that makes the bonus powerful: A H6 is normally worth 46 points. With the bonus, it becomes 71 points. A H5 is normally 56 points, and with the bonus, it becomes 81 points. That 25-point swing is often enough to pull Maths from your seventh-best subject into your top six, fundamentally changing your overall score.

It is important to understand the order of operations. The CAO does not calculate your best six subjects first and then add the bonus to your total. The bonus is added to the Maths score before the best six subjects are identified. This means Maths is evaluated with its bonus included when determining if it is one of your top six results. If Maths was your seventh-best subject, the bonus might make it your fourth or fifth-best, bumping a different subject out of the top six.

The Best Six Rule: How CAO points are actually summed

The CAO looks at the results from a single sitting of the Leaving Certificate. You can sit as many subjects as you want, but only six will count. Your points total is the sum of the scores for your six highest-performing subjects.

This rule is straightforward but often misunderstood. If you sit eight subjects and get 80 points in all of them, you do not get 640 points. You get 480 points, because only six subjects are counted.

Figure 2: The Best Six Rule. Even if you take seven subjects, the lowest-scoring one is dropped from your CAO total.

Because of the one-sitting rule, the CAO does not combine your best results from different years. If you sit the Leaving Cert in 2025 and repeat a subject in 2026 to improve your grade, your 2026 points are calculated using only your 2026 results. You cannot mix a H1 from 2025 with a H2 from 2026 to maximise your score. Your points are tied to a specific year's cohort.

LCVP Link Modules and Foundation Level

The Leaving Certificate Vocational Programme (LCVP) is an optional programme that focuses on enterprise and preparation for the world of work. The Link Modules are assessed with a written exam and a portfolio. The points awarded for LCVP are: Distinction (66 points), Merit (46 points), and Pass (28 points).

LCVP points can be used as one of your best six subjects. However, there is a catch: not every higher education institution accepts LCVP as meeting the minimum entry requirements for every course. For example, a course might require two H5 grades. If you have one H5 and an LCVP Distinction, some universities might accept the LCVP towards the points total, but it might not satisfy the specific subject requirement. Always check the individual institution's prospectus.

Foundation Level is only available for Mathematics and Irish. Foundation Maths carries 20 points for an F1 and 12 points for an F2. Foundation Irish is generally not awarded CAO points. Points for Foundation Maths are only awarded by certain institutions, so it is not a universal currency for entry.

Worked examples: Three student profiles

Let us look at how the calculator handles different scenarios. These examples demonstrate how the best six rule and the maths bonus interact in practice.

Example 1: The STEM Student

Sarah wants to study Engineering. She sat eight subjects.

  • Higher Maths: H4 (66 points)
  • Higher Physics: H3 (77 points)
  • Higher Chemistry: H4 (66 points)
  • Higher English: H5 (56 points)
  • Higher Irish: H5 (56 points)
  • Ordinary French: O2 (46 points)
  • Higher Applied Maths: H2 (88 points)
  • LCVP: Distinction (66 points)

First, we apply the Maths bonus. Sarah got a H4 in Higher Maths, so her Maths score becomes 66 + 25 = 91 points.

Now we sort her subjects from highest to lowest:

  1. Maths: 91
  2. Applied Maths: 88
  3. Physics: 77
  4. Chemistry: 66
  5. LCVP: 66
  6. English: 56
  7. Irish: 56
  8. French: 46

The top six are Maths (91), Applied Maths (88), Physics (77), Chemistry (66), LCVP (66), and English (56). The total is 444 points. Sarah's Irish and French results are dropped. The Maths bonus added 25 points, making it her highest-scoring subject.

Example 2: The Arts Student

David wants to study History. He sat seven subjects and did not take Higher Maths.

  • Ordinary Maths: O1 (56 points)
  • Higher English: H1 (100 points)
  • Higher History: H2 (88 points)
  • Higher Geography: H3 (77 points)
  • Higher French: H4 (66 points)
  • Higher Art: H3 (77 points)
  • Higher Music: H5 (56 points)

No Maths bonus applies here. The sorted list is:

  1. English: 100
  2. History: 88
  3. Geography: 77
  4. Art: 77
  5. French: 66
  6. Maths: 56
  7. Music: 56

The top six are English (100), History (88), Geography (77), Art (77), French (66), and Maths (56). The total is 464 points. Music is dropped because it ties with Maths for 56 points, but only one can be the sixth subject. In this case, David's Ordinary Maths O1 was strong enough to make the top six, proving that a high Ordinary grade can be just as valuable as a middling Higher grade.

Example 3: The LCVP Advantage

Emma sat six subjects and achieved a Distinction in LCVP.

  • Higher Biology: H4 (66 points)
  • Higher Chemistry: H5 (56 points)
  • Higher English: H4 (66 points)
  • Higher Maths: H7 (37 points)
  • Ordinary Irish: O2 (46 points)
  • LCVP: Distinction (66 points)

Emma did not get the Maths bonus because she got a H7 (below the H6 threshold). Her sorted points are:

  1. Biology: 66
  2. English: 66
  3. LCVP: 66
  4. Chemistry: 56
  5. Irish: 46
  6. Maths: 37

All six subjects count because she only sat six. Her total is 337. The LCVP Distinction essentially saved her score, pulling it up significantly compared to her core subjects alone.

The CAO application timeline and offers process

Calculating your points is only one part of the journey. You need to navigate the CAO application process correctly. The CAO is a centralised system, so a single application covers almost all undergraduate courses in Ireland.

  • November to January: CAO opens for applications. You must register and pay the application fee. If you apply by the early deadline (usually late January), you pay a reduced fee.
  • February to June: Change of Mind facility opens. You can log in and change the courses you have listed. You can add, remove, or reorder your course choices as many times as you want until the closing date (usually 1st July).
  • June: Leaving Certificate exams take place.
  • August: Leaving Cert results are released. The CAO calculates points and the first round of offers are sent out.
  • August to September: Subsequent rounds of offers. If you do not get your first choice, you might get an offer for a lower preference. You can accept a lower offer and still wait for a higher one in the next round.

A crucial strategy is the order of your course choices. The CAO processes offers based on your order of preference. If you put a lower-points course first, you will likely be offered that course immediately, and you may not be considered for a higher-points course later. Always place your dream courses at the top, followed by realistic options, and finish with safe backups.

Ties, random selection, and what happens next

Because the CAO points system uses large blocks (e.g., 10 points per subject band), many students end up with exactly the same points total. If there are more students with a specific score than there are places available on a course, the university uses a tie-breaker system.

The primary tie-breaker is random selection. The CAO generates a random number for each applicant. If the cut-off is 450 points and twenty students have exactly 450 points competing for ten places, the ten students with the lowest random numbers will be offered the places. This is why you sometimes hear of a student with the exact points requirement failing to get an offer while another student with the same points succeeds.

It is worth noting that individual universities may apply their own tie-breakers in specific circumstances, such as giving priority to mature students or students with specific disabilities. However, the random selection remains the baseline for the majority of standard applicants.

Common mistakes students make

Adding up all subjects. The most common error is adding seven or eight subjects together. Only six count. If you have 8 subjects, you are overestimating your score by hundreds of points.

Ignoring the Maths bonus threshold. Students often think a H7 in Higher Maths is better than an O1 in Ordinary Maths because it is a "higher" level. In fact, a H7 is 37 points and an O1 is 56 points. Furthermore, you only get the bonus for H6 or above.

Assuming points are the only requirement. A student might have 500 points but fail to meet the minimum requirement of a H5 in a specific subject. If you do not meet the minimum entry requirements, your points are irrelevant.

Forgetting about the portfolio or interview. Some courses, such as Art, Architecture, Music, or Medicine, require a portfolio or an HPAT (Health Professions Admission Test). These assessments are scored separately and added to your CAO points. Always check if your desired course requires additional assessments.

Points are not eligibility

This calculator shows your points total. It does not tell you whether you are eligible for a particular course. Every course has minimum entry requirements, usually a set number of Higher and Ordinary level passes, and many courses require specific subjects or grades. You must meet those requirements before your points are even considered.

For example, an Engineering course might require a H4 in Higher Maths and a H4 in a science subject. Even if you have 600 points, if you only have a H5 in Maths, you are not eligible for that course. Always read the course description in the CAO handbook or the university's prospectus carefully.

This is an independent calculator built to match the published CAO points scale and bonus rules. It is not the official CAO calculator, and it does not know the specific entry requirements for your courses. Use it for quick planning and comparison. For the definitive figure, use the official CAO calculator on cao.ie and check the course literature for the institutions you are applying to.

Frequently asked questions

How are Leaving Cert points calculated for the CAO?

The CAO takes your best six subjects from one sitting of the Leaving Certificate, converts each grade to a points value using the common points scale, adds 25 bonus points if you sat Higher Level Maths and got an H6 or better, and then sums the six highest scores. Only one sitting counts, and only recognised subjects are included. LCVP Link Modules can count as one of the six subjects if they are among your best results.

What is the new Leaving Cert grading system?

Since 2017, the Leaving Certificate uses bands instead of the old A1, A2, B1 system. Higher level grades run from H1 to H8, and Ordinary level grades run from O1 to O8. H1 and O1 cover 90 to 100 percent, H2 and O2 cover 80 to 90 percent, and the bands continue down in ten-point steps. A H1 is worth 100 points, a H2 is 88, and the values step down to 37 for a H7 and 0 for a H8. Ordinary level grades carry lower values.

How do the Higher Maths bonus points work?

If you sit Higher Level Mathematics and get a H6 or above (40 percent or more), the CAO adds 25 bonus points to your Maths score. For example, a H5 in Higher Maths is worth 56 points, and with the bonus it becomes 81 points. The bonus is applied before the best six subjects are selected, so it can pull Maths into your top six even if it would otherwise have been your seventh-best subject.

Can I count more than six subjects?

No. The CAO counts only your six best subjects from a single sitting. You can sit eight or nine subjects, and this calculator lets you enter up to nine so you can see which six are actually counting. Subjects outside the top six do not add to your points total, even if you did well in them.

Does LCVP count for CAO points?

Yes. Leaving Certificate Vocational Programme Link Modules can count as one of your six subjects. A Distinction is worth 66 points, a Merit is worth 46 points, and a Pass is worth 28 points. Not every higher education institution counts LCVP as meeting eligibility requirements for every course, so check the individual institution's literature if you are relying on it for entry.

What about Foundation Level subjects?

Foundation Level is only available for Mathematics and Irish. Foundation Maths carries 20 points for an F1 and 12 points for an F2, but only certain institutions award points for it. Foundation Irish is not generally awarded points. The calculator includes Foundation Maths because it appears on the official grid, but you should check the specific course requirements before relying on it.

Do I need to meet minimum entry requirements as well as points?

Yes. Points are only one part of entry. Every course also has minimum entry requirements, typically a certain number of Higher and Ordinary level passes, and some courses require specific subjects or grades. You must meet the minimum requirements before your points are even considered. The calculator shows your points score, not your eligibility for a particular course.

Is this the official CAO calculator?

No. This is an independent calculator built to match the published CAO points scale and bonus rules. The official CAO points calculator is on cao.ie. Use this tool for quick planning and comparison, and treat the official CAO calculator and your offer letter as the definitive source.

Does the calculator store my grades?

No. Every calculation runs inside your browser tab. The grades you enter and the total you get are never uploaded to any server. If you press the AI insight button, only an anonymous summary of your subject count, total points, and whether the Maths bonus applied is sent to a small serverless function that runs once and then stops.
