Understanding UK Honours Degree Classifications

In the United Kingdom and across higher education institutions adhering to the British degree framework, undergraduate Bachelor's degrees with Honours (BA, BSc, BEng, LLB) are classified into distinct academic tiers based on your weighted percentage average across qualifying years.

How UK Universities Calculate Degree Marks

While each university publishes its own Academic Regulations in accordance with the Quality Assurance Agency for Higher Education (QAA), three foundational principles govern degree algorithms:

1. Year 1 Exclusion

First-year modules (Level 4) serve as foundational qualifying credits. You must pass them (usually 40%+), but their marks carry 0% weight toward your final honours classification.

2. Year Weighting Ratios

Most universities apply the 1:2 ratio (33.3% Year 2 + 66.7% Year 3) or 1:3 ratio (25% Year 2 + 75% Year 3) to reward "exit velocity" and academic progression.

3. Borderline Discretion

If your overall mark falls within 2% of a classification boundary (e.g. 68.0%–69.9% for a First), university exam boards promote you if at least 50% of your final-year credits are in the higher classification.

Frequently Asked Questions

What percentage mark do I need for a First-Class Honours (1st) degree in the UK? ▼

In the UK higher education system, an overall weighted average of 70.00% or higher earns a First-Class Honours (1st) degree. Many universities also operate a borderline discretion policy for students achieving between 68.00% and 69.99% if at least 50% of their final-year credits are in the First-Class band.

Does Year 1 (Level 4) count towards my final degree classification? ▼

At the vast majority of UK universities, Year 1 (Level 4) does not count towards your final degree classification. Students must pass Year 1 (typically with an average of at least 40%) to progress to Year 2, but only Level 5 (Year 2) and Level 6 (Year 3) grades are factored into the final honours calculation.

What is the standard year weighting ratio across UK universities? ▼

The most common degree algorithm is the 1:2 ratio (33.3% Year 2 + 66.7% Year 3). Other common models include 1:3 (25% Year 2 + 75% Year 3) and equal weighting (50% Year 2 + 50% Year 3). For 4-year integrated master's degrees (MEng, MSci), a 20:40:40 or 1:2:2 ratio across Years 2, 3, and 4 is typically used.

How does the "drop lowest module" rule work? ▼

Some UK universities calculate your degree using the best 100 out of 120 credits in Year 2 or Year 3, effectively discarding your single lowest-scoring 20-credit module (provided it is not a compulsory core module or dissertation). You can toggle this rule on or off in the calculator settings.
