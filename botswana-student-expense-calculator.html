Complete Guide to Student Living Costs & DTEF Allowances in Botswana

Navigating tertiary education in Botswana—whether studying at the University of Botswana (UB) in Gaborone, the Botswana International University of Science and Technology (BIUST) in Palapye, BUAN in Sebele, or private institutions like Botho University and BAC—requires careful financial management. While government sponsorship through the Department of Tertiary Education Financing (DTEF) covers full tuition and a monthly living allowance, students frequently face living cost deficits if expenses are not proactively planned.

1. How DTEF Student Sponsorship & Allowances Work

The Department of Tertiary Education Financing (DTEF) operates under the Ministry of Tertiary Education, Research, Science and Technology (MOTE) to disburse tuition grants, student loans, and monthly stipends to qualifying Botswana citizens. The monthly allowance structure differs fundamentally based on your housing status:

  • Off-Campus Living Allowance: Students who reside outside campus hostels receive a full monthly living stipend (typically between P1,420 and P1,900 per month, subject to current ministerial fiscal schedules). This allowance is intended to cover accommodation rent, utility contributions, food, daily combi transport, and personal necessities.
  • On-Campus Resident Allowance: Students allocated rooms in official university hostels (such as UB residences or BIUST student villages) receive a smaller incidental monthly stipend (around P300 to P500 per month) because their accommodation fees are settled directly through university financial administration.
  • Book & Project Allowances: In addition to monthly stipends, DTEF typically provides an annual or semester book allowance at the start of the academic year for textbooks, stationery, and laboratory equipment.

2. Typical Monthly Expense Breakdown for Tertiary Students

Student living costs in Botswana vary by location. Gaborone has the highest accommodation and grocery rates, while Palapye (BIUST) and Francistown offer slightly lower housing costs but may involve higher intercity transport expenses.

Accommodation / Rent: P800 – P1,800 / month
Single room rentals in popular student areas near UB (Tlokweng, Village, Extension 2, Block 6, Broadhurst) range from P1,100 to P1,800. In Palapye near BIUST, private rooms range from P800 to P1,400. Sharing a 2-bedroom house with other students reduces rent to P600–P900 per student.
Food & Groceries: P600 – P1,200 / month
Buying staples (maize meal, rice, pasta, canned fish, eggs, milk, sugar, cooking oil) from Choppies or Sefalana costs approximately P600–P850/mo. Daily campus cafeteria meals cost P25–P45 per meal, which quickly adds up to P750–P1,350/mo if consumed daily.
Combi & Taxi Fares: P200 – P450 / month
Standard public combi fares in Gaborone and major towns range from P6 to P8 per single trip. A student taking two combis daily (home to campus and back) spends around P12–P16 per day, equating to P260–P350 per month over a 22-day academic month.
Mobile Data & Connectivity: P100 – P250 / month
While university campuses offer eduroam and institutional Wi-Fi, off-campus study requires reliable mobile internet. Mascom, Orange, and BTC offer specialized student bundles ranging from P50 to P150 per month for nocturnal and research data.
Utilities & Gas: P80 – P200 / month
Prepaid BPC electricity tokens, shared water bills, and 9kg/19kg cooking gas refills are typically shared among roommates in multi-tenant compounds.

3. Step-by-Step Worked Budgeting Scenarios

Scenario A: Off-Campus UB Student in Tlokweng

- **Total Monthly Income**: DTEF Allowance (P1,600) + Parent Support (P400) = **P2,000.00**
- **Total Monthly Expenses**: Rent (P1,000) + Groceries (P600) + Combi (P280) + Data (P100) + Utilities (P80) + Stationery (P60) = **P2,120.00**
- **Net Balance**: Deficit of **-P120.00 / month** (P600 deficit over a 5-month semester).
- **Remedy**: Meal prepping 4 days a week saves P150/month, converting the deficit into a P30 positive reserve.

Scenario B: BIUST On-Campus Student in Palapye

- **Total Monthly Income**: DTEF Incidentals (P400) + Part-time Lab Tutoring (P400) = **P800.00**
- **Total Monthly Expenses**: Campus Cafeteria (P450) + Personal Hygiene (P120) + Mobile Data (P80) + Stationery/Printing (P80) = **P730.00**
- **Net Balance**: Surplus of **+P70.00 / month** (P350 reserve per semester for holiday travel back to Maun/Gaborone).

4. Practical Money-Saving Strategies for Botswana Students

  • Form a Grocery Pool: Buy bulk 10kg bags of maize meal, rice, samp, and cooking oil with roommates at Sefalana or Choppies to save up to 25% compared to individual shopping.
  • Utilize Campus Wi-Fi for Heavy Downloads: Download lecture recordings, PDFs, and video resources on the high-speed campus eduroam network during the day to avoid depleting mobile data at night.
  • Walk When Distance Permits: If living in Tlokweng close to UB main gate or Village, walking instead of taking a combi saves P300+ every month.
  • Keep an Emergency Buffer: Reserve at least P100 each month in a mobile money wallet (MyZaka, Orange Money, or Smega) for unexpected medical visits or urgent printing runs.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How much is the monthly DTEF student living allowance in Botswana? ▼

The Department of Tertiary Education Financing (DTEF) provides monthly living allowances to sponsored students attending approved institutions. Off-campus students receive approximately P1,420 to P1,900 per month for living and accommodation expenses, while on-campus resident students receive an incidental allowance (around P300 to P500 per month) since institutional meals and housing are subsidized.

What is the average monthly cost of living for a student in Gaborone? ▼

A student living off-campus in Gaborone (such as in Tlokweng, Block 6, Block 8, or Broadhurst) typically spends between P2,200 and P3,800 per month, covering single room rental (P900–P1,600), groceries and food (P700–P1,200), daily combi transit (P250–P450), mobile data (P150–P300), and personal hygiene/stationery (P200–P350).

Is living on-campus cheaper than off-campus at UB or BIUST? ▼

Living on-campus is generally much more predictable and cost-effective because water, electricity, Wi-Fi, and transport costs are virtually zero. However, hostel accommodation space is limited and prioritizes first-year students and special categories.

What should I do if my DTEF allowance is delayed? ▼

Allowance disbursements can occasionally experience administrative delays at the start of a semester. Students should maintain at least one month's basic food and emergency reserves, notify their landlord in advance with proof of sponsorship, and register with the university's Student Welfare Directorate for emergency food hamper or student relief support.
