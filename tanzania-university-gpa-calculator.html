What this calculator measures

This tool takes the course units you are registered for in a semester, the credit weight attached to each one, and the letter grade you earned, and returns three numbers that Tanzanian universities use to track academic standing: the semester Grade Point Average, the cumulative Grade Point Average, and the credit-weighted quality points behind both figures.

The calculator also projects a degree classification from your cumulative record. On the 5.0 scale used across TCU-registered universities, the projection uses the thresholds published by UDSM, UDOM, SUA, Mzumbe, and most other public and private institutions.

Everything runs in your browser. No course code, credit weight, or grade leaves your device. The only part of the page that reaches the network is the AI insight button, and it only fires when you press it.

The Tanzanian 5.0 grading scale

The Tanzania Commission for Universities sets the standard grading framework for undergraduate degrees across public and private universities. Marks combine continuous assessment, usually around 40 percent of the total, with the final university examination, usually around 60 percent, and the combined score maps to one of five letter grades.

GradeScore bandGrade pointReading
A70 to 100 percent5.0Excellent
B+60 to 69 percent4.0Very good
B50 to 59 percent3.0Good
C40 to 49 percent2.0Satisfactory pass
D35 to 39 percent1.0Marginal fail, supplementary route
E0 to 34 percent0.0Full fail, repeat required

A grade of C at 2.0 points is the minimum statutory pass. A grade of D is a marginal fail that opens a supplementary examination route at most institutions, and a grade of E requires the student to repeat the unit. The point value of each grade is what feeds into the GPA calculation, not the raw percentage.

The GPA and CGPA formulas

Higher education units in Tanzania carry a credit weight, most commonly 7.5, 10, 12, or 15 credits, where one credit represents roughly ten notional learning hours. The GPA is a credit-weighted average, not a plain average of the letter grades.

Quality points for one unit equal the credit weight multiplied by the grade point for that unit. A 12-credit unit passed with an A gives 12 times 5.0, which is 60 quality points. An 8-credit unit passed with a C gives 8 times 2.0, which is 16 quality points.

Semester GPA is the total semester quality points divided by the total semester credits. A semester with 178 quality points spread across 42 credits gives a GPA of about 4.24.

Cumulative CGPA extends the calculation across every semester a student has completed. Add the prior cumulative quality points to the current semester quality points, add the prior credits to the current credits, then divide. This is the figure that determines degree classification at graduation.

A note on rounding. Most Tanzanian universities report GPA and CGPA to two decimal places. Do not round any intermediate step, or the final figure will drift slightly from the one your registrar prints on the transcript.

Degree classification thresholds

At the end of the degree programme, the cumulative CGPA determines the classification awarded on the transcript. The table below matches the standard bands applied by TCU-registered universities.

ClassificationCGPA on 5.0 scaleReading
First Class Honours4.40 to 5.00Outstanding
Upper Second Class, Class II Division I3.50 to 4.39Very good
Lower Second Class, Class II Division II2.70 to 3.49Good
Pass Degree2.00 to 2.69Minimum graduation threshold
Fail or DiscontinuationBelow 2.00Below the statutory degree floor

Upper Second Class is the classification most Tanzanian postgraduate programmes look for. TCU guidance states that direct Master's admission requires at least Upper Second at 3.50 or above. Lower Second Class at 2.70 to 3.49 is often accepted when combined with a postgraduate diploma or a strong record of relevant professional experience.

First Class Honours opens the door to academic staff positions such as Tutorial Assistant, to scholarship funding, and to competitive international postgraduate routes. It is a demanding band that requires sustained performance across the whole degree, not just the final year.

A worked semester example

Take a first-year B.Sc Computer Science student at the University of Dar es Salaam completing a 42-credit semester.

Basic Computer Programming is 12 credits and scored 74 percent, which is an A at 5.0 points, giving 60 quality points. Foundations of Analysis is 12 credits and scored 66 percent, a B+ at 4.0, giving 48 quality points. Computer Architecture is 10 credits and scored 55 percent, a B at 3.0, giving 30 quality points. Development Studies is 8 credits and scored 81 percent, an A at 5.0, giving 40 quality points.

Total credit weight is 42. Total quality points are 178. The semester GPA is 178 divided by 42, which is 4.24. That places the student in the Upper Second Class band at the end of the first semester, close to the First Class line but not quite there.

Now suppose the same student entered the year with a prior record of 120 credits and a cumulative CGPA of 3.65. The prior quality points are 120 times 3.65, which is 438. Adding the two together gives 162 credits and 616 total quality points. The updated CGPA is 3.80, still in the Upper Second band. Every strong semester from here moves the average further toward the First Class boundary at 4.40.

Supplementary exams and probation

Two academic rules catch students off guard, and both are worth understanding before they cost a semester.

The supplementary examination is the standard route back from a marginal fail. A grade of D at 35 to 39 percent usually opens the right to sit a supplementary paper at the start of the next academic year. When the supplementary is passed, most Tanzanian universities cap the recorded grade at C, which is 2.0 points, regardless of how high the retake score was. That cap exists to keep a second attempt from inflating the GPA, and it means a supplementary pass still pulls the CGPA down compared to a clean pass on the first attempt.

The academic probation rule sets a floor at 2.00. A student whose semester GPA or cumulative CGPA falls below 2.00 is placed on probation and may be required to repeat the year. Persistent failure to lift the average above 2.00 can lead to discontinuation from the programme. The probation system is meant to give a struggling student a defined window to recover, but the window is short and the consequences of missing it are real.

Common mistakes students make

Mixing credit weights. A 12-credit core unit is not the same as an 8-credit elective. Double-check the credit listing in your programme handbook before you type anything in. A single wrong credit weight across several units can move the final GPA by a tenth of a point or more.

Confusing a pass with a clean pass. A supplementary pass capped at C is not the same as a C earned on the first attempt, even though both appear on the transcript as C. The cap only affects the recorded grade, not the effort it took to get there, but it matters when you are tracking how many units you cleared cleanly.

Ignoring the prior record. A semester GPA on its own does not tell you where you stand. Universities use the cumulative CGPA for classification and probation decisions. Always enter the prior record if you want a meaningful picture of your actual standing.

Assuming a strong final year can rescue a weak start. The cumulative average is credit-weighted, and by the final year the denominator is large. A student who takes three weak years and then tries to lift the average in the last two semesters will find that the mathematics is against them. The most effective way to lift a CGPA is early, when each new credit still moves the average significantly.

Not checking what counts toward CGPA. Some universities exclude certain remedial or non-graded modules from the CGPA calculation. Others cap the number of times a failed unit can be retaken. Check your faculty handbook before you assume the calculator is wrong.

Limits of the calculator

This tool computes what you tell it. It cannot see your transcript, it cannot verify grades, and it cannot know if your faculty applies a weighting rule that differs from the standard one. It is a planning aid, not an official statement of your academic standing.

It also cannot predict a future semester with certainty, beyond letting you test scenarios. If you want to know whether a target CGPA is realistic, run the calculator with hypothetical grades and credit weights. That is the fastest way to judge before the semester starts whether a goal is achievable or whether a different strategy is needed.

Finally, the classification on this page is a projection, not a guarantee. Tanzanian universities reserve the right to adjust classification bands in a given year, especially in borderline cases or where a faculty applies progressive upper-year weighting. Confirm the exact rules with your faculty before you plan your final year around them.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum pass mark at Tanzanian universities?

On the TCU 5.0 scale, a Grade C at 2.0 grade points is the minimum passing grade for a course unit. A Grade D at 1.0 point is a marginal fail that typically opens a supplementary examination route, and a Grade E at 0.0 points is a full fail that requires repeating the unit.

What CGPA is required for First Class Honours in Tanzania?

On the 5.0 scale used at UDSM, UDOM, SUA, Mzumbe, and other TCU-registered universities, First Class Honours requires a cumulative CGPA of 4.40 to 5.00. Upper Second Class runs from 3.50 to 4.39, Lower Second Class from 2.70 to 3.49, and a Pass degree from 2.00 to 2.69.

What CGPA do I need for a Master's degree in Tanzania?

The TCU expects direct Master's admission to require at least an Upper Second Class bachelor's degree, which is a cumulative CGPA of 3.50 or above. Lower Second Class at 2.70 to 3.49 is often accepted when combined with a postgraduate diploma or a strong record of relevant professional experience.

How is GPA calculated on the Tanzanian 5.0 scale?

Each course unit carries a credit weight. Quality points equal the credit weight multiplied by the grade point for that unit. The semester GPA is the total semester quality points divided by the total semester credits. Cumulative CGPA extends the same calculation across every semester completed.

What happens if I score a Grade D in a core unit?

Most Tanzanian universities treat Grade D at 35 to 39 percent as a marginal fail and allow a supplementary examination. When the supplementary is passed, the recorded grade is usually capped at Grade C, which is 2.0 grade points, regardless of the score in the retake.

How are continuous assessment and final exams weighted in Tanzania?

Most course units split the total mark between continuous assessment, usually 40 percent, and the final university examination, usually 60 percent. Continuous assessment typically includes tests, quizzes, laboratory practicals, and group work. Students normally need a minimum continuous assessment score to be eligible for the final exam.

At what GPA is a student placed on academic probation?

Under the standard TCU and Senate regulations, a student whose semester GPA or cumulative CGPA falls below 2.00 is placed on academic probation and may be required to repeat the year. Persistent failure to lift the average above 2.00 can lead to discontinuation from the programme.

Does the calculator store my grades?

No. Every calculation runs in your browser. No course code, credit weight, or grade is sent anywhere, and nothing is stored. The only part of the page that reaches the network is the AI insight button, and only when you press it.
