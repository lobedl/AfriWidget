What this calculator measures

This tool takes the courses you are registered for in a semester, the credit units attached to each one, and the letter grade you earned, and returns three numbers that Nigerian universities use to track academic standing: the semester Grade Point Average, the cumulative Grade Point Average, and the credit-weighted quality points behind both figures.

The calculator also projects a degree honours classification from your cumulative record. On the NUC 5.0 scale, the projection uses the standard thresholds applied across federal, state, and private universities in Nigeria. On the NBTE 4.0 scale, it uses the polytechnic bands that lead to distinctions, credits, and passes at the diploma level.

Everything runs in your browser. No course code, credit unit, or grade leaves your device. The only part of the page that reaches the network is the AI insight button, and it only fires when you press it.

Nigerian grading scales in plain language

Nigeria's higher education system runs on two scales. The National Universities Commission sets the standard for universities, and the National Board for Technical Education sets the standard for polytechnics and colleges of technology. The two scales look similar on the surface but diverge in the middle bands.

The NUC 5.0 scale is a simple five-letter system. A is 70 percent and above and carries 5 grade points. B covers 60 to 69 percent at 4 points. C covers 50 to 59 percent at 3 points. D covers 45 to 49 percent at 2 points. E covers 40 to 44 percent at 1 point. F is anything below 40 percent and carries zero points, which means the course must be retaken or replaced before graduation.

The NBTE 4.0 scale has more bands. A is 75 percent and above at 4 points. AB sits between A and B at 3.5 points. B is 60 to 64 percent at 3 points. BC is 55 to 59 percent at 2.5 points. C is 50 to 54 percent at 2 points. Below 50 percent is a fail. The finer bands let polytechnic diploma awards separate students who would otherwise look identical on a five-letter scale.

The GPA and CGPA formulas

The two figures most students track are calculated from the same building block.

Quality points for a course are the credit units multiplied by the grade point. A three-unit course passed with an A on the NUC scale gives 3 times 5, which is 15 quality points. A two-unit course passed with a C gives 2 times 3, which is 6.

Semester GPA is the sum of all quality points in the semester divided by the sum of all credit units in the semester. A semester with 61 quality points spread across 14 credit units gives a GPA of about 4.36.

Cumulative CGPA extends the calculation across every semester a student has completed. Add the prior cumulative quality points to the current semester quality points, add the prior credits to the current credits, then divide.

A note on rounding. Most Nigerian universities keep GPA and CGPA at two decimal places. Do not round any intermediate step, or the final CGPA will drift slightly from the figure your registrar prints on the transcript.

Degree honours thresholds

Nigerian universities classify the bachelor's degree into one of five bands based on the cumulative CGPA at graduation. The thresholds below match the benchmarks set by the NUC and adopted at the majority of federal, state, and private universities.

Second Class Upper Division is the band most Nigerian postgraduate programs and graduate employers look for. First Class Honours opens research positions, federal scholarships, and international Master's pathways. Second Class Lower Division still earns the degree and clears the minimum for many postgraduate diplomas, but the door to the most competitive routes narrows.

The NBTE 4.0 scale uses a different set of labels for polytechnic diplomas: Distinction at 3.50 and above, Upper Credit from 3.00 to 3.49, Lower Credit from 2.50 to 2.99, and Pass from 2.00 to 2.49. Upper Credit is the standard threshold for HND admission and for university direct-entry transfers.

A worked example

Take a 100-level first-semester student at a Nigerian federal university with five courses registered and fourteen credit units.

GST 111 is worth 2 units and scored 74 percent, which is an A at 5.0 points, giving 10 quality points. MTH 101 is worth 3 units and scored 72 percent, also an A, giving 15 quality points. PHY 101 is worth 3 units and scored 65 percent, a B at 4.0, giving 12 quality points. CHM 101 is worth 3 units and scored 55 percent, a C at 3.0, giving 9 quality points. BIO 101 is worth 3 units and scored 80 percent, an A at 5.0, giving 15 quality points.

Total credit units are 14. Total quality points are 61. The semester GPA is 61 divided by 14, which is 4.36. That places the student in the Second Class Upper Division band at the end of the first semester.

Now suppose the same student had a prior cumulative record from a previous semester of 22 credits and 94 quality points. Adding the two together gives 36 total credits and 155 total quality points. The cumulative CGPA is 4.31, still in the Second Class Upper range but closer to the First Class line than a single semester alone would suggest.

Carryovers and probation

Two academic rules catch students off guard, and both are worth understanding before they cost a semester.

The carryover is a failed course that must be retaken before graduation. An F grade gives zero quality points, but the credit units still sit in the total credit units registered. That is what pulls the CGPA down so sharply after a bad result: the denominator grows, the numerator stays the same. When the course is retaken and passed, the new quality points and credit units are added to cumulative totals. At most Nigerian universities, both the original fail and the new pass stay on the transcript and both count in the CGPA.

The academic probation rule says that any student whose semester GPA or cumulative CGPA drops below 1.50 for a single semester is placed on probation. If the CGPA does not rise above the probation line by the end of the following semester, the student can be required to withdraw from the programme. The exact threshold varies by university, so check your student handbook.

Common mistakes students make

Forgetting that F units count. A failed course still adds its credit units to the denominator. If a student fails a four-unit course, that failure drags the CGPA by more than a two-unit course would. Track failed units carefully and retake them as early as possible.

Mixing scales. A polytechnic student should use the NBTE 4.0 scale, and a university student should use the NUC 5.0 scale. Entering the wrong scale into the calculator produces a number that does not match the transcript.

Ignoring the prior record. A single semester GPA is not the same as a CGPA. Universities use the cumulative figure for honours classification, for probation decisions, and for postgraduate eligibility. Always enter the prior record if you want a useful number.

Assuming First Class is out of reach. The CGPA is a weighted average, and the weight of a strong semester grows with every additional credit unit added. A student with a low CGPA in 100 level can still climb into a higher band in 300 and 400 level if the trend improves steadily.

Not checking what counts toward CGPA. Some universities exclude remedial or elective courses from the CGPA. Others include a fourth attempt at a failed course with a capped grade. Check your faculty handbook before you assume the calculator is wrong.

Limits of the calculator

This tool computes what you tell it. It cannot see your transcript, it cannot verify grades, and it cannot know if your faculty applies a different weighting rule. It is a planning aid, not an official statement of your academic standing.

It also cannot predict a future semester with certainty, beyond letting you test scenarios. If you want to know whether a target CGPA is realistic, run the target forecaster with your current record and see what average the remaining semesters would need to hit. That is the fastest way to judge whether a goal is achievable before the semester starts.

Finally, the honours classification on this page is a projection, not a guarantee. Nigerian universities reserve the right to adjust honours bands in a given year, especially in borderline cases or where a faculty applies progressive upper-year weighting. Confirm the exact rules with your department before you plan your final year around them.

Frequently asked questions

How is CGPA calculated in Nigerian universities?

On the NUC 5.0 scale, CGPA is the total cumulative quality points divided by the total cumulative credit units registered. Each course earns quality points equal to its credit units multiplied by the grade point for its grade, where A is 5, B is 4, C is 3, D is 2, E is 1, and F is 0.

What CGPA is required for First Class Honours in Nigeria?

Under the NUC 5-point scale, First Class Honours requires a cumulative CGPA of 4.50 to 5.00. Second Class Upper Division runs from 3.50 to 4.49, Second Class Lower Division from 2.40 to 3.49, Third Class from 1.50 to 2.39, and a Pass degree from 1.00 to 1.49.

How does a carryover course affect CGPA?

A failed course earns zero quality points but its credit units remain in the total credit units registered. When the course is retaken and passed, the new grade points and credit units are added. At most Nigerian universities, both the original fail and the new pass remain on the transcript and both count in the CGPA denominator.

What is the difference between GPA and CGPA?

Grade Point Average covers a single semester: the semester total quality points divided by the semester total credit units. Cumulative Grade Point Average aggregates every semester completed from 100 level onward and is the figure used for degree classification.

What is the difference between the NUC 5.0 and NBTE 4.0 scales?

Nigerian universities under NUC regulation grade on a 5-point scale where A is 5 points and F is zero. Polytechnics under NBTE regulation use a 4-point scale with more grade bands, including AB at 3.5 points and BC at 2.5 points, so a candidate can score in between the main letters.

How is CGPA used for postgraduate admission in Nigeria?

Most Nigerian postgraduate programs require a minimum of Second Class Lower Division, which is a CGPA of 2.40 or higher on the NUC 5.0 scale. Competitive Master's programs typically ask for Second Class Upper Division at 3.50 or above, and some research programs require First Class.

Does continuous assessment count toward the final grade?

Yes. In Nigerian universities, the continuous assessment portion of a course, usually between 30 and 40 percent, combines with the final semester examination, usually 60 to 70 percent, to make the total score that maps to a letter grade.

Does this calculator store my grades?

No. Every calculation runs in your browser. No course name, credit unit, or grade is sent anywhere, and nothing is stored. The only part of the page that reaches the network is the AI insight button, and only when you press it.
