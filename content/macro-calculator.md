## Precision Nutrition & Flexible Macronutrient Planning

Whether your objective is sustainable fat loss, lean muscle hypertrophy, athletic endurance performance, or body recomposition, total daily calorie intake is only half the equation. The precise ratio of **Macronutrients—Protein, Carbohydrates, and Fats**—determines whether energy deficits burn body fat while preserving lean muscle mass, or whether calorie surpluses synthesize new muscle tissue rather than excess adipose storage.

AfriWidget’s **Macro Calculator** uses scientifically validated energy expenditure equations (Mifflin-St Jeor & Harris-Benedict) combined with flexible macronutrient split guidelines (IIFYM - If It Fits Your Macros) to calculate your ideal daily macronutrient targets in grams and calories.

## The Science of Macronutrient Split Calculations

Macronutrients are the energy-providing nutrients required by the body in large daily quantities. Each macronutrient serves specific physiological functions and provides a known caloric density:

- **Protein (4 Calories per gram)**: Essential for muscle tissue repair, enzyme synthesis, metabolic recovery, and satiety during calorie deficits.
- **Carbohydrate (4 Calories per gram)**: The body's primary fuel source for high-intensity exercise, glycogen replenishment, brain function, and central nervous system stamina.
- **Fat (9 Calories per gram)**: Crucial for hormone production (testosterone, estrogen, thyroid), cellular membrane integrity, joint health, and fat-soluble vitamin absorption (A, D, E, K).

## Energy Expenditure Mathematics: TDEE Baseline

To determine daily macronutrient targets, the calculator first determines your **Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)**:

1. **Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)**: Calculated using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation:
   - *For Men*: **BMR** = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age in years) + 5
   - *For Women*: **BMR** = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age in years) - 161

2. **TDEE Multiplier**: BMR is multiplied by an activity factor ranging from 1.2 (Sedentary) to 1.9 (Very Heavy Athletic Training).

3. **Goal Adjustment**:
   - **Weight Loss (Fat Loss / Cut)**: Creates a 15%–20% caloric deficit below TDEE.
   - **Maintenance**: Matches TDEE exactly for body recomposition and steady weight maintenance.
   - **Weight Gain (Muscle Building / Lean Bulk)**: Adds a 10%–15% caloric surplus above TDEE.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between counting macros and counting calories?
Counting calories tracks total energy intake, whereas counting macros tracks the specific breakdown of protein, carbohydrates, and fats that make up those calories. Counting macros ensures you consume sufficient protein for muscle retention and adequate healthy fats for hormone health, yielding superior body composition results compared to calorie tracking alone.

### How much protein do I need per day?
For active individuals and strength training, science recommends consuming 1.6 to 2.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight (0.7 to 1.0 grams per pound). During calorie deficits, higher protein intake (up to 2.4g/kg) helps prevent lean muscle tissue breakdown.

### Should I track raw or cooked food weights?
It is best to consistently weigh foods in their raw or dry state before cooking. Cooking alters water weight significantly (e.g., meat shrinks while rice expands), which can introduce tracking discrepancies if raw and cooked weights are mixed.

### How often should I recalculate my macros?
Recalculate your macronutrient targets every 4 to 6 weeks, or whenever your body weight changes by 3 to 5 kilograms. As your body weight drops during a cut, your Basal Metabolic Rate decreases slightly, requiring periodic macro adjustments.

### Is my physical and health data private?
Yes. AfriWidget executes all health and macro calculations entirely in client-side JavaScript. No age, weight, height, or gender metrics are stored, logged, or sent to external servers.
