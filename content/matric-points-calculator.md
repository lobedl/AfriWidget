## Comprehensive Guide to South African Matric Points, NSC Pass Levels, and Tertiary Qualifications

In South Africa, completing Grade 12 culminates in the awarding of the **National Senior Certificate (NSC)** by Umalusi, the official Council for Quality Assurance in General and Further Education and Training. Whether written through the Department of Basic Education (DBE public schools) or the Independent Examinations Board (IEB private schools), your final Matric results determine both your overall **Matric Points** and your legal **Pass Level Endorsement** (Bachelor’s Degree, Diploma, Higher Certificate, or NSC Pass).

## The Four Official South African Matric Pass Categories (Umalusi Standards)

To qualify for tertiary education at South African universities, universities of technology, or TVET colleges, learners must meet specific subject combinations and percentage thresholds established by the Higher Education Act and Umalusi regulations:

### 1. Bachelor’s Degree Pass (B &ndash; Formerly "Matric Exemption")
A Bachelor’s Degree Pass is the highest secondary school achievement level in South Africa. It legally qualifies you to apply for undergraduate degree programmes (such as MBChB, LLB, BCom, BSc, BA, and BEng) at all 26 public universities:
- Must achieve at least **40% (Level 3)** in your Home Language.
- Must achieve at least **50% (Level 4)** in four designated 20-credit subjects (excluding Life Orientation).
- Must achieve at least **30% (Level 2)** in two other subjects.
- Must pass at least **6 out of 7 subjects**.

### 2. Diploma Pass (D)
A Diploma Pass qualifies you to apply for 3-year National Diploma programmes at Universities of Technology (e.g., TUT, VUT, CPUT, DUT) and comprehensive universities:
- Must achieve at least **40% (Level 3)** in your Home Language.
- Must achieve at least **40% (Level 3)** in three other recognized 20-credit subjects.
- Must achieve at least **30% (Level 2)** in two other subjects.
- Must pass at least **6 out of 7 subjects**.

### 3. Higher Certificate Pass (HC)
A Higher Certificate Pass qualifies you to apply for 1-year Higher Certificate programmes and bridging courses at colleges and universities:
- Must achieve at least **40% (Level 3)** in your Home Language.
- Must achieve at least **40% (Level 3)** in two other recognized subjects.
- Must achieve at least **30% (Level 2)** in three other subjects.
- Must pass at least **6 out of 7 subjects**.

### 4. Senior Certificate / NSC Pass (Basic Pass)
The minimum legal pass to receive a Matric certificate without tertiary endorsement:
- Must achieve at least **40% (Level 3)** in your Home Language.
- Must achieve at least **40% (Level 3)** in two other subjects.
- Must achieve at least **30% (Level 2)** in three other subjects.
- You can fail one subject (scoring below 30%), provided your School-Based Assessment (SBA) portfolio is complete.

## Official DBE / Umalusi 7-Point Achievement Scale

The Department of Basic Education converts percentage marks into achievement levels:

| Level | Percentage Mark Range | Points Allocated | Rating Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Level 7** | **80% &ndash; 100%** | **7 Points** | Outstanding Achievement (Subject Distinction) |
| **Level 6** | **70% &ndash; 79%** | **6 Points** | Meritorious Achievement |
| **Level 5** | **60% &ndash; 69%** | **5 Points** | Substantial Achievement |
| **Level 4** | **50% &ndash; 59%** | **4 Points** | Moderate Achievement |
| **Level 3** | **40% &ndash; 49%** | **3 Points** | Adequate Achievement |
| **Level 2** | **30% &ndash; 39%** | **2 Points** | Elementary Achievement |
| **Level 1** | **0% &ndash; 29%** | **1 Point** (or 0 pts) | Not Achieved |

## Formula for Calculating Matric Points

The Matric points calculation is determined by summing the achievement level points across your subjects:

1. **Academic Core Matric Points (6 Subjects)**:
   **Core Matric Points** = Points(Home Language) + Points(First Additional Language) + Points(Mathematics or Maths Lit) + Points(Elective 1) + Points(Elective 2) + Points(Elective 3)
   *(Maximum: 42 Points)*

2. **Total Matric Points Including Life Orientation (7 Subjects)**:
   **Total Matric Points** = Core Matric Points + Points(Life Orientation)
   *(Maximum: 49 Points)*

3. **Average Matric Percentage**:
   **Average Percentage** = (Sum of all 7 percentage marks) ÷ 7

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What is the difference between Matric Points and Admission Point Score (APS)?
"Matric Points" represents the raw points generated across all your Grade 12 subjects on the standard 7-point NSC achievement scale and determines your legal certificate pass status (Bachelor, Diploma, or Higher Certificate). "APS" (Admission Point Score) is the specific calculation method used by individual universities to rank and screen applicants for faculty admissions, which may enforce specific subject weightings or exclude Life Orientation.

### What is the "Designated Subject List" in Matric?
Historically, the Department of Higher Education mandated that to achieve a Bachelor's pass, four of your subjects had to come from an approved "Designated Subject List" (e.g., Mathematics, Physical Sciences, Accounting, History, Languages). In 2018, the Minister of Basic Education officially eliminated the designated subject distinction; today, any recognized 20-credit NSC subject (excluding Life Orientation) passed at 50%+ counts toward your Bachelor's degree requirement.

### Can I get a Bachelor’s pass if I fail one subject?
Yes. Under DBE and Umalusi regulations, you only need to pass 6 out of your 7 subjects to obtain a National Senior Certificate. As long as you achieve at least 40% in your Home Language, at least 50% in four recognized subjects, and at least 30% in two other subjects, a failure in one elective (scoring below 30%) will not prevent you from obtaining a Bachelor's Degree Pass.

### Does a Level 7 in Life Orientation count as a distinction?
Yes. On your official Matric statement of results, any subject scored at 80% or above is recorded as an official NSC Subject Distinction (Level 7). However, keep in mind that many university faculties (such as Wits Health Sciences or UP Engineering) exclude Life Orientation when calculating your competitive faculty points.

### What should I do if I missed a Bachelor’s pass by one subject?
You have multiple official pathways:
1. **Apply for Re-marking or Re-checking**: If your mark is within 1% to 3% of the next grade level (e.g., 49% or 79%), you can request a DBE re-mark within 21 days of results release.
2. **Write the May/June Supplementary Exams**: The Department of Basic Education's *Second Chance Matric Programme* allows candidates to rewrite up to two subjects to upgrade their marks and pass endorsement.
3. **Enroll in a Higher Certificate or Bridging Course**: Completing a 1-year accredited Higher Certificate allows you to articulate directly into a Bachelor’s degree in your second year.
