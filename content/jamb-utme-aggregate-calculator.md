## Comprehensive Guide to Nigerian JAMB UTME Aggregate Scoring &amp; University Admissions

In Nigeria, admission into federal, state, and private universities is coordinated through the **Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board (JAMB)** via the Unified Tertiary Matriculation Examination (UTME). However, gaining admission into high-demand programmes (such as Medicine &amp; Surgery, Law, Nursing Science, Pharmacy, Mechanical Engineering, and Computer Science) requires understanding how universities compute composite **Aggregate Admission Scores** by combining your UTME score (out of 400), your Senior School Certificate Examination (SSCE / WAEC / NECO / NABTEB) O’Level grades, and institutional Post-UTME screening assessments.

The **AfriWidget JAMB UTME Aggregate Calculator** provides a transparent, standardized computation of your aggregate admission points. It implements the standard institutional evaluation metric: converting your UTME score to a base 50-point scale and your 5 core O'Level credit subjects to a 20-point scale for a combined 70-point aggregate benchmark.

## Formula for Calculating the JAMB Aggregate Score

The composite aggregate score on this platform is calculated as follows:

### 1. UTME Component (Maximum 50 Points)
Your raw JAMB UTME score (scaled out of 400) is scaled down to a 50-point basis:
**UTME Contribution** = Raw JAMB Score ÷ 8

*(For example: A JAMB score of 320 ÷ 8 = 40.0 Points out of 50).*

### 2. O’Level Component (Maximum 20 Points)
The 5 required O'Level subjects corresponding to your university faculty requirements (e.g., English, Mathematics, Biology, Chemistry, Physics) are converted using the 4.0-point scale:
**O'Level Contribution** = Points(Subject 1) + Points(Subject 2) + Points(Subject 3) + Points(Subject 4) + Points(Subject 5)

*(For example: Five A1 distinctions = 5 × 4.0 = 20.0 Points out of 20).*

### 3. Total Composite Aggregate Score (Maximum 70 Points)
**Total Aggregate Score** = UTME Contribution + O'Level Contribution

## Nigerian University Admission Quotas: Merit, Catchment &amp; ELDS

Federal Government guidelines mandate that university admissions in Nigeria are allocated across three primary quotas:
1. **Merit Quota (45%)**: Awarded strictly to the highest-scoring candidates nationwide, regardless of state of origin.
2. **Catchment Area Quota (35%)**: Allocated to candidates originating from the geographical catchment states associated with that federal university.
3. **Educationally Less Developed States (ELDS) Quota (20%)**: Reserved for candidates from designated states (such as Adamawa, Bauchi, Bayelsa, Ebonyi, Gombe, Jigawa, Kano, Katsina, Kebbi, Nasarawa, Niger, Rivers, Sokoto, Taraba, Yobe, and Zamfara) who meet baseline faculty cut-offs.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What is the minimum JAMB UTME cut-off mark for university admission in Nigeria?
The JAMB National Policy Meeting establishes the minimum baseline benchmark annually (typically 140 to 160 for universities, 100 to 120 for polytechnics, and 100 for colleges of education). However, top federal universities (such as UNILAG, UI, OAU, UNN, UNILORIN, and ABU) set their institutional minimums higher, typically at 200+ for degree programmes.

### Can I combine two O’Level sittings (e.g., WAEC and NECO)?
Most Nigerian federal and state universities permit two sittings (e.g., combining WAEC May/June with NECO June/July) to satisfy the five-credit requirement. However, some competitive faculties or institutions (such as University of Ibadan for Medicine) award maximum O'Level screening points only for results obtained in a single sitting.

### How do Post-UTME screening exams affect the aggregate score?
Certain universities (such as UNILAG, OAU, UNIBEN, and FUTA) conduct an independent Computer-Based Test (Post-UTME) scored out of 30 or 100 marks. In such institutions, the final 100% aggregate is computed as: 50% JAMB + 30% Post-UTME CBT + 20% O'Level points.

### What happens if I score a D7 or E8 in an elective subject?
Grades of D7, E8, or F9 are passes or fails and do not confer academic credit. For Nigerian university degree admissions, all five required prerequisite subjects must be passed at Credit level (C6 or better). A subject with D7 cannot be used in your 5-subject O'Level calculation.

### Does a higher aggregate guarantee admission?
Meeting the published departmental cut-off score gives you strong eligibility under the merit or catchment list. However, admission also depends on JAMB CAPS confirmation, programme quota limits set by regulatory bodies (e.g., MDCN for Medicine, Council of Legal Education for Law, COREN for Engineering), and timely acceptance on the JAMB portal.
