What this calculator does

This tool estimates the UNEB standing that the grades you enter would produce under the national standard. It covers the three UNEB levels that decide placements in Uganda: the Primary Leaving Examination at Primary 7, the Uganda Certificate of Education at Senior 4, and the Uganda Advanced Certificate of Education at Senior 6. Each level has its own grading scale and its own placement rules, so the three tabs handle them separately rather than forcing them into a single shape.

For PLE, the tool sums the four core subjects into an aggregate out of 36 and returns the official Division. For UCE, it selects the Best 8 from the ten subjects you can enter and returns the aggregate out of 72 with the corresponding Division. For UACE, it sums three Principal subjects, General Paper, and Subsidiary Mathematics or Subsidiary ICT into the twenty-point scale and reports the number of Principal Passes, which is the figure that direct university admission depends on.

Every calculation runs inside your browser. No grade, no subject name, and no personal detail is uploaded. If you press the AI insight button, only an anonymous summary of the level, the aggregate or points total, the Division or Principal Pass count, and the pathway band is sent to a small serverless function that runs once and then stops.

The UNEB grading scale

UNEB uses a nine-point scale at PLE and UCE, and a shorter six-point scale with two subsidiary slots at UACE. The PLE and UCE scale runs from Distinction 1 at one point down to Fail 9 at nine points, and the aggregate is the sum of the individual grades. Lower is better on this scale, so a candidate who earns D1 in every subject ends up with the smallest possible aggregate.

The UACE scale is different because the structure of the qualification is different. The three Principal subjects use a six-point scale from A at six down to O at one, with F contributing nothing, and the two subsidiary subjects each contribute one point for a pass. The maximum is twenty, reached with three Grade A Principal subjects plus passes on General Paper and the Subsidiary.

PLE grading and Senior 1 placement

The Primary Leaving Examination is taken at the end of Primary 7. Every candidate sits four papers: English Language, Mathematics, Integrated Science, and Social Studies with Religious Education. The grades are summed into an aggregate from 4 at the top down to 36 at the bottom. Division 1 covers 4 to 12, Division 2 covers 13 to 24, Division 3 covers 25 to 28, Division 4 covers 29 to 32, and an aggregate of 33 or above is ungraded.

The aggregate at PLE is the primary sorting instrument for Senior 1 placement. National schools, regional schools, and community schools publish their own cut-offs, and the more selective ones take candidates with the lowest aggregates. A Division 1 with an aggregate of 4 or 6 is competitive for the top-tier national boarding schools. A Division 1 with an aggregate of 10 or 12 is competitive for strong regional and district schools, and the placement is more sensitive to the specific subject strengths and to the schools' own preferences than to the aggregate alone.

A candidate with a Division 2 aggregate is normally placed in a traditional secondary school or a well-run private school, and a candidate with a Division 3 or Division 4 aggregate enters the community school system or a BTVET skills training programme. The ungraded band is a signal that the candidate should consider repeating Primary 7 or moving into a non-formal vocational track with a clear upgrade path.

UCE Best 8 and the Division rules

The Uganda Certificate of Education is taken at the end of Senior 4. Candidates sit between eight and ten subjects, and UNEB selects the Best 8 that satisfy the subject grouping rules. English Language and Mathematics are compulsory in the counted eight. At least one science and at least one humanities subject must be included. The remaining slots are filled with the strongest of the other subjects on the list, whether those are additional sciences, humanities, languages, or electives.

The aggregate is the sum of the eight grades, running from 8 at the top down to 72 at the bottom. The Division rules then apply on top of the aggregate, and they involve more conditions than a simple banding of the total. Division 1 requires an aggregate between 8 and 32, with a minimum of seven credits at C6 or better, plus passes in English, Mathematics, and at least one science or humanities subject depending on the school's internal rules. Division 2 requires an aggregate between 33 and 45 with at least six credits. Division 3 requires an aggregate between 46 and 58 with at least three credits or eight passes. Division 4 requires an aggregate between 59 and 72 with at least one credit or two passes.

The Division rules are cumulative. A candidate who reaches a Division 1 aggregate of 30 but only has six credits falls into Division 2, because the seven-credit condition is separate from the aggregate. The same applies to the English, Mathematics, and science conditions. All of the conditions for a Division must hold at the same time for the candidate to be placed in that Division.

Beyond the Division itself, the specific grades in the specific subjects matter for Senior 5 admission. A candidate who wants to take a science combination such as PCM, PCB, or BCM needs Credit 6 or better in the relevant sciences and in Mathematics, and different schools apply slightly different thresholds. A candidate who wants to take an arts combination such as HEL, HEG, or DEG needs Credit grades in History, Geography, Literature, or the relevant languages. The Division opens the door, and the subject grades determine which combinations are available inside it.

UACE and the twenty-point scale

The Uganda Advanced Certificate of Education is taken at the end of Senior 6. Three Principal subjects are scored on the six-point scale where A earns six, B earns five, C earns four, D earns three, E earns two, and O earns one. A Grade F contributes nothing. General Paper and Subsidiary Mathematics or Subsidiary ICT each add one point for a subsidiary pass, and the maximum total is twenty points.

The number of Principal Passes, not the total alone, is what direct university admission depends on. A Principal Pass is a Grade E or better on a Principal subject, which is two points or more on the scale. Two Principal Passes at the same sitting is the minimum for direct degree entry under the framework published by the National Council for Higher Education, and the public universities apply it. A candidate with one Principal Pass can enter a diploma programme, and a candidate with subsidiary passes only can enter a certificate programme or a skills-based pathway.

Public university admission for degree programmes uses the total alongside subject weights. The Public Universities Joint Admissions Board applies weightings to the three Principal subjects based on how relevant each is to the specific programme, adds points for the O-Level record, and applies the female affirmative bonus. The twenty-point UACE total is the foundation of that calculation, and the weighted total that determines the cut-off for a specific programme is built on top of it.

UACE total = (Principal 1) + (Principal 2) + (Principal 3) + General Paper + Subsidiary Maths or ICT
Maximum = 20 points (three Grade A Principals plus two subsidiary passes)
Direct degree entry minimum = 2 Principal Passes at the same sitting

A worked example at each level

Primary Leaving Examination example

A candidate expected to earn Distinction 2 in English, Distinction 1 in Mathematics, Distinction 1 in Integrated Science, and Distinction 2 in Social Studies and Religious Education has an aggregate of two plus one plus one plus two, which equals six. That places the candidate in Division 1 with the second-lowest aggregate the scale allows, and the placement outlook is competitive for national boarding schools. If the same candidate had earned Credit 4 in English instead of Distinction 2, the aggregate would be eight, still inside Division 1 but with a different profile for the schools that apply their own subject thresholds.

UCE example

Take a candidate who sat ten subjects with the following grades: English Language C4, Mathematics C3, Biology C5, Chemistry C6, Physics D2, Geography C4, History P7, CRE C6, Commerce P8, and Luganda C5. The Best 8 with English and Mathematics included, and with the science and humanities conditions satisfied, takes the eight lowest-point grades from this list. The first seven are Physics at 2, Mathematics at 3, English at 4, Geography at 4, Biology at 5, Luganda at 5, and Chemistry at 6 or CRE at 6. The eighth is whichever of Chemistry or CRE is kept, and the other one plus History and Commerce drop out.

The aggregate is 2 plus 3 plus 4 plus 4 plus 5 plus 5 plus 6 plus 6, which equals 35. That falls in the Division 2 band of 33 to 45. The candidate has seven credits or better across the counted eight, which satisfies the credit condition for Division 1, but the aggregate of 35 is above the Division 1 ceiling of 32, so the Division is 2. If the candidate had moved History up to C6 and dropped Commerce entirely, the aggregate would improve by only a point because History already sits at 7 in the current selection. A stronger performance in one of the counted subjects, particularly Physics or Mathematics, moves the aggregate further than a change in a discarded subject.

UACE example

A candidate with Grade A in Physics, Grade B in Chemistry, Grade A in Mathematics, a pass on Subsidiary ICT, and a pass on General Paper has a total of six plus five plus six plus one plus one, which equals nineteen. That is three Principal Passes and one point short of the maximum, and the profile is competitive for the top engineering and science programmes at Makerere and at the other public universities, subject to the specific weightings that each programme applies. If the same candidate had earned a Grade E in one Principal subject instead of a Grade A, the total would drop to fifteen and the profile would still clear the two Principal Pass minimum, but the competitive position on the more selective programmes would shift accordingly.

Common mistakes when forecasting UNEB results

A handful of recurring errors make a candidate's own estimate diverge from what UNEB will actually record, and some of them affect the placement decision that follows.

Assuming the aggregate is the only condition for a Division. At UCE, the Division depends on the aggregate and on the credit, English, Mathematics, and science conditions. A candidate who checks only the aggregate can be surprised when the Division comes back lower than expected. The tool audits each condition and reports the outcome UNEB would record.

Forgetting that the Best 8 has fixed subjects. English and Mathematics are always counted, regardless of the grade earned. A candidate whose weakest grades are in English and Mathematics still has those subjects in the Best 8. A strong performance in an elective does not displace a compulsory subject from the counted eight.

Treating mock grades as identical to UNEB grades. Joint Mock examinations such as the Wakiso, Kampala, Masaka, and Mukono mocks are designed to mirror the national difficulty, and the correlation with final results is usually within one to three aggregate points. The mock grade is not the national grade, and the examiners apply their own moderation to the actual papers. The tool shows you what the same grades would produce under the national standard, which is the relevant reference for planning.

Overlooking the same-sitting rule at UACE. Both Principal Passes for direct degree entry must come from the same UACE sitting. A candidate who earns one Principal Pass in one year and the second in the next year cannot combine them for direct entry, and the realistic alternative is a diploma route that bridges to a degree later.

Ignoring General Paper and Subsidiary in UACE. General Paper and Subsidiary Mathematics or Subsidiary ICT each contribute one point to the twenty-point total. A fail on either removes the point and reduces the total, and on competitive programmes where the cut-off margin is a fraction of a point, one point can decide the offer. The two subsidiary subjects are not the main event, but they are not negligible either.

Reading the Division as the final placement. The Division determines whether Senior 5 admission is available and at which institutions. The specific subject grades determine which combinations are available inside that Division. A candidate with Division 1 but weak science grades may be limited to arts combinations, and a candidate with Division 2 but very strong science grades may be offered science combinations at a school that takes the subject profile into account. The two levels of decision are separate, and the tool reports both.

From result to placement

The UNEB result is the input to a placement process that happens at three points: Senior 1, Senior 5, and university entry. Each placement has its own rules, and each has its own way of using the UNEB output.

Senior 1 placement is managed by the Ministry of Education and Sports, which publishes national cut-offs and coordinates the placement of Primary 7 candidates into government and government-aided secondary schools. Candidates who prefer a private school or a specific institution apply directly and are admitted under that school's own selection criteria.

Senior 5 placement is done by the individual secondary schools. Each school publishes the A-Level combinations it offers and the UCE grade requirements for each combination. Candidates apply to their preferred schools directly, and the schools select on the basis of the Division, the specific subject grades in the subjects that make up the A-Level combination, and their own capacity for each combination.

University entry runs through the PUJAB system for the public universities. The UACE total is combined with subject weights and O-Level points to produce the weighted score used for the cut-off on each programme, and candidates are ranked on that score. The twenty-point UACE total is the foundation of the ranking, and the additional components shift the final figure by a few points either way on competitive programmes.

Where this calculator stops

This tool estimates the aggregate, Division, or twenty-point total from the grades you enter, applying the standard UNEB scale and the published Division rules. It does not know the specific school cut-offs for Senior 1 placement, the specific combination requirements that each Senior 5 school applies, or the specific programme cut-offs that each university publishes in the PUJAB cycle. Those details change year on year and are published by the receiving institutions, not by UNEB itself.

The grades you enter are the grades you expect, and the estimate is what those grades would produce under the national standard. Actual results depend on the papers the candidate sits, the moderation the examiners apply, and the performance on the day. Use this calculator to see where the current trajectory points, to understand which subject would improve the aggregate the most if it moved up a grade, and to plan the placement decisions with a clear view of the range of outcomes the current grades support.

Frequently asked questions

How is the PLE aggregate calculated?

The Primary Leaving Examination is taken at the end of Primary 7. Every candidate sits four papers: English Language, Mathematics, Integrated Science, and Social Studies with Religious Education. Each paper is graded from D1 at 1 point down to F9 at 9 points, and the four grades are summed into an aggregate from 4 at the top of the scale down to 36 at the bottom. Lower is better. Division 1 covers aggregates from 4 to 12, Division 2 covers 13 to 24, Division 3 covers 25 to 28, Division 4 covers 29 to 32, and an aggregate of 33 or above is ungraded.

How is the UCE Best 8 aggregate calculated?

The Uganda Certificate of Education is taken at the end of Senior 4. Candidates typically sit between eight and ten subjects, and UNEB selects the eight best grades that satisfy the subject grouping rules. English Language and Mathematics must be included, and the subject combination rules require at least one science and at least one humanities subject in the counted eight. Each subject is graded on the stanine scale from D1 at 1 point down to F9 at 9 points, and the eight grades are summed into an aggregate from 8 at the top down to 72 at the bottom. Lower is better.

What are the UCE Division thresholds?

Division 1 requires an aggregate between 8 and 32 with a minimum of seven credits at C6 or better, plus passes in English, Mathematics, and at least one science or humanities subject depending on the school's internal rules. Division 2 requires an aggregate between 33 and 45 with a minimum of six credits. Division 3 requires an aggregate between 46 and 58 with at least three credits or eight passes. Division 4 requires an aggregate between 59 and 72 with at least one credit or two passes. An aggregate above 72, or fewer than two passes, results in an ungraded certificate.

How is the UACE twenty-point score calculated?

The Uganda Advanced Certificate of Education is taken at the end of Senior 6. Three Principal subjects are scored on a six-point scale where A earns six, B earns five, C earns four, D earns three, E earns two, and O earns one, for a maximum of eighteen points from the three Principals. General Paper and Subsidiary Mathematics or Subsidiary ICT each add one point for a subsidiary pass, taking the maximum to twenty. The score is used by the Public Universities Joint Admissions Board, known as PUJAB, along with subject weights and O-Level points to produce the university cut-off figure.

What is the minimum for direct university admission in Uganda?

Under the framework published by the National Council for Higher Education and applied by the public universities, the minimum for direct entry into a Bachelor's degree programme is two Principal Passes obtained at the same sitting of the UACE examination. A Principal Pass means a Grade E or better on a Principal subject, which is two points or more on the UNEB scale. Candidates with one Principal Pass can enter a diploma programme, and candidates with subsidiary passes only can enter a certificate or skills training programme.

Can mock examination scores accurately predict final UNEB results?

Regional Joint Mock examinations such as the Wakiso, Kampala, Masaka, and Mukono mocks are designed to mirror the difficulty and coverage of the national papers, and the statistical correlation between mock grades and final UNEB results is typically within one to three points on the aggregate. The estimator applies the standard UNEB bell curve and grade boundaries to the grades you enter, and the range you get is the range the same grades would produce under the national standard. The actual result depends on the specific papers sat, the moderation the examiners apply, and the candidate's performance on the day, so the estimate is a planning figure rather than a guarantee.

What happens if a candidate fails General Paper or Subsidiary Mathematics?

A fail on General Paper or Subsidiary Mathematics removes the point that the subject would have contributed to the twenty-point total. The candidate still receives the UACE certificate provided the two Principal Pass rule is met, and the university applications proceed with the reduced total. A strong performance on the three Principal subjects can compensate for a lost subsidiary point, because the Principals carry eighteen of the twenty possible points.

Does this calculator store my grades or share my results?

No. Every calculation runs inside your browser tab and nothing is stored. If you press the AI insight button, only an anonymous summary of the exam level, the aggregate or points total, the Division or Principal Pass count, and the pathway band is sent to a small serverless function that runs once and then stops. Your subject names, individual grades, and any personal identifiers never leave the browser.
