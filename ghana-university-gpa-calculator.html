Understanding GPA and CWA Systems in Ghanaian Higher Education

Tertiary education in Ghana operates under the regulatory oversight of the Ghana Tertiary Education Commission (GTEC). Public universities, technical universities, and accredited private colleges utilize two primary computational methodologies to determine academic merit and award Bachelor's degrees: the Grade Point Average (GPA) system and the Cumulative Weighted Average (CWA) system.

Understanding how credit weighting and grading bands operate is crucial for semester planning, maintaining scholarship requirements, avoiding academic probation, and aiming for postgraduate admission benchmarks worldwide.

UG

UG Legon & UPSA 4.0 Scale

University of Ghana (Legon), University of Professional Studies Accra (UPSA), Ghana Communication Technology University (GCTU), and most private universities use the 4.0 letter grade point multiplier.

  • • Grade A (80–100%) = 4.0 Grade Points
  • • Grade B+ (75–79%) = 3.5 Grade Points
  • • Grade B (70–74%) = 3.0 Grade Points
  • • Grade C+ (65–69%) = 2.5 Grade Points
  • • Grade C (60–64%) = 2.0 Grade Points
  • • Grade D+ (55–59%) = 1.5 Grade Points
  • • Grade D (50–54%) = 1.0 Grade Point (Pass)
  • • Grade E (45–49%) = 0.5 Grade Points (Trail)
  • • Grade F (0–44%) = 0.0 Grade Points (Fail)
KN

KNUST & UCC CWA Scale

Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology (KNUST), University of Cape Coast (UCC), University of Mines and Technology (UMaT), and University of Energy and Natural Resources (UENR) calculate Cumulative Weighted Average percentage directly from raw examination marks.

  • • First Class Honours = 70.00% and above
  • • Second Class Upper = 60.00% to 69.99%
  • • Second Class Lower = 50.00% to 59.99%
  • • Pass Degree = 40.00% to 49.99%
  • • Fail / Trailed Course = Below 40.00% (or <50% for core professional modules)

Step-by-Step Computation Formulas (Zero LaTeX Plaintext)

1. Semester Grade Point Average (GPA) Formula

Each course carries a specific number of Credit Hours (typically 1 to 4 credits). The quality points earned for each course equal the course credit hours multiplied by the numeric grade point of the letter grade achieved:

Quality Points for Course = Credit Hours × Grade Point Value
Total Semester Quality Points = Sum of (Credit Hours × Grade Point Value for all courses)
Semester GPA = Total Semester Quality Points ÷ Total Semester Credit Hours

2. Cumulative Weighted Average (CWA) Formula

In the CWA system, your numeric score (0 to 100) is multiplied directly by the credit hours assigned to that module:

Weighted Score for Course = Credit Hours × Raw Percentage Mark
Total Weighted Score = Sum of (Credit Hours × Raw Percentage Mark for all courses)
Semester CWA = Total Weighted Score ÷ Total Registered Credit Hours

3. Practical Worked Example (UG Legon Bachelor of Science Student)

Consider a Level 200 student taking 5 courses totaling 15 credit hours:

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What happens if I trail a course (Grade E or F) in a Ghanaian university? ▼

In Ghanaian universities, a grade of F (or E in prerequisite modules) is considered a trail. While you earn 0 grade points, the credit hours are still included in your total registered semester credits, which pulls down your GPA/CWA significantly. You must re-register and re-sit the course during supplementary exams or the next academic cycle to replace or clear the failed module.

How do I calculate what grades I need to move from Second Class Lower to Second Class Upper? ▼

Check the "Combine with Previous Cumulative Standing" box on our calculator. Input your completed credit hours and current CGPA (for instance, 45 credits at 2.85 CGPA). Then simulate scoring A's and B+'s in your upcoming 18 credits. The calculator will instantly display your projected new CGPA.

Can I use this calculator for Technical Universities in Ghana? ▼

Yes. Accredited Ghanaian Technical Universities (such as Accra Technical University, Kumasi Technical University, Takoradi Technical University, Ho Technical University, Cape Coast Technical University) use either the 4.0 GPA or CWA scale according to their respective academic boards. Select the appropriate calculation system on the tool.
