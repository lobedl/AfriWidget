How this university eligibility checker works

Students often search for a cut-off point because they want a quick answer to a difficult question: does my score make this course realistic? This checker turns that question into a transparent comparison across 27 universities in Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, South Africa, Uganda, and Tanzania. It includes 22 popular programmes per university, grouped into Health, Tech, Engineering, Business, Law, and Humanities & Sciences. It is deliberately labelled a planning tool because actual admissions rules change by year, programme, campus, subject combination, quotas, and applicant demand.

Choose the system printed on your result. JAMB UTME runs from 0 to 400 and is stronger when higher. WASSCE aggregates and ACSEE admission points use a lower-is-better comparison. KCSE programme points, South African APS, and UACE points are stronger when higher, but each university can apply subject clusters, NBTs, faculty rules, or an annual selection list. The page changes its institutions, course baselines, score range, and comparison direction when the system changes.

Understanding the three statuses

High Admission Chance means the entered score exceeds the selected planning baseline by at least two points. Borderline / Highly Competitive means the score matches the baseline or is only one point stronger; cut-offs can vary yearly based on quotas, demand, and programme capacity. Below Estimated Cut-off means the result is weaker than the displayed baseline, so the checker shows alternative programmes at the same university when possible. These labels are not admission promises.

The two-point band is applied in the correct direction for each scoring system: higher scores need to be at least two points above the baseline, while lower-is-better aggregates need to be at least two points below it. A score exactly on the baseline is therefore competitive rather than high chance. This makes the result easy to audit, but it cannot replace a current official selection list or faculty decision.

Why official requirements still matter

A course baseline can be only one part of eligibility. Universities may require specific grades in English, Mathematics, or sciences, a valid combination of examination sittings, a subject cluster, an NBT, a post-UTME, an interview, a portfolio, or a national placement process. Some official pages publish a programme cut-off table, while others publish only minimum entry requirements and tell applicants to verify the current cycle. The source link and confidence note shown under each result tell you which situation applies.

Use the result responsibly

Enter the score exactly as it appears on the official result. If you are still waiting for results, treat the output as a scenario rather than a prediction. Use the category filter and searchable university and course fields to compare options quickly, but do not use an old cut-off list as if it were current. Every displayed baseline is a dated planning reference and should be checked against the linked official admissions page before an application decision.

Frequently asked questions

Does High Admission Chance guarantee admission?

No. It means only that the entered score is at least two points beyond the displayed planning baseline. Subject requirements, competition, spaces, and official rules still apply.

Why are the scoring systems compared differently?

JAMB, KCSE, APS, and UACE points are normally stronger when higher. WASSCE and ACSEE aggregate-style scales are normally stronger when lower. The checker preserves those directions.

Can I trust the baseline as an official guarantee?

No. Some rows link to official programme cut-off tables; others link to official admissions requirements where no fixed departmental cut-off is published. Always verify the current cycle with the university.

Build a shortlist, not a single prediction

A useful admissions shortlist usually contains a mix of ambitious, competitive, and safer choices. Use the status card to identify where your score appears to sit against a baseline, then verify the complete entry requirements for each course. A below-threshold result does not mean there are no academic options; it may mean that another institution, related programme, or future application route deserves investigation.

Keep the source and date of every cut-off you review. University websites, prospectuses, examination policies, and application portals should take priority over copied lists or old social posts. If the course is regulated or professional, check any additional registration or subject requirements too. This checker is most useful when it starts a careful research process rather than ending one.

You can also use the checker before results are released by entering different scenarios. Label those results as estimates, and revisit them when the official score and current course requirements are published. Save the official source beside your notes so you can update the comparison when a new admissions cycle begins.
