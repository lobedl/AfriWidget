What this HELB repayment calculator measures

This tool takes your outstanding Higher Education Loans Board (HELB) loan balance, your planned monthly repayment installment, your loan category interest rate, and the monthly administrative ledger fee, then simulates repayment month by month on a reducing-balance schedule. It calculates the exact number of monthly installments required to reach a zero balance, cumulative interest charged across the cycle, cumulative ledger maintenance fees, and total Kenyan shillings repaid from start to closure.

Many Kenyan university and TVET graduates underestimate the lifetime cost of slow repayment because a 4.0 per cent annual interest rate sounds negligible next to commercial bank personal loans priced at 16 to 22 per cent. What changes the arithmetic is the combination of compound time and the fixed KES 100 monthly ledger maintenance fee. On a standard KES 160,000 undergraduate balance at 4.0 per cent per year, paying only the statutory floor of KES 1,500 per month stretches repayment across 145 months (twelve years and one month) and adds KES 41,756 in interest plus KES 14,500 in ledger fees—bringing extra charges to KES 56,256 (35.2 per cent above the principal) and total repayment to KES 216,256. Raising that monthly deduction to KES 3,500 clears the identical debt in 52 months (four years and four months) and trims combined interest and ledger fees to KES 19,529, saving KES 36,727 in cash charges and nearly eight years of payroll deductions.

Every simulation runs locally inside your browser tab. No National ID number, KRA PIN, payslip figure, or employer name is requested or stored. If you click the optional AI insight button after running a schedule, only an anonymous numeric summary of the loan scheme, principal, monthly installment, payoff duration, total interest, and total fees is sent once to generate short commentary.

How HELB financing works under Kenya's Student-Centred Funding Model

The Higher Education Loans Board operates under the Higher Education Loans Board Act (Chapter 213A, Laws of Kenya) as the statutory revolving fund financing Kenyan students enrolled in chartered public and private universities, Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) polytechnics, Kenya Medical Training College (KMTC) campuses, and accredited postgraduate or professional programmes. Repayments collected from past cohorts directly recapitalise disbursements for incoming students.

Under the Student-Centred Funding Model administered jointly through the Higher Education Financing (HEF) portal, the Universities Fund, and HELB, total programme funding is no longer allocated as a flat block grant per student. Instead, applicants are evaluated using the Means Testing Instrument (MTI)—which weighs household income, family size, secondary school category, orphanhood or disability status, and affirmative-action indicators—and placed into one of five household need bands (Band 1 through Band 5). Total assessed cost (tuition plus student upkeep) is then split into three distinct streams:

  • Government Scholarship (Universities Fund / Ministry of Education): A non-repayable grant paid directly to the university or TVET college. It carries zero interest and never appears on your post-graduation HELB loan statement.
  • Household Contribution: The direct out-of-pocket fee share paid by the student's family or sponsor to the institution each academic year, scaling from 5 per cent in Band 1 up to 40 per cent in Band 5.
  • HELB Student Loan (Tuition plus Upkeep): The repayable credit stream covering both institutional tuition loan allocations and direct-to-student upkeep stipends (typically KES 40,000 to KES 60,000 per year for undergraduates depending on band). Only this third stream enters your HELB loan ledger, carries the subsidised 4.0 per cent annual interest rate, and requires post-graduation repayment.

Because Band 4 and Band 5 students receive a smaller non-repayable scholarship share and a larger loan share on high-cost clinical, engineering, or architectural degrees, cumulative four-year undergraduate loan balances now vary wider than under the pre-2023 differentiated unit cost system. Exact reducing-balance interest, ledger fee, and coverage formulas

Unlike flat-rate microfinance or SACCO development loans that compute interest on the original principal for every year of the contract, HELB amortises student loans on a monthly reducing balance. Each monthly remittance you or your employer sends is allocated in strict priority order: first to that month's accrued interest, second to the KES 100 administrative ledger maintenance fee, and third—whatever remains—directly to reducing your outstanding principal balance.

Monthly Interest Rate (r_m) = (Annual Rate % / 100) / 12
Interest Charged in Month t = Opening Balance_t x r_m
Principal Repaid in Month t = Monthly Installment - Interest_t - Monthly Ledger Fee
Closing Balance_t = Opening Balance_t - Principal Repaid_t
Minimum Non-Negative Installment Floor = ceil((Opening Principal x r_m) + Ledger Fee + 1)

Understanding how these five equations interact explains two behaviours that regularly surprise borrowers when they inspect an annual HELB statement:

  • The effective cost rate on small installments exceeds 4.0 per cent: On an opening balance of KES 80,000 (typical for a two-year TVET diploma), monthly interest at 4.0 per cent per annum is KES 266.67, while the KES 100 monthly ledger fee is equivalent to an extra 1.50 per cent annualised charge in Month 1—and as your balance shrinks to KES 20,000 near the end of the loan, that same KES 100 monthly fee equals 6.00 per cent annualised on the remaining debt. Shortening the number of active months is the only way to eliminate recurring ledger charges.
  • Every installment shifts progressively toward principal: Because next month's interest is multiplied against a smaller closing balance, the interest slice shrinks every single month even when your payroll deduction stays constant. On a KES 160,000 loan paid at KES 3,500 per month, principal repayment rises from KES 2,867 in Month 1 (81.9 per cent of your payment) to KES 3,115 in Month 26 (89.0 per cent) and KES 3,352 in Month 48 (95.8 per cent).

Notice the fifth line in the formula block: the minimum non-negative installment floor. If your monthly remittance is smaller than or equal to Month 1 interest plus the KES 100 ledger fee, zero shillings reach principal and the debt never amortises. For a KES 160,000 undergraduate loan at 4.0 per cent, Month 1 interest (KES 533.33) plus ledger fee (KES 100) equals KES 633.33, so the KES 1,500 statutory minimum safely clears the hurdle. By contrast, on a KES 450,000 Band 2 degree balance at 4.0 per cent, Month 1 interest is KES 1,500 plus the KES 100 ledger fee—totalling KES 1,600. Paying only KES 1,500 per month on a KES 450,000 balance causes KES 100 of negative amortisation in the first month; the borrower must pay at least KES 1,601 to shrink the balance, and realistically KES 4,500 to KES 7,500 per month to clear the debt within a practical career horizon.

Comparing undergraduate, TVET, postgraduate, and salaried schemes

Not every HELB account carries the 4.0 per cent subsidised undergraduate rate. Over the past decade, HELB has expanded beyond direct pre-service university loans to administer TVET artisan and diploma credit, postgraduate master's and doctoral scholarships/loans, civil servant training revolving funds, and salaried continuing-education products such as the Jielimishe loan scheme. Selecting the wrong category in a repayment projection distorts both your required minimum payment and total interest by a factor of three.

Key structural differences across HELB's major lending windows include:

  • Undergraduate Direct & TVET Diploma/Craft Loans (4.0% p.a.): Targeted at pre-service students placed via KUCCPS or admitted to accredited universities and TVET institutions. Carries the twelve-month post-completion grace period and KES 100 monthly ledger fee.
  • Postgraduate Master's & PhD Loans (10.0% p.a.): Offered to graduates pursuing recognised master's or doctoral degrees in Kenyan universities, typically capped between KES 200,000 and KES 450,000 across two to three years. Interest accrues at 10.0 per cent per annum on a reducing balance, meaning Month 1 interest alone on a KES 300,000 balance is KES 2,500 before the KES 100 ledger fee.
  • Salaried / Jielimishe & Partner Revolving Schemes (12.0% p.a.): Designed for employed learners financing part-time degrees, professional certifications (such as CPA, CPS, ACCA, or higher diplomas), or institutional partner funds. Unlike pre-service undergraduate loans, salaried Jielimishe facilities normally enter check-off payroll repayment immediately upon disbursement (no twelve-month grace period) over fixed tenors of 12 to 48 months, and often require guarantors or employer salary check-off undertakings.

Step-by-step worked HELB repayment examples

The four real-world scenarios below walk through exact shilling-by-shilling outputs from the calculator so you can verify your own numbers or adapt them to your payslip budget.

Example 1: Standard undergraduate graduate comparing KES 3,500 vs. KES 8,000 per month

Wanjiku finishes a four-year Bachelor of Commerce degree with a cumulative HELB loan balance of KES 160,000 at 4.0 per cent per annum and a KES 100 monthly ledger fee. She starts formal employment at the end of her grace period and authorises payroll to deduct KES 3,500 per month.

  • Month 1: Interest on KES 160,000 at (4.0% / 12) = KES 533.33. Adding the KES 100 ledger fee consumes KES 633.33, so KES 2,866.67 reduces principal, leaving a closing balance of KES 157,133.33.
  • Month 2: Interest on KES 157,133.33 drops to KES 523.78. After the KES 100 ledger fee, principal repaid rises to KES 2,876.22, bringing closing balance to KES 154,257.11.
  • Full cycle at KES 3,500/month: The loan reaches zero in 52 months (4 years, 4 months), accumulating KES 14,329 in interest and KES 5,200 in ledger fees for a total outlay of KES 179,529.
  • Accelerated comparison at KES 8,000/month: After a promotion, if Wanjiku had paid KES 8,000 per month from day one, payoff drops to 22 months (1 year, 10 months), interest falls to KES 5,933, ledger fees fall to KES 2,200, and total repayment drops to KES 168,133—saving KES 11,396 and 30 months of deductions.

Example 2: Self-employed TVET electrical technician paying via M-Pesa

Otieno completes a two-year national diploma at a national polytechnic with a total HELB TVET balance of KES 80,000 at 4.0 per cent per annum. Working as an independent solar and electrical contractor without a formal employer payroll, he sets a standing M-Pesa reminder to remit KES 1,500 per month starting in Month 12 after graduation.

At KES 1,500 per month, Month 1 covers KES 266.67 in interest plus KES 100 in ledger fees, sending KES 1,133.33 to principal. Over the full amortisation schedule, Otieno clears the loan in 64 months (5 years, 4 months), paying KES 8,898 in interest and KES 6,400 in ledger fees (KES 95,298 total). Notice that on smaller TVET balances paid slowly, cumulative KES 100 ledger fees (KES 6,400) amount to 71.9 per cent of the entire interest bill. Doubling his monthly M-Pesa remittance to KES 3,000 clears the account in 29 months (2 years, 5 months) and cuts combined interest and fees from KES 15,298 down to KES 6,960—saving KES 8,338.

Example 3: Postgraduate master's borrower at 10.0 per cent per annum

Achieng takes a KES 300,000 HELB postgraduate loan at 10.0 per cent per annum to complete a two-year Master of Public Health degree. Because 10.0 per cent divided by 12 equals 0.8333 per cent per month, her Month 1 interest alone is KES 2,500, plus the KES 100 ledger fee—meaning any payment of KES 2,600 or less would fail to reduce her balance by a single shilling. Selecting KES 7,500 per month allocates KES 4,900 to principal in Month 1 and clears the KES 300,000 facility in 50 months (4 years, 2 months), with KES 67,603 in total interest and KES 5,000 in ledger fees (KES 372,603 total repaid). Because her rate is 10 per cent rather than 4 per cent, prepaying HELB delivers a guaranteed 10 per cent risk-free return—competitive with after-tax yields on Kenyan money market funds and Treasury bills.

Example 4: Applying a KES 30,000 SACCO dividend lump sum to a KES 200,000 loan

Mutua owes KES 200,000 on an undergraduate loan (4.0 per cent p.a., KES 100 fee) and pays KES 3,500 per month via payroll. Left untouched, that schedule runs 66 months (5 years, 6 months) and costs KES 22,991 in interest plus KES 6,600 in ledger fees (KES 29,591 extra cost; KES 229,591 total). When he receives an annual SACCO dividend, he pays a one-off KES 30,000 lump sum via Paybill 200800, immediately dropping his principal to KES 170,000 while keeping his KES 3,500 payroll check-off unchanged. You can model this directly in the calculator by entering KES 170,000 as the new balance: remaining payoff falls to 55 months (4 years, 7 months) with KES 16,279 in interest and KES 5,500 in ledger fees—saving KES 7,812 in future interest and ledger charges and eliminating 11 full months of deductions.

Statutory payroll deductions, employer deadlines, and the 25% cap

Repayment compliance in Kenya is governed by strict statutory duties placed on both graduates and employers under Sections 15, 16, and 17 of the Higher Education Loans Board Act (Cap 213A):

  • Loanees' duty to notify and service: Within twelve months of completing studies (or upon entering employment), a beneficiary must inform HELB of their postal/digital contact and employer details and ensure monthly repayments commence once the grace period lapses.
  • Employer duty to inquire and deduct: Kenyan employers hiring graduates from tertiary institutions are legally obligated to check HELB beneficiary status during onboarding, deduct the billed monthly installment from payroll, and remit those funds to HELB on or before the 15th day of the following calendar month.
  • Statutory 25 per cent basic salary ceiling: Under the HELB Act, mandatory payroll deductions for HELB loan recovery are capped so they do not exceed one-quarter (25 per cent) of the employee's monthly basic salary, unless the employee voluntarily instructs payroll in writing to deduct a higher sum to accelerate clearance.
  • Separate employer vs. employee penalties: If an employer deducts HELB from your payslip (or fails to deduct after formal billing notice) and misses the 15th-of-the-month remittance deadline, HELB levies a penalty of 5 per cent of the unremitted deduction per month directly against the employer. Conversely, if a graduate ignores their account past the twelve-month grace period with no active remittance, the loanee incurs a statutory default penalty of KES 5,000 per month in arrears.

A frequent payroll trap occurs when an employee changes jobs mid-year. Your old employer stops remitting on your final payslip, while your new employer's HR team may take one to three payroll cycles to register your National ID on their HELB employer portal schedule. During those transition months, bridge the gap yourself by sending at least KES 1,500 via M-Pesa Paybill 200800 before the end of each month so your account never trips an automated arrears flag.

Self-employed and diaspora payments: M-Pesa Paybill 200800 and USSD *642#

Roughly half of Kenyan university and TVET graduates earn income outside traditional corporate check-off payrolls—through consulting, agribusiness, clinical private practice, retail trade, remote international contracts, or employment abroad. HELB treats direct self-remittances identically to employer payroll deductions for compliance and amortisation purposes, provided your payment carries your exact National ID reference so it posts to your loan ledger rather than an unallocated suspense account.

To make an instant direct repayment via Safaricom M-Pesa:

  1. Open your SIM Toolkit or M-Pesa App and choose Lipa na M-Pesa, then select Paybill.
  2. Enter Business Number 200800 (official HELB Loan Repayment Paybill).
  3. For Account Number, enter your Kenyan National ID Number exactly as registered on your HELB loan application (do not enter your university registration number, phone number, or invoice code).
  4. Enter the repayment amount in Kenyan shillings (for example, 1500, 3500, or a lump sum), enter your M-Pesa PIN, and retain the confirmation SMS from HELB/M-Pesa.

Should you prepay a 4% HELB loan or invest in a Kenyan Money Market Fund? Financially disciplined borrowers sometimes ask whether extra cash should clear a 4.0% HELB undergraduate loan or earn 10% to 13% net of 15% withholding tax in a regulated Kenyan Money Market Fund (MMF) or Treasury bill. Mathematically, if your loan balance is large (say KES 240,000, where the KES 100 fee adds only 0.5% effective drag, making total loan cost ~4.5% p.a.), paying a sensible installment such as KES 3,000–3,500 while building an emergency MMF reserve yields positive net interest margin. However, once your remaining HELB balance drops below KES 25,000, the KES 100 monthly ledger fee alone exceeds 4.8% to 12% per year on the small balance—making a quick lump-sum payoff strictly superior.

HELB Compliance Certificate vs. Final Clearance Certificate

Job applicants, county public service board candidates, tenders bidders, and regulated professionals in Kenya frequently use the terms "Compliance Certificate" and "Clearance Certificate" interchangeably, yet HELB issues two legally distinct documents under Chapter Six (Leadership and Integrity) of the Constitution of Kenya:

  • HELB Compliance Certificate (12-Month Validity): Issued to three groups—Kenyan citizens who never took a HELB loan, beneficiaries still inside their statutory twelve-month grace period, and active loanees whose monthly repayments are current and free of unserviced arrears. It is valid for one year from the date of issue and must be renewed annually on the HEF/HELB portal or eCitizen until the loan is fully retired. Under Kenyan public-service onboarding reforms, HELB Compliance Certificates are issued free of charge.
  • HELB Final Clearance Certificate (Permanent Closure): Issued once—and permanently—only after your principal, accrued reducing-balance interest, KES 100 monthly ledger fees, and any penalties reach exactly KES 0.00 and HELB formally closes the loan ledger. Once you hold a Final Clearance Certificate, you never need to renew annual HELB compliance again.

Preventing final-month payroll over-deductions: Because employer payroll remittances take several days to reconcile after the 15th of the month, thousands of Kenyan employees accidentally allow their employer to keep deducting KES 3,500 or KES 5,000 for two or three months after their loan has already hit zero. While HELB refunds overpayments upon formal claim verification, processing a refund requires submitting bank details, copies of ID, and payslips and can take weeks. When your calculator schedule or portal statement shows two installments remaining, download an official interim payoff quote from the HELB portal, pay the exact residual shilling balance directly via M-Pesa Paybill 200800, generate your Final Clearance Certificate, and hand a copy to your HR payroll officer before the next payroll cut-off date.

Practical strategies to clear your HELB loan faster

Clearing a student loan efficiently is less about dramatic austerity and more about removing dead administrative months from the schedule:

  • Start voluntary micro-payments during your 12-month grace period: Even remitting KES 1,000 to KES 1,500 per month from an internship or side hustle during your grace year prevents early interest and ledger charges from compounding on top of your graduation principal.
  • Step up deductions by KES 1,000 whenever gross pay rises: If you start your career at KES 1,500 per month, raising your instruction to KES 2,500 at your first annual salary review cuts nearly six years off a KES 160,000 loan and saves over KES 27,000 in non-principal charges.
  • Sweep irregular windfalls straight to Paybill 200800: Directing a portion of annual performance bonuses, KRA income-tax refunds, SACCO dividends, or consultancy retainers to principal shortens the tail end of the schedule without locking you into a higher mandatory monthly payroll commitment during tight months.
  • Monitor official HELB penalty waiver windows if your account is in arrears: If prolonged unemployment pushed your loan into default and KES 5,000 monthly penalties have accumulated, watch official HELB announcements closely. HELB periodically opens time-bound penalty amnesty windows (historically waiving 80 per cent to 100 per cent of accrued penalties when borrowers settle principal and interest in a lump sum or structured agreement). Negotiating settlement during an active waiver window can erase hundreds of thousands of shillings in statutory arrears charges.

Common HELB repayment mistakes and misunderstandings

Most expensive student loan surprises trace back to five avoidable assumptions:

  • Assuming the 12-month grace period freezes all account charges: The statutory twelve-month window shields you from compulsory payroll recall and the KES 5,000 monthly default penalty, but your disbursed account ledger remains open. Confirm on your portal statement how grace-period ledger fees and interest apply to your specific cohort agreement rather than waiting blindly until Month 13.
  • Treating the KES 1,500 statutory minimum as a recommended 4-year plan: KES 1,500 was legislated as an affordability floor for entry-level earners, not an optimal payoff schedule. On balances above KES 200,000, paying KES 1,500 stretches debt across 16 to 25 years.
  • Using your university admission number instead of your National ID on M-Pesa: Entering a registration string such as "C01/1234/2021" in Paybill 200800 routes cash into unallocated suspense, leaving your National ID loan account appearing unpaid and blocking your Compliance Certificate until manual reconciliation is completed.
  • Assuming HR automatically stops or starts deductions without verification: Always inspect your first three payslips at a new employer to verify both that the HELB line item appears and—via *642# or the web portal 30 days later—that the deducted cash actually reached HELB.
  • Paying third-party cyber-cafe brokers for a "fast" Compliance Certificate: Application and download of a genuine HELB Compliance Certificate through the official HEF/HELB portal or eCitizen is free for compliant accounts. Third-party intermediaries cannot legally bypass an unserviced default flag.

2. Posting Dates and Payroll Lags: Actual HELB ledger interest depends on the exact calendar date each employer e-slip or M-Pesa payment settles on HELB's core banking system, plus shilling-rounding rules on the final closing month.

3. Policy and Rate Updates: Statutory interest rates, means-testing band rules, ledger fees, and amnesty terms are subject to amendment under the Higher Education Loans Board Act (Cap 213A) and gazetted Ministry of Education regulations.

Always verify your figures: Before signing an irrevocable employer salary check-off form, remitting a final lump-sum settlement, or making major household debt-versus-investment decisions, download your official real-time loan statement from hef.co.ke or helb.co.ke, visit an accredited HELB desk at any Huduma Centre, or consult a licensed financial planner or certified public accountant (CPA-K).

Frequently asked questions

What is the interest rate on HELB student loans in Kenya?

Direct undergraduate and TVET student loans disbursed by the Higher Education Loans Board carry a subsidised interest rate of 4.0 per cent per year, calculated monthly on the reducing principal balance. Postgraduate master's and doctoral scholarships or loans typically carry 10.0 per cent per year, while salaried Jielimishe and partner-scheme loans generally carry 12.0 per cent per year. In addition, HELB charges a statutory administrative ledger maintenance fee of KES 100 per month while an active loan balance remains open.

What is the minimum monthly HELB repayment amount?

For direct undergraduate and TVET loans, the standard statutory minimum monthly repayment floor is KES 1,500 for both salaried and self-employed beneficiaries. However, your monthly payment must also exceed your first month's interest plus the KES 100 ledger fee so the balance actually shrinks; on larger postgraduate or 12 per cent salaried loans, that mathematical coverage floor is higher (for example, KES 2,601 per month on a KES 300,000 loan at 10 per cent).

When does HELB loan repayment begin after graduation?

Under the Higher Education Loans Board Act (Cap 213A), mandatory repayment begins one year (twelve months) after you complete the academic programme funded by the Board, or earlier if the Board issues formal recall notice upon confirmed employment. Voluntary repayments during study or during the twelve-month post-study window are accepted at any time without prepayment fees and immediately reduce future interest and ledger fee exposure.

What is the penalty for defaulting on a HELB loan or late employer remittance?

Under Section 15 of the HELB Act, a loanee who fails to commence or maintain scheduled monthly repayments after the twelve-month grace period is liable to a statutory default penalty of KES 5,000 for each month in arrears. Separately, an employer who deducts or is required to deduct HELB installments and fails to remit them by the 15th day of the following month faces an employer-borne penalty of 5 per cent of the unremitted deduction per month.

How do I pay my HELB loan directly via M-Pesa if I am self-employed or abroad?

Open M-Pesa Lipa na M-Pesa, select Paybill, enter Business Number 200800, and enter your Kenyan National ID number as the Account Number before confirming the KES amount and your PIN. Kenyan mobile subscribers can also dial *642# or use the Higher Education Financing (HEF) / HELB self-service portal to generate an instant payment prompt, while diaspora graduates can remit via bank transfer or international M-Pesa remittance partners referencing their National ID number.

What is the difference between a HELB Compliance Certificate and a Clearance Certificate?

A HELB Compliance Certificate is a twelve-month validity document issued free of charge to non-beneficiaries, graduates still inside their grace period, or active borrowers whose monthly repayments are up to date, satisfying Chapter Six onboarding checks. A HELB Clearance Certificate is the permanent closure document issued only after your principal, interest, ledger fees, and any penalties reach zero and the Board formally closes the loan ledger.

Does the KES 100 monthly ledger fee stop when I finish repaying?

Yes. The KES 100 monthly administrative ledger fee applies only while your loan account holds an open balance. Once your final installment clears the remaining shilling balance and HELB reconciles the account to zero, monthly ledger fees cease permanently. Requesting an updated payoff quote before your final two payroll cycles helps prevent salary over-deductions after payoff.

Do I have to repay the government scholarship portion under the new funding model?

No. Under Kenya's Student-Centred Funding Model administered through the Higher Education Financing (HEF) portal, university and TVET support separates into a non-repayable government scholarship, a direct household contribution, and a repayable HELB loan. Only the disbursed HELB tuition and upkeep loan component enters your loan ledger and accrues 4 per cent annual interest.

Can I pay more than the minimum every month or make a lump-sum payment?

Yes. HELB charges zero early-settlement or overpayment penalties. You can instruct your employer's payroll team in writing to deduct a higher fixed monthly amount, or send voluntary top-ups and lump sums via M-Pesa Paybill 200800 at any time. Because interest is computed monthly on the reducing balance, every extra shilling directly lowers next month's principal and shortens the number of months you pay the KES 100 ledger fee.

Does this calculator store my HELB balance, National ID, or salary details?

No. Every amortisation loop runs locally inside your browser tab using client-side JavaScript, and we never ask for your name, National ID number, KRA PIN, or employer. Only if you click the optional AI insight button is an anonymous numeric summary of loan category, principal, monthly installment, payoff months, interest, and ledger fees transmitted once to generate short commentary.
