Cookie Policy What We Store, and Why

This page explains, in plain language, the small number of cookies AfriWidget uses. It also explains what those cookies do, what they do not do, and how to turn off the ones you would rather not have.

Effective Date: 1 January 2026 • Last Updated: 5 October 2026

The Short Version

AfriWidget uses a very small number of cookies. One is required for the site to remember whether you clicked Accept or Decline on the cookie banner. The others come from Google Analytics and Google AdSense, and you can turn them off whenever you like. No cookie ever contains the numbers you enter into a calculator.

  • One functional cookie remembers your cookie choice.
  • Google Analytics cookies help us count visits in aggregate.
  • Google AdSense cookies help decide which ads to show.
  • You can decline the analytics and advertising cookies from the banner.
  • Every tool still works perfectly if you decline everything.

Change your mind? You can update your cookie preference at any time. Clicking the button clears your saved choice and shows the banner again, so you can pick Accept or Decline afresh.

1. What a Cookie Actually Is

A cookie is a small text file that a website asks your browser to store. It usually contains a short string of letters and numbers, plus an expiry date. It is not a program. It cannot run on your device, cannot open files, cannot install software, and cannot carry a virus. It just sits there until either the website asks to read it again or the expiry date passes and it is deleted.

Cookies are used on almost every modern website, and they serve many different purposes. Some are helpful, some are essential, and a few have given the whole idea a bad reputation by being used for aggressive cross-site tracking. We want to be clear about which type we are using and why.

There are two main kinds of cookie worth knowing about:

  • First-party cookies are set by the website you are actually visiting. On AfriWidget, that means cookies set by afriwidget.com.
  • Third-party cookies are set by a different company whose service is embedded in the page. On AfriWidget, that means cookies set by Google (through Analytics and AdSense).

Cookies can also be either session cookies (which disappear the moment you close your browser) or persistent cookies (which stay on your device for a set period). The functional cookie we set is persistent, because its whole point is to remember your preference next time you visit.

2. Why AfriWidget Uses Cookies

We use cookies for three reasons, and only three reasons:

  1. To remember whether you accepted or declined the cookie banner, so we do not keep asking you on every page.
  2. To count visits to the site in aggregate, so we can see which tools are useful and which ones need work.
  3. To display advertisements through Google AdSense, which is how we cover the cost of running the site without charging users.

That is the complete list. We do not use cookies to build a profile of you, to remember what you typed into any calculator, to track you across other websites on our own account, or to sell your behaviour to data brokers. If we ever wanted to add a new type of cookie, we would update this page first and tell you clearly what changed.

4. Cookies and Your Calculator Inputs

This is the section we most want people to read, because it addresses the worry that most people have when they see the word "cookie".

When you use a calculator on AfriWidget — whether it is a GPA calculator, a loan calculator, a BMI tool, or anything else — the numbers you type are processed entirely inside your browser. They are held in your device's memory while the page is open and then they are gone. None of those numbers are ever written to a cookie, to local storage, to session storage, or to any server.

The one exception to local storage is your cookie consent preference itself, which is stored as a simple "accepted" or "declined" value so the banner does not keep reappearing. That value has nothing to do with the numbers you have entered anywhere on the site.

You can verify this yourself in about thirty seconds. Open your browser's developer tools, switch to the Application or Storage tab, and look at the cookies and local storage entries for afriwidget.com. You will see a small handful of entries, all of which relate to consent and to Google's services. You will not see anything resembling the inputs you typed into a calculator.

5. How to Decline or Delete Cookies

You have several ways to control cookies on AfriWidget and across the web.

Through our banner

The first time you visit, a small banner appears at the bottom of the screen with two buttons: Decline and Accept All. If you click Decline, we will not load the analytics or advertising cookies. All of our tools still work exactly as normal, because none of them depend on those cookies.

If you change your mind later, click the "Manage Cookie Preferences" button at the top of this page. It clears your saved choice and shows the banner again so you can pick afresh.

Through your browser settings

Every modern browser lets you view, block, and delete cookies. The exact menu varies, but the general location is under Settings → Privacy or Preferences → Privacy. Look for an entry called "Cookies and site data" or similar. From there you can:

  • Block all cookies (this may break some websites).
  • Block only third-party cookies (this disables the analytics and advertising cookies but keeps our functional one).
  • Delete all cookies when you close your browser.
  • Allow cookies only for websites you trust.

Through Google's own controls

Google provides its own tools for managing the cookies its services set:

  • Visit adssettings.google.com to control personalised advertising on your Google account.
  • Visit tools.google.com/dlpage/gaoptout to install the official Google Analytics opt-out browser add-on.
  • Visit optout.aboutads.info or optout.networkadvertising.org to opt out of interest-based advertising from multiple networks at once.

Through browser extensions

Extensions such as uBlock Origin, Privacy Badger, and Ghostery can block analytics and advertising scripts across the sites you visit. AfriWidget works perfectly with all of them. Blocking advertising scripts will not affect any of our tools in any way.

6. Do Not Track and Other Browser Signals

Some browsers include a "Do Not Track" (DNT) setting that is meant to tell websites the visitor does not want to be tracked. In practice, there is no agreed technical standard for what a website should do when it receives a DNT signal, so different sites handle it differently. AfriWidget does not currently change its behaviour based on DNT signals.

The honest reason for this is that complying with DNT in one country but not another would be misleading. Instead, we give you direct control through the banner and through the browser options described above. That way the decision stays in your hands regardless of what the browser is signalling behind the scenes.

We will revisit this position if an industry-wide standard for DNT compliance is ever adopted.

7. Cookies on Mobile Devices

Everything on this page applies equally on phones and tablets. Mobile browsers such as Chrome on Android, Safari on iOS, Samsung Internet, and Firefox Mobile all let you view and clear cookies from their settings menus.

One thing worth knowing on mobile: many browsers now block third-party cookies by default. If yours does, the analytics and advertising cookies described above will not be set at all, and you will see our banner only for the functional cookie. That is completely fine. Our tools work the same either way.

If you clear your browser's cookies on your phone, your saved consent preference will also be cleared, and the banner will appear again the next time you visit. That is expected behaviour, not a bug.

8. Changes to This Policy

If the change is significant — for example, if we were to introduce a new type of tracker that we have never used before — we would also mention it clearly on the homepage so that regular visitors do not miss it.

Small wording tweaks do not trigger a homepage notice, but every change, however small, shows up here first.

10. Plain-Language Glossary

Cookie

A small text file a website asks your browser to store. It cannot run code, cannot open files, and cannot carry a virus.

First-party cookie

A cookie set by the site you are actually visiting. On AfriWidget, that is us.

Third-party cookie

A cookie set by a different company whose service is embedded in the page. On AfriWidget, that is Google.

Session cookie

A cookie that disappears the moment you close your browser.

Persistent cookie

A cookie that stays on your device for a set period, until you delete it or it expires.

Local storage

A separate browser feature that lets a site save small amounts of data on your device. We use it only to remember your cookie choice, and for nothing else.

IP anonymisation

Stripping the last part of an IP address before recording it, so the resulting number cannot be traced back to a specific person or device.

Aggregated data

Information presented as totals or averages rather than as individual records. "500 people visited this week" is aggregated. "John visited at 3pm" is not.

Do Not Track (DNT)

A browser setting that asks websites not to track the visitor. There is no agreed standard for how sites should respond, which is why behaviour varies.

Questions about cookies on AfriWidget

Do I have to accept cookies to use the tools?

No. Every tool on AfriWidget works whether you accept cookies or decline them. The only cookie that still gets set if you decline is the one that remembers your choice, and that is because it is the thing that stops the banner reappearing on every page.

What happens if I click Decline?

We do not load the analytics or advertising cookies. You will still see advertisements when you browse the site, because advertising is how the free tools are funded, but those ads will not be personalised using your browsing history. The tools themselves behave identically to how they behave for someone who clicked Accept.

Can I change my mind later?

Yes. Click the "Manage Cookie Preferences" button at the top of this page. It clears your saved choice and shows the banner again, so you can pick the other option. You can also clear cookies from your browser settings if you prefer, which has the same effect.

Do any cookies contain the numbers I enter into a calculator?

No. Not ever. Your calculator inputs stay in your browser's memory while the page is open and are gone the moment you close or refresh it. They are not written to cookies, not saved to local storage, and not sent to any server. You can verify this yourself by opening your browser's developer tools and looking at the cookies and storage for this site.

Why do you use Google Analytics if you say you care about privacy?

Because without some form of analytics, we would have no idea which tools people find useful and which ones are not worth the maintenance. We have deliberately configured Analytics to be as privacy-friendly as possible: IP anonymisation is switched on, we do not enable any of the advertising features inside Analytics, and the data is aggregated rather than individual. If you would rather not be counted at all, declining the banner removes Analytics entirely.

Can I block all cookies and still use the site?

Yes. You can block all cookies in your browser settings and every AfriWidget tool will still work. The only side effect is that the cookie banner will not be able to remember your choice, so it may appear again on a future visit. That is a small price to pay for total control, and we fully understand why some people prefer it.

Do you use cookies from Facebook, TikTok, or other social networks?

No. We do not run Facebook Pixel, TikTok Pixel, Twitter Pixel, LinkedIn Insight Tag, or any other social network tracker. We do not run Hotjar, Mixpanel, Segment, or any behavioural analytics tool beyond Google Analytics. The complete list of cookies on AfriWidget is on this page, and if we ever add a new one, we will update this page before it goes live.

What is the difference between a cookie and local storage?

They are two different browser features that both store small pieces of data on your device. Cookies are sent back to the server with every request, while local storage stays on your device and is only read by the site's own scripts. On AfriWidget we use local storage for one thing only: remembering whether you clicked Accept or Decline on the cookie banner. That is the same job the functional cookie does, and we use whichever is supported so your preference is not lost.

I use a shared computer. Do cookies from my session affect anyone else?

Only to a very limited extent. Your cookie consent preference will carry over to the next person who uses the same browser on that computer, so they will not see the banner. Google Analytics may treat the two of you as the same visitor, which slightly skews our statistics but has no other effect. None of your calculator inputs are stored anywhere, so there is nothing sensitive to worry about. If you want to leave the machine clean, clear your cookies before you finish.

Are cookies dangerous?

Cookies themselves are not dangerous. They are plain text files that cannot run code, cannot install software, and cannot carry a virus. What worries many people is not the cookie but the practice of tracking someone across multiple websites, which uses cookies as one ingredient. On AfriWidget, the only cross-site tracking that could take place is through Google AdSense and only if you have accepted advertising cookies. If you decline, that possibility disappears.

Cookie question we did not answer?

Write to us and we will get back to you, or use the button above to change your current preference in one click.
