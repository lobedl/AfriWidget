What the BECE aggregate is

The Basic Education Certificate Examination is the exam every Junior High School student in Ghana sits at the end of Form Three. It is set and marked by the West African Examinations Council, and the results are used by the Ghana Education Service to place successful candidates into Senior High Schools, Technical Schools, and Vocational Schools across the country.

The BECE aggregate is a single number that summarises those results. It is calculated by adding the grades from four core subjects plus the two best elective grades. Grades run from 1 to 9, where 1 is the highest achievement and 9 is the lowest, so a lower aggregate always indicates a stronger performance. The best possible aggregate is 6 (six Grade 1 results) and the worst is 54 (six Grade 9 results).

Parents and students sometimes confuse the aggregate with a percentage. It is not. It is a points total, and it is the number that appears on the placement letter sent out by the Computerised School Selection and Placement System. Understanding it early makes the placement process much less confusing, because every school and programme in Ghana is described in terms of a cut-off aggregate rather than a percentage or a letter grade.

The WAEC grade scale

The BECE grade scale is identical in structure to the WASSCE grade scale, just with numbers instead of letters. Every subject is awarded one of nine grades, and each grade carries a fixed number of points.

11Highest achievement
22Very good
33Good
44Credit standard
55Credit standard
66Credit standard
77Pass

99Fail

Grades 1 through 6 are the ones that count as credits, which is why senior high programmes often ask for "credit passes" in specific subjects. A Grade 7 or 8 is technically a pass but does not count as a credit, and Grade 9 is a fail. A single Grade 9 in a required subject can disqualify a candidate from placement into a programme that demands credit in that subject, regardless of how strong the rest of the aggregate is.

How the aggregate is put together

The version used by the Ghana Education Service and CSSPS is simple to describe. Four core subject grades are added together. Then the two best elective grades are added on top. The sum of those six numbers is your BECE aggregate.

Because a lower aggregate indicates stronger results, the aim is to score as close to 6 as possible. A candidate who gets a Grade 1 in all four core subjects and a Grade 1 in both counted electives ends up with an aggregate of 6. That is the theoretical best, and only a small number of candidates achieve it each year.

The most common mistake people make is assuming that all electives count. They do not. Only your two strongest count. That is why the calculator asks you for up to three elective grades: it uses the two that produce the lowest point total, leaving the third aside as a backup.

Core subjects and electives

The four core subjects are English Language, Mathematics, Integrated Science, and Social Studies. Every candidate sits all four, and every candidate has all four counted in the aggregate. There is no option to drop one of the cores in favour of a stronger elective, and there is no substitution between them.

The elective pool is broader, and candidates typically sit between three and five electives. Common choices include:

  • Religious and Moral Education
  • Ghanaian Language (Twi, Ewe, Ga, Fante, and others)
  • French
  • Information and Communications Technology
  • Pre-Technical Skills
  • Pre-Vocational Skills
  • Home Economics or Catering
  • Visual Arts or Music

The exact list of electives available depends on the subjects your Junior High School offers. Whichever electives you took, only your best two will be counted towards the aggregate.

A worked example

Here is a typical candidate. English Language: Grade 2. Mathematics: Grade 3. Integrated Science: Grade 1. Social Studies: Grade 4. Elective 1: Grade 2. Elective 2: Grade 5. Elective 3: Grade 7.

The core contribution works out as 2 + 3 + 1 + 4 = 10 points.

The three electives are 2, 5, and 7. Sorted from lowest to highest, they are 2, 5, 7. The best two are 2 and 5, giving an elective subtotal of 7 points. The third elective is dropped.

The total BECE aggregate is 10 + 7 = 17.

That would place this candidate firmly within reach of most well-regarded Senior High Schools in Ghana, though probably not the very top institutions, which often admit at an aggregate in the single digits or low teens. It would sit comfortably above the cut-off for a large number of good mixed and single-sex schools across the country.

What different aggregates open up

The bands below are indicative, not official cut-offs. Each school sets its own minimum, and those minimums move from year to year depending on how many candidates applied and how many places were available. What the bands do give you is a rough sense of where you stand before you fill in your school choices.

AggregateTypical placement
6 to 9The most selective Category A schools, including Prempeh College, Achimota, Mfantsipim, Wesley Girls, and Yaa Asantewaa, plus the top technical and vocational institutions.
10 to 14Category A and strong Category B schools across all regions. Most boarding schools with a good academic reputation.
15 to 19Category B schools and many Category C schools. A wide range of day and boarding options in every region.
20 to 25Category C schools and day schools in most districts. Still possible to get a school of choice, especially if you choose realistically.
26 to 30Mostly day schools and community day secondary schools. Some technical and vocational placements also fall in this band.
31 and abovePlacement is possible but options narrow. Technical and vocational programmes, or day schools near your JHS, are the most realistic targets.

None of these ranges are guarantees. A candidate with an aggregate of 8 who chooses only the very top schools might end up unplaced on the first round, while a candidate with an aggregate of 15 who spreads their choices sensibly can end up at a school they are very happy with.

How CSSPS placement actually works

The Computerised School Selection and Placement System, or CSSPS, is the platform the Ghana Education Service uses to place every BECE graduate into a Senior High School, Technical School, or Vocational School. It is not a simple matter of the highest aggregate getting the first choice. It is a matching process between candidates and schools, and understanding how it works helps you use it well.

After the BECE results come out, every candidate logs into the CSSPS portal and selects six schools in ranked order. For each school they also choose a programme, such as General Science, General Arts, Business, Visual Arts, Technical, Home Economics, or Agricultural Science. The portal records both the school and the programme for each choice.

CSSPS then runs the matching. It goes through candidates in order of aggregate, and for each candidate it tries to place them in their first choice. If the school and programme still have space, the candidate is placed. If they are full, CSSPS tries the second choice, and so on down the list. If none of the six choices can accommodate the candidate, they are placed automatically in a school with available space, usually a day school near their home district.

Three practical consequences follow from this. First, spreading your choices across a range of realistic aggregates matters more than aiming only at the very top. Second, the programme you choose within a school can matter as much as the school itself, because different programmes have different cut-offs. Third, self-placement is available for candidates who are not placed on the first round or who want to change their placement; the exact rules and deadlines are announced each year by GES.

Common mistakes with BECE aggregate

A handful of errors show up again and again when families try to work out their aggregate before the official placement letter arrives.

Including all electives instead of the best two. Only the two strongest electives count. Adding all three or four overstates the aggregate and leads to expectations that will not match the official letter.

Forgetting that four cores always count. Unlike the WASSCE aggregate where you can sometimes drop a weak subject, BECE does not allow that. A weak English grade counts towards the total whether you like it or not.

Confusing the aggregate with a percentage. An aggregate of 20 is not twenty percent. It is twenty points across six subjects. The scale runs from 6 (best) to 54 (worst), not from 100 down to 0.

Assuming the aggregate is a placement guarantee. It is not. Placement depends on the schools you chose, the programmes you chose, the demand from other candidates, and where the available places sat in that particular year.

Missing the CSSPS deadline. The portal opens for a defined window each year, and the choices you enter in that window are what get used. If you miss it, you rely on self-placement or the second round, which narrows your options considerably.

Not asking about subject-specific requirements. Some programmes require a credit pass in Mathematics or Integrated Science specifically, regardless of aggregate. A candidate with a strong aggregate but a Grade 7 in one of those subjects can be filtered out of a programme they wanted.

Where this calculator stops

This tool applies the standard BECE aggregate formula: four core subjects plus the best two electives, with each grade contributing its face value in points. That is the formula the Ghana Education Service and CSSPS use, and it matches the version in the official WAEC Ghana materials.

What it does not know is the school choice you will enter, the programme you will select, the cut-off for those choices in the year you apply, or the demand pattern from other candidates. All of those influence the outcome of placement, and none of them can be predicted from an aggregate alone.

What it does give you is a clean, accurate starting figure. Use it to understand where you roughly stand, to see how much difference improving one subject would make, and to prepare for the school selection process with realistic choices. The final placement letter from CSSPS is what decides.

Frequently asked questions

What is a BECE aggregate score?

A BECE aggregate is a single number that summarises a candidate's Basic Education Certificate Examination results. It is the sum of four core subject grades and the best two elective grades. Grades run from 1 (highest) to 9 (lowest), so a lower aggregate always indicates a stronger performance. The best possible aggregate is 6 and the worst is 54.

Which four subjects are compulsory in the BECE aggregate?

English Language, Mathematics, Integrated Science, and Social Studies. All four are counted, and none is dropped in favour of another. If you did not sit one of them or received no grade, the standard aggregate cannot be calculated until that grade is provided by WAEC.

How are the elective subjects chosen?

The best two of your elective grades are used. Candidates typically sit three or four electives drawn from a long list that includes Religious and Moral Education, Ghanaian Language, French, Information and Communications Technology, and pre-technical or pre-vocational skills. Only the two strongest count.

Why is a lower BECE aggregate better?

Because Grade 1 in the WAEC scale is worth 1 point and Grade 9 is worth 9 points. Adding smaller numbers produces a smaller total, so a lower aggregate always reflects stronger grades across the subjects being counted. It is the opposite of the percentage system most people are used to.

How is the BECE aggregate used for Senior High School placement?

The Computerised School Selection and Placement System (CSSPS), run by the Ghana Education Service, uses your aggregate together with your school choices to place you in a Senior High School, Technical School, or Vocational School. Each school and each programme has its own cut-off aggregate, and placement depends on how many candidates applied and where the available slots were.

What is a good BECE aggregate for a top Senior High School?

The most selective schools, such as Prempeh College, Achimota, Mfantsipim, Wesley Girls, and Yaa Asantewaa, typically admit candidates with an aggregate in the single digits or the very low teens. A specific aggregate that guarantees placement does not exist, because the cut-off moves each year depending on the applicant pool and the number of available places.

Does my BECE school choice matter as much as my aggregate?

Both matter. CSSPS placement considers your aggregate and your ranked list of school and programme choices together. Choosing schools that sit realistically within your aggregate band, and including at least one or two options you are very likely to qualify for, usually leads to a better outcome than aiming only at the top.

Can I rewrite BECE if I am not happy with my aggregate?

A rewrite is possible but not straightforward. Most candidates who want to improve their BECE results either re-register as a private candidate or enrol in a remedial programme. The exact process and deadlines are set by WAEC Ghana each year, so check their official announcements if you are considering this route.
