Frequently asked questions

Does a high admission chance result guarantee admission?

No. A high chance result only means the score you entered clears the planning baseline by two points or more. Subject requirements, quotas, competition, and current-year policy still decide the outcome. Treat the result as a sign that a programme is worth investigating, not as a promise.

Why are some scores compared the other way round?

JAMB, KCSE cluster points, APS, and UACE points are stronger when the number is higher. WASSCE aggregates and ACSEE admission aggregates are stronger when the number is lower. The checker preserves the correct direction for each system so that a stronger result always reads as stronger.

Where do the baselines come from?

Each baseline is a planning reference drawn from official admissions pages, published prospectuses, and past-year placement lists. They are updated periodically but they are not live official cut-offs. Verify the current cycle with the specific institution before making any application decision.

What is the two-point band and why does it matter?

A score has to beat the baseline by at least two points (or two units, in the case of aggregates) to be flagged as a high chance. A score right on the baseline or one point above is treated as borderline. The band exists because real cut-offs move up and down between cycles, and a result that sat comfortably above the line last year can slip just below it the next.

Can I use this checker before my results are released?

Yes. Enter a range of plausible scores and treat each result as a scenario rather than a prediction. When your official results arrive, run the check again with the actual number and revisit your shortlist against the current published requirements.

Does the checker know about subject requirements?

No. It compares a score against a course baseline. It does not know whether you hold the required credit in English, Mathematics, or a science subject. A programme can reject a strong score if a required subject is missing, so read the programme's entry requirements before you rely on any number here.

How accurate is the African university data?

The dataset is a planning aid built from published sources and past cycles. Baselines shift from year to year, and some programmes do not publish fixed cut-offs at all. For anything formal, use the official admission notice for the exact programme and year you are applying to.

Is my score sent anywhere?

No. Everything you type stays inside your browser, and the eligibility check is performed locally. If you choose to press the optional AI button, only an anonymous summary of your inputs and the resulting status is sent to generate a short written response. Nothing is stored.

What should I do if the result is below the baseline?

A below-baseline result is a signal to explore options, not a dead end. Look at related programmes at the same university, less competitive institutions, different entry routes such as diploma-to-degree, and the possibility of improving specific grades in a subsequent sitting. Speak with an admissions officer before ruling anything out.
