How the O’Level subject checker works

Choosing subjects early can make a university application much easier. WAEC and NECO results are used for many West African admissions routes, but every university and programme may publish its own minimum subjects, grades, and combinations. This tool turns a common search question into a simple planning reference: which subjects should a student keep in mind when considering Medicine, Law, Computer Science, Accounting, Nursing, Pharmacy, or Engineering?

The course selector shows two groups. The core group contains subjects that commonly appear as required subjects or important admission foundations. The elective group contains subjects that are commonly accepted or expected for that course. The result card also highlights a critical warning so a student does not accidentally drop Mathematics, Chemistry, Biology, Physics, English, or another subject that may be central to the intended programme.

Medicine, Nursing, and Pharmacy

Health programmes usually require a strong science combination. Medicine commonly centres on English Language, Mathematics, Biology, Chemistry, and Physics. Nursing often gives special importance to Biology and Chemistry, while Pharmacy commonly depends on Chemistry and Biology. These are planning combinations, not a substitute for a university brochure. Some institutions require particular grades, accept specific alternatives, or use a different rule for direct entry, foundation, or transfer applicants.

Law, technology, and business

Law applications commonly require English Language and may require or strongly prefer Literature in English, Government, History, or another humanities subject. Computer Science and Engineering normally depend on Mathematics, with Physics, Chemistry, Further Mathematics, or Technical Drawing varying by institution and branch. Accounting commonly uses English and Mathematics, with Economics, Financial Accounting, or Business Studies strengthening the planned combination. A suitable subject list does not guarantee a place; it only helps prevent an avoidable subject mismatch.

How to verify before registration

Use the checker before registering subjects or choosing an application route. Then open the current admission requirements for the exact university and course. Check the number of credits, minimum grade in each subject, accepted examination sittings, age or direct-entry rules, and whether a subject must be taken at one sitting. Nigeria, Ghana, and other West African systems can use similar subject names while applying different details, so location matters.

If you are selecting secondary-school subjects, speak with a teacher or counsellor before dropping a science, language, or mathematics subject. If you already have a result, compare the subjects and grades against the official course page rather than assuming that a general combination is enough. Professional programmes may also have accreditation rules, interviews, screening tests, or additional documents.

Frequently asked questions

Does this checker show official JAMB or university requirements?

No. It shows common planning combinations. The official university admission brochure and current examination-body guidance always take priority.

Are WAEC and NECO combinations always identical?

They are often comparable, but acceptance, grading, subject names, and sitting rules can differ. Confirm the exact policy for the institution and application year.

Why are some subjects described as recommended?

Universities may publish a required subject plus acceptable alternatives. The tool keeps that uncertainty visible instead of presenting one generic list as a guarantee.

A simple subject-planning checklist

Write down the exact university, course title, examination body, examination year, and result sitting before comparing a subject combination. Then check the official prospectus for required credits, minimum grades, accepted sittings, and any subject-specific alternatives. If two courses are possible, compare both lists rather than assuming that one science or humanities combination works everywhere.

Use this page as an early warning system: it can help you notice that Mathematics, Chemistry, Biology, Physics, English, or another subject deserves attention. It cannot decide eligibility for a real application. Keep the official requirement page beside your notes and revisit it when the admissions cycle changes.

Keep a course shortlist

Subject planning is stronger when it is connected to a shortlist. Write down your first-choice course, two related courses, and the subjects each institution accepts. This makes it easier to notice where one subject combination keeps several options open. Review the list with a teacher or counsellor before registration, especially when a course involves science practicals, professional licensing, or a competitive screening process.

The checker is intentionally cautious. It highlights common requirements but does not claim that every WAEC or NECO candidate with those subjects is eligible. Use the official institution page for the final decision, and save a copy of the current requirements with the date you checked them.
