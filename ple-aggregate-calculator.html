What this calculator does

This tool takes the four numeric grades on a candidate's PLE result slip, adds them together, states the resulting Division from the official UNEB bands, and shows which tier of Senior 1 secondary school the aggregate matches. It is the same arithmetic the Uganda National Examinations Board runs when it issues results, done here in the browser so it works at any speed and never sends anything across the network.

The reason a dedicated page for this is worth having is that the aggregate and the Division are not the same number. Two candidates can walk into the same office with the same aggregate and walk out with different classifications, because the Division bands are not just a matter of the total. A single failing grade moves a candidate down a tier even when the aggregate is well within the band above. Reading the result slip correctly, and understanding what the number actually unlocks, saves parents and schools a great deal of confusion during the Senior 1 selection period.

Every calculation runs on the device. If the AI insight button is pressed, only an anonymous summary of the four grades, the aggregate, the Division, and the estimated school tier is sent to a small serverless function that runs once and stops. The candidate's name, index number, and school are never part of that payload.

The four compulsory PLE subjects

Every PLE candidate sits four papers, no more and no fewer. The board chooses the content, and there is no electives system at this level. The four subjects are fixed, and each carries equal weight in the aggregate.

English Language. The paper tests reading comprehension, vocabulary, language structure, composition, and grammar. It is the paper that most closely predicts later O-Level performance in the humanities and is one of the two subjects a candidate cannot fail without losing the highest Division.

Mathematics. Arithmetic, fractions, geometry, measurement, and simple algebra and data handling. Like English, it is one of the two subjects whose failure bars a candidate from Division 1 regardless of the total.

Integrated Basic Science. A combined paper that draws on human biology, health habits, agriculture, environmental science, and physical science. The content is deliberately wide because Ugandan primary schools expect students to reach Senior 1 with a working knowledge across several scientific areas rather than a narrow specialism.

Social Studies and Religious Education. History, geography, civics, and either Christian or Islamic Religious Education depending on the candidate's school. It is the paper that tests knowledge of Uganda, East Africa, and the wider world from a primary-level perspective.

Because all four subjects carry the same weight, there is no strategic advantage in being very strong in one subject and weak in another. A single Fail 9 adds nine points to the aggregate and disqualifies the candidate from the highest Division, and that is a heavier penalty than the arithmetic alone suggests.

The nine-point grading scale

UNEB grades every PLE subject on a nine-point scale, from Distinction 1 at the top to Fail 9 at the bottom. The numeric value is what feeds the aggregate, and lower is better throughout.

Because the points run from one to nine and there are four subjects, the best possible aggregate is four (four D1s) and the worst is thirty-six (four F9s). The middle of the range is eighteen, which sits inside Division 2. In practice, a Distinction 1 is awarded to a small fraction of candidates in each subject, and a Fail 9 is awarded to another small fraction at the bottom. Most candidates land somewhere between C4 and C6, which is where the Division boundaries actually fall.

The boundary that catches people. Aggregate 12 is the last aggregate that qualifies for Division 1, and aggregate 13 is the first that lands in Division 2. The difference between a total of 12 and a total of 13 is one point, which is the difference between one subject grade and the next one up. That single point can decide which school a candidate is eligible for.

How the aggregate and the Division differ

The aggregate is arithmetic. The Division is a rule applied on top of the arithmetic. UNEB publishes the bands, and then adds pass-profile conditions that a raw total does not capture.

Aggregates 4 to 12 qualify for Division 1, with the conditions that there are no F9 grades in any subject and no F9 in English or Mathematics specifically. Aggregates 13 to 22 qualify for Division 2. Aggregates 23 to 28 fall into Division 3. Aggregates 29 to 34 fall into Division 4. Aggregates 35 to 36, or a candidate with multiple fails, receives Division U, which is the ungraded category that requires a repeat or a shift into vocational training.

The pass-profile conditions matter more than most families realise. A candidate who scores Distinction 1 in Mathematics and Science but Fails 9 in English ends up with an aggregate that looks impressive and a Division that is not, because the fail in English bars the top band. The remedy is straightforward but it costs time: the candidate sits the failed subject again at the next cycle, either as a repeat P.7 candidate or as a resit through a school that supports private candidates. It is worth catching that situation early rather than discovering it at the placement stage.

A worked example at the top of Division 1

Take a candidate whose result slip shows the following four grades.

English LanguageDistinction 11
MathematicsDistinction 22
Integrated Basic ScienceCredit 33
Social Studies & Religious EducationCredit 44

Adding one plus two plus three plus four gives an aggregate of ten. That sits inside the Division 1 band of four to twelve, and the pass profile is clean: no failing grades anywhere, no fail in English or Mathematics. The candidate has achieved Division 1 with an aggregate of 10, and the tool places them in the second tier of competitive national schools rather than the top of the elite tier, where aggregates of four to eight tend to fill the Senior 1 places.

Now change one grade. If Social Studies moves from Credit 4 to Pass 7, the aggregate rises to thirteen, and the candidate drops from Division 1 to Division 2. If English moves from Distinction 1 to Distinction 2 and Science moves from Credit 3 to Credit 4, the aggregate is eleven, still Division 1, and the school tier stays the same. The calculator lets a family test these scenarios in seconds, which is genuinely useful when a candidate is trying to work out whether a particular subject needs extra attention before the exam or during a repeat cycle.

Senior 1 school tiers and typical cutoffs

Secondary school admission in Uganda is not governed by a single national cutoff. Each school receives more applications than it has places, and the school itself decides how to fill them, usually under the guidance of the Ministry of Education and Sports and with reference to the previous year's admissions.

Tier 1 — elite national boarding. Schools like King's College Budo, Mount Saint Mary's College Namagunga, Saint Mary's College Kisubi, Gayaza High School, Namilyango College, Saint Mary's Boarding SS Kitende, Uganda Martyrs SS Namugongo, Ntare School, Maryhill High School, and Bweranyangi Girls' typically admit Senior 1 candidates with aggregates between four and eight. Competition for those places is intense, and a clean Division 1 record is the starting point rather than the guarantee.

Tier 2 — prominent national and regional. Aggregates nine to fourteen. These are strong schools with national reputations in specific fields, and candidates in this range usually receive offers from at least one of the schools they apply to, provided they list a realistic spread of preferences.

Tier 3 — regional and district secondary. Aggregates fifteen to twenty-two, which sits inside Division 2. Districts and regional secondary schools accept a wide range of Division 2 candidates, and under the Universal Secondary Education programme many public schools reserve places for candidates from their catchment area.

Tier 4 — community and technical. Aggregates twenty-three and above, which covers Division 3, Division 4, and the ungraded band. Community secondary schools and technical and vocational institutes accept candidates across these tiers, and the pathway into O-Level certification remains open even where the top-tier boarding route has closed.

Common mistakes in PLE planning

Six mistakes come up repeatedly when families and schools work through PLE results and Senior 1 placement.

Reading the aggregate as if it were a percentage. An aggregate of ten is not ten per cent. It is the sum of four small integers, and an aggregate of ten is a strong Division 1 record. A family that expects the number to behave like a percentage on a hundred-point scale will misread the result completely.

Assuming all fails are treated the same. They are not. A Fail 9 in Social Studies costs the candidate points and moves the aggregate up, but it does not automatically bar Division 1 the way a Fail 9 in English or Mathematics does. The English and Mathematics condition is the one that catches families by surprise.

Not checking the result slip against the school's own record. UNEB issues the official slip, but every school keeps its own records of the grades its candidates achieved. If the two do not match, the discrepancy should be raised with the school before the placement process begins, because the schools make admissions decisions based on the UNEB slip and not on their internal records.

Sending applications to elite schools only. A candidate with aggregate 8 sometimes misses every Tier 1 school they applied to because the places at the very top fill first. A realistic spread of one or two top-tier choices, two or three strong regional schools, and at least one community school as a fallback gives the candidate a real offer at the end of the placement period rather than a waiting list position.

Ignoring the Senior 1 subject offering of the target school. A school with a strong O-Level science programme is the right destination for a candidate who scored well in Mathematics and Science. A school whose strength is in languages and humanities may be the better choice for a candidate whose best grades are in English and Social Studies. The school matches matter as much as the aggregate.

Repeating P.7 without a plan. Repeating Primary Seven is a legitimate choice for a candidate whose grades were close to a meaningful boundary and who has a clear picture of which subject to improve. It is a poor choice when the candidate is going through the same syllabus without support for the specific gap that held them back. If a family is considering a repeat, they should speak to the head teacher about the specific subject plan first.

Where this tool stops

This calculator estimates the aggregate, the Division, and the school tier from the four grades you enter. It does not know the specific cutoffs that any individual school applied in the most recent admission cycle, does not know the national distribution of grades for the year of the exam, and does not model the effect of a remark, a repeat, or a supplementary sitting. It also does not know the specific candidate's district of origin or any of the other factors a school may weigh when it fills the last few places.

It does not replace the official channels. UNEB issues the result slip, the Ministry of Education and Sports publishes the placement guidance, and each school makes its own admission decisions. What the tool gives you is a fast, transparent way to see where a candidate sits, test how much a single grade change would move the total, and plan a realistic set of school applications before the placement period opens.

Use the result as a planning aid. The slip is the record. The school is the decision. The calculator is the map that helps you plan the route between them.

Frequently asked questions

How is the PLE aggregate score calculated in Uganda?

The Primary Leaving Examination aggregate is the sum of the numeric grade points from four compulsory subjects: English, Mathematics, Integrated Science, and Social Studies including Religious Education. Each subject is graded on a nine-point scale from Distinction 1 down to Fail 9. The four points add up to a total between four and thirty-six, and a lower total is better because point one is the highest grade.

What aggregate qualifies for Division 1 in PLE?

A candidate achieves Division 1 with a total aggregate between four and twelve, provided there are no failing grades in the four subjects, and no fail in English or Mathematics specifically. Division 1 is the highest tier and unlocks the competitive national secondary schools. A perfect score of aggregate four requires Distinction 1 in every subject, which is rare but is the target every P.7 candidate is working towards.

What PLE aggregate do top Ugandan secondary schools require for Senior 1?

The most competitive national boarding schools in Uganda, including King's College Budo, Mount Saint Mary's Namagunga, Gayaza High, Saint Mary's College Kisubi, Namilyango College, Ntare School, and Saint Mary's Kitende, typically admit Senior 1 candidates with aggregates between four and eight. School admission cutoffs move slightly each year depending on the national performance distribution, so a published figure from one year is a reference rather than a fixed rule.

Can a student get Division 1 with a fail in one subject?

No. UNEB rules require a pass in every examined subject for Division 1, and specifically a non-fail grade in English and Mathematics. A single Fail 9 in any of the four subjects disqualifies the candidate from Division 1 even if the aggregate is twelve or lower. The remedy is to sit the subject again at the next examination cycle, either as a repeat candidate or through a school-supported resit.

What is the difference between the PLE aggregate and the Division?

The aggregate is the exact numeric total of your four subject points, between four and thirty-six. The Division is the performance tier that UNEB assigns based on that aggregate and the pass profile of each subject. Two candidates can have the same aggregate and end up in different divisions if one has a fail that disqualifies a higher tier. The aggregate is the arithmetic. The Division is the classification rule applied on top of it.

Does a low PLE aggregate close off secondary education entirely?

No. Division 3 and Division 4 candidates still qualify for community secondary schools under the Universal Secondary Education programme and for technical and vocational institutes. The pathway is different from the one that leads to a national boarding school, but it is a real pathway that leads to O-Level certification and further options at the post-secondary level. Repeating Primary Seven is also possible if the candidate and the school agree that another attempt is worthwhile.

Is the PLE aggregate the only criterion for Senior 1 admission?

The PLE aggregate is the primary criterion, but it is not the only one. National schools apply their own cutoffs each year, and those cutoffs depend on how many places they have and how many candidates meet the minimum. Some schools also consider district of origin, sibling attendance, and in a few cases interviews or aptitude tests for specialised streams. The aggregate decides whether you are in the pool. The school decides who comes out of it.

How do I improve my PLE aggregate if I have not yet sat the exam?

Improving the aggregate comes down to raising the grade in whichever subject is currently the weakest, because each point you remove from the total moves the aggregate by a full point. Moving English from Pass 7 to Credit 5 lowers the aggregate by two. Moving Science from Credit 5 to Distinction 2 lowers it by three. Focused practice on past UNEB papers, topic-by-topic revision, and early help for the subject that lags behind are the practical ways to do it before the exam rather than after.

Does the tool store my grades or share my name?

No. Every calculation runs inside your browser tab and nothing is stored. If you press the AI insight button, only an anonymous summary of the four grades, the aggregate, the Division, and the estimated school tier is sent to a small serverless function that runs once and then stops. Candidate names, index numbers, school names, and any personal identifiers never leave your device.

Is this an official UNEB tool?

No. It is an independent planning aid built to follow the UNEB grading structure and the Division bands the board publishes. The official PLE result slip, issued by UNEB through your school, is the document that governs Senior 1 placement. Use this tool to plan realistically, and treat the official slip as the definitive source.
