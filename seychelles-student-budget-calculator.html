Navigating Student Cost of Living in the Seychelles

Studying in Seychelles, whether at the University of Seychelles (UniSey) in Anse Royale, the School of Advanced Level Studies (SALS), or vocational institutions like the Seychelles Institute of Technology (SIT) and Seychelles Tourism Academy (STA), requires proactive financial budgeting. As a high-income small island developing state (SIDS), Seychelles imports a significant proportion of its foodstuffs and consumer goods, creating a distinctive cost-of-living structure priced in Seychelles Rupees (SCR). Mastering personal finances ensures that students can focus on academic success without financial distress.

Core Monthly Expenditure Categories in Mahé and the Inner Islands

Student expenditure patterns in Seychelles typically divide into six primary pillars:

1. Accommodation & Housing (SCR 1,500 – 4,000)

For students from Praslin, La Digue, or southern Mahé who cannot commute daily, shared student accommodation or hostel quarters around Anse Royale and Mont Fleuri averages SCR 1,500 to SCR 3,500 per room. Students residing with family incur zero rent, dramatically freeing up financial bandwidth.

2. Food & Groceries (SCR 1,200 – 2,500)

Purchasing basic food staples (rice, lentils, canned tuna, fresh fish from local markets, bread) from the Seychelles Trading Company (STC) supermarket provides the most economical nutritional sustenance. Daily takeaway lunches near campuses typically cost SCR 60 to SCR 90 each.

3. Public Bus Transit — SPTC (SCR 150 – 450)

The Seychelles Public Transport Corporation (SPTC) provides comprehensive bus connectivity across Mahé and Praslin. Utilizing the registered SPTC Student Travel Card provides discounted journeys compared to the standard cash fare, capping monthly commuting costs at approximately SCR 200 to SCR 400.

4. Mobile Internet & Wi-Fi (SCR 250 – 500)

Mobile data packages provided by Cable & Wireless Seychelles (CWS) and Airtel Seychelles are essential for accessing lecture recordings, university Moodle portals, and research databases. Opting for monthly student data bundles maximizes gigabytes per Rupee spent.

5. Academic Materials & Personal Care (SCR 400 – 800)

Photocopying, printing thesis chapters and lab manuals, stationery, toiletries, and laundry services require steady monthly cash allocations. Maintaining an emergency buffer of at least SCR 200 per month guards against unexpected medical or academic costs.

Understanding ANHRD Student Allowances & Stipends

The Agency for National Human Resource Development (ANHRD) provides monthly living stipends to qualifying Seychellois students enrolled in accredited full-time tertiary programs:

  • Local UniSey Degree Students: Receive a monthly government maintenance allowance (typically between SCR 2,500 and SCR 4,500) designed to defray transport, food, and study material expenses while tuition is paid directly by the state.
  • Inner Island Living Allowances: Students whose permanent domicile is on Praslin, La Digue, or Silhouette who relocate to Mahé for tertiary study receive enhanced accommodation allowances or subsidized placement in government youth hostels.
  • Academic Conditionality: Continued disbursement of ANHRD allowances is strictly contingent on maintaining a minimum GPA of 2.00 (Pass) and meeting faculty attendance requirements.

Pragmatic Money-Saving Strategies for Students in Seychelles

  1. Prepare Packed Lunches: Cooking batch meals from STC groceries instead of buying daily take-away boxes saves between SCR 800 and SCR 1,200 each month.
  2. Register for the SPTC Student Bus Card: Avoid paying single cash fares on buses. The smart student card reduces daily transit expenditure by up to 50%.
  3. Leverage Campus Wi-Fi: Download lecture PDFs, recorded seminars, and heavy software updates while connected to campus networks to conserve cellular data.
  4. Form Book-Sharing Circles: Purchase textbooks collectively or access digital library repositories through UniSey’s online library service.
  5. Track Weekly Outflows: Check spending every Sunday to verify that discretionary expenses do not exceed your planned daily allowance.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How much is the standard ANHRD student allowance in Seychelles? ▼

The monthly allowance varies based on academic level, institution, and residential status. Local undergraduate degree students typically receive approximately SCR 3,000 to SCR 4,500 per month, with supplementary living allowances provided for students relocating from the inner islands.

Are students permitted to work part-time in Seychelles? ▼

Yes. Seychellois tertiary students can undertake part-time employment, weekend shifts in hospitality, or paid vocational internships, provided their working hours do not conflict with mandatory lecture timetables or compromise academic standing.

What happens to my ANHRD allowance if I fail a semester exam? ▼

If a student fails multiple modules or falls below the mandatory 2.00 CGPA threshold, ANHRD policies mandate an academic review. In cases of unexcused academic probation, the monthly maintenance stipend may be temporarily suspended until re-examinations are cleared.

How can students from Praslin or La Digue minimize travel costs? ▼

Students traveling between Mahé and the inner islands can access student concession rates on the Cat Cocos ferry or travel on government-subsidized scheduled charter tickets organized during term breaks.
