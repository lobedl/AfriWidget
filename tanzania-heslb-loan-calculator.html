Complete Guide to Tanzania HESLB Loans, Allocation Criteria & Repayment Rules

The Higher Education Students' Loans Board (HESLB) is the statutory body established under the Higher Education Students' Loans Board Act (Cap. 178) of the laws of Tanzania. HESLB manages the issuance of revolving loans and grants to eligible, needy Tanzanian students admitted into accredited higher education institutions across Tanzania and overseas. Understanding how means-testing is audited, what statutory cost items are covered, and how repayment obligations work is essential for every undergraduate applicant.

1. HESLB Means-Testing System (MTS) & Allocation Categories

HESLB does not issue flat loans to all students. Instead, it utilizes an automated Means-Testing System (MTS) to evaluate an applicant's socio-economic profile and degree neediness. Priority is given to students based on statutory criteria:

Category A: High Vulnerability (100% Allocation Guarantee)

Total orphans (loss of both biological parents), applicants with severe physical disabilities, and students from certified extreme poverty backgrounds under TASAF oversight.

Category B: National Priority Programs (STEM, Health & Education)

Students admitted into Doctor of Medicine (MD), Dental Surgery, Pharmacy, Nursing, Civil/Mechanical/Electrical Engineering, Agricultural Sciences, Actuarial Science, and Science Education.

Category C: Single Parents & Low-Income Households

Means-tested allocations ranging from 20% to 80% tuition support alongside standard Meals and Accommodation allowances.

2. Breakdown of HESLB Statutory Cost Items

An annual HESLB loan package comprises distinct items defined by governing legislation:

  • Meals and Accommodation (MA): Paid directly to the student's personal bank account in quarterly or semester installments. Standard rate is TZS 10,000 per day for ~180 academic days per year (approx. TZS 1,800,000 to TZS 2,000,000 annually).
  • Tuition Fee (TU): Disbursed directly to the university on behalf of the student. Capped at a statutory ceiling of TZS 3,100,000 per year for undergraduate programs. The percentage awarded (20% to 100%) depends on the applicant's Means-Testing score.
  • Books and Stationery (BS): A fixed statutory grant/loan of TZS 200,000 per academic year paid directly to the student.
  • Field Practical Training (FPT): Special practical training allowance calculated at TZS 10,000 per day for an average of 62 field days (approx. TZS 620,000 per year for eligible programs).
  • Special Faculty Requirements (SFR): Program-specific funds for medical kits, engineering drawing equipment, laboratory coats, and survey instruments (ranging from TZS 200,000 to TZS 500,000).
  • Research Project (RES): A one-time allowance allocated during the final year of study for undergraduate thesis or field project completion (typically TZS 500,000).

3. Loan Administration Fee, 6% VRF & Statutory 15% Repayment Rules

HESLB loans are revolving credit facilities that require strict compliance upon graduation:

1. Loan Administration Fee (1%)

A one-time fee of 1.0% is levied on the total cumulative principal loan disbursed over your study duration to cover board administration costs.

2. Value Retention Fee (VRF 6% Per Annum)

Unlike commercial bank interest, HESLB charges a 6% annual Value Retention Fee on the unpaid principal balance to protect the revolving fund against inflation and currency depreciation.

3. Statutory Salary Deductions (15% Monthly)

Under Section 19 of the HESLB Act, employers are legally mandated to deduct 15% of the beneficiary's monthly basic salary and remit it directly to HESLB until the full debt is cleared.

4. Grace Period (24 Months)

Beneficiaries enjoy a 24-month (2-year) moratorium post-graduation before active repayment recovery commences. However, VRF continues to accrue on the outstanding balance.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What happens if my salary is not enough or I become self-employed? ▼

Self-employed or informal sector beneficiaries are required under HESLB regulations to submit a minimum statutory monthly installment of TZS 100,000 (or 10% of declared taxable monthly income) directly via HESLB control numbers or mobile money integration (GePG).

How does HESLB calculate the 6% Value Retention Fee (VRF)? ▼

The Value Retention Fee is calculated as 6% per annum on the outstanding loan balance at the end of each financial year. It is not an interest fee; rather, it is indexed to offset currency inflation and sustain the revolving loan fund for future generations.

Can I apply for a loan if I studied private secondary school? ▼

Yes. While applicants from government secondary schools and needy backgrounds are prioritized during means-testing, applicants from private schools who meet admission prerequisites for priority degree programs (such as Medicine, Engineering, Science Education, and Agriculture) can still receive partial or full loans depending on their Means-Testing System (MTS) score.

How do I get an HESLB Loan Clearance Certificate? ▼

Once your total principal, 1% administration fee, and accrued VRF are fully settled through employer deductions or direct payments, HESLB conducts a final audit and issues an official HESLB Loan Clearance Certificate, which is often required for senior government and public sector appointments.
